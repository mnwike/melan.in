A parent's guide to curly and coily hair

A simple routine for washing, detangling and styling children's curly and coily hair, based on advice from paediatric dermatologists.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Curly and coily hair is drier and more fragile than straight hair. Moisture and gentle handling solve most problems.
  • Detangle when the hair is wet and full of conditioner, never dry.
  • Your child's hair is growing even when it does not look longer. Tight curls hide length.

Mixed evidence

Wash day

  • Wash the scalp every one to two weeks. Braids or cornrows do not change this: the scalp still needs washing.
  • Shampoo the scalp, condition the lengths.
  • Deep condition regularly.
  • Rinse well. Product left on the scalp causes flaking.

Strong evidence

Detangling without tears

Studies of tightly coiled hair show that combing it wet takes less force and breaks fewer hairs than combing it dry. Work in sections with conditioner in, using your fingers first, then a wide-tooth comb or a wet brush, starting at the ends and moving up to the roots. A detangling spray helps between washes.

Strong evidence

Styling

  • Keep styles loose. If your child says it hurts, loosen it.
  • Avoid long, heavy extensions on young children.
  • Do not use chemical relaxers on children's hair.
  • Trim damaged ends without worrying about length.

Strong evidence

Scalp

Go easy on oils and grease on the scalp. They do not treat a flaky scalp and can make it worse. Scaling with patches of hair loss in a child needs a doctor.

When to see a doctor

Patches of hair loss, broken hairs with scaling, a sore scalp, or hair thinning at the hairline.

Common questions

How often should I wash my child's afro hair?

About every one to two weeks, including when it is in braids.

Should I comb curly hair wet or dry?

Wet, with conditioner in. It takes less force and causes less breakage.
