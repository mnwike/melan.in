Dandruff treatment

Dandruff is driven by a scalp yeast and is treated with medicated shampoo. How to treat it if you wash weekly, wear braids or oil your hair.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Dandruff is not dryness. It is a reaction to a yeast that feeds on scalp oil.
  • Medicated shampoo is the treatment. Oiling the scalp usually makes it worse.
  • It comes back when treatment stops, so most people need a maintenance wash.

Strong evidence

What causes it

A yeast called Malassezia lives on everyone's scalp and feeds on oil. In some people its by-products irritate the skin, which sheds faster as flakes. A more inflamed, itchy form with greasy scale is seborrhoeic dermatitis. Stress, cold weather and infrequent washing make both worse.

Strong evidence

Shampoos that work

  • Ketoconazole 2%: the best-proven. Twice a week for a month, then once every week or two.
  • Zinc pyrithione, selenium sulfide, piroctone olamine, coal tar or salicylic acid.
  • Leave on the scalp for three to five minutes before rinsing.
  • Rotate if one stops working.

Mixed evidence

If you wash weekly or wear braids

  • Apply medicated shampoo to the scalp only, then use your usual moisturising shampoo and conditioner on the lengths.
  • With braids or locs, dilute it in an applicator bottle and work along the parts.
  • Ask a GP for a steroid scalp lotion or foam for flares: it fits a weekly routine.
  • Selenium sulfide can dry and discolour hair. Rinse well.

Mixed evidence

Oil and dandruff

Coconut, olive and most plant oils are food for the yeast. Oiling a flaky scalp, a habit across South Asia, Africa and the Caribbean, tends to stick the flakes down for a day and worsen the problem. Oil the lengths. Treat the scalp.

When to see a doctor

Thick scale stuck to the scalp, hair loss with flaking, weeping or crusting, or flaking in a child with broken hairs, which may be ringworm.

Common questions

What is the best treatment for dandruff?

A medicated shampoo such as ketoconazole 2%, left on the scalp for a few minutes, then used regularly to keep it away.
