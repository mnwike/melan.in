The essentials: a simple kit for skin and hair

Three short kits built on evidence: five things for skin, six for hair, and a shave kit. What each does, what to look for on the label, and what to skip.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Good skin and hair come from a few things done steadily, not from many products.
  • We name types of product and what to look for on the label, not brands.
  • 'Perfect' skin is not a thing. Comfortable, even and protected is.

Strong evidence

The skin kit: five things

  • Gentle cleanser. Look for: 'soap-free', glycerin. Skip: scrubs and strong fragrance.
  • Moisturiser, used on damp skin. Look for: glycerin, shea butter, ceramides, petrolatum.
  • Sunscreen, SPF 30 or more, tinted with iron oxides if you get dark marks.
  • One treatment for your main concern: azelaic acid or niacinamide for marks, salicylic acid for spots and bumps, a retinoid at night for texture.
  • A richer body cream or ointment for winter, hands, elbows and shins.

Mixed evidence

The hair kit: six things

  • Shampoo for the scalp, every one to two weeks. A sulfate-free one if your hair is dry.
  • Conditioner every wash, and a deeper one regularly.
  • Leave-in conditioner or cream. Look for: water first, then glycerin, fatty alcohols, oils or butters.
  • An oil or butter to seal the ends.
  • A wide-tooth comb or your fingers. Detangle wet with conditioner in.
  • A satin bonnet or pillowcase.

Mixed evidence

The shave kit

  • Clippers or a guarded trimmer, or a single-blade razor.
  • A slick, fragrance-free shaving gel.
  • A plain moisturiser for afterwards.
  • A salicylic or glycolic acid product for the days between.

Limited evidence

How to check a product

Paste the ingredient list into our ingredient checker, or look the product up in the explorer. The first five ingredients make up most of what is in the bottle.

Mixed evidence

What you can skip

  • Lightening creams without a prescription.
  • Hair vitamins, unless a blood test says you need them.
  • Ten-step routines. More products means more chances of irritation and dark marks.

When to see a doctor

A rash, burning or new dark patches after starting a product: stop it and see a pharmacist or GP.

Common questions

What skincare do I actually need?

A gentle cleanser, a moisturiser and a sunscreen, plus one treatment for your main concern.

What products does afro hair need?

Shampoo, conditioner, a leave-in, something to seal the ends, a wide-tooth comb and a satin bonnet.
