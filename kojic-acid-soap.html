Kojic acid soap

Kojic acid soap is sold worldwide to lighten dark spots. What kojic acid does, why a soap delivers little of it, the side effects, and the fakes to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Kojic acid can modestly fade dark marks when left on the skin in a cream or serum.
  • In a soap it is on the skin for seconds, so the effect is small and the drying effect is large.
  • Overuse causes irritation, which in darker skin leads to more dark marks.

Strong evidence

What kojic acid is

Kojic acid is made by fungi during the fermentation of rice, as in sake and soy sauce production. It slows tyrosinase, the enzyme that makes melanin. Regulators in Europe allow it in cosmetics at low concentrations.

Limited evidence

Does the soap work?

The studies showing benefit used leave-on creams, usually combined with other ingredients, over two to three months. No good trial has tested kojic acid soap. Soap is rinsed off almost at once, and the popular bright orange bars are strongly alkaline, which strips the skin. People who see a result often leave the lather on for minutes, which is exactly what causes burning and peeling.

Mixed evidence

Side effects and fakes

  • Dryness, tightness, stinging and peeling.
  • Allergic contact dermatitis, a recognised reaction to kojic acid.
  • Rebound darkening after irritation.
  • More sunburn and sun-darkening, so sunscreen is essential.
  • Counterfeit bars are common, and some lightening soaps have been found to contain steroids or mercury.

Limited evidence

A better way to use it

If you want kojic acid, a leave-on serum or cream at a low concentration, used on the marks only, with moisturiser and daily sunscreen, is both more effective and gentler. Azelaic acid and niacinamide are alternatives with better evidence in darker skin.

When to see a doctor

Burning, blistering, or skin that becomes darker, thinner or patchy after a lightening soap.

Common questions

Does kojic acid soap lighten skin?

Only slightly, if at all. Kojic acid works better in leave-on products, and the soap often irritates.
