Trichology: the key papers

A reading list of the research behind afro-textured hair science: hair shape and growth worldwide, fragility, CCCA genetics, traction, ageing and hair care.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A short, plain-language reading list for trichologists, students and curious readers.
  • The field is small. A handful of research groups produced most of it.
  • Several of the largest studies were run by a cosmetics company's own laboratories. Read with that in mind.

Mixed evidence

Hair shape and growth around the world

  • De la Mettrie and colleagues, 2007. Measured curl in hair from people in many countries and proposed eight curl types defined without ethnicity. The foundation for describing hair by shape.
  • Loussouarn and colleagues, 2005. 511 volunteers. Reported lower density and slower growth in African participants, fast growth but lower density in Asian participants, and highest density in European participants, with wide variation inside every group.
  • Loussouarn and colleagues, 2016. 2,249 young adults from 24 ethnic groups on five continents. Confirmed the broad pattern and showed how much scalp area, sex and geography also matter.

Mixed evidence

Structure and fragility

  • Franbourg and colleagues, 2003. Found no difference in the keratin or amino acid make-up of African, Asian and European hair. The differences were in shape and mechanical behaviour.
  • Khumalo and colleagues, 2000. Examined shed African hair under the microscope and described the knots and breaks that explain why it is hard to grow long. A founding paper from Cape Town.
  • Cutis reviews, 2022 and 2025. Clinical summaries of what is known about caring for highly textured hair. The 2025 review found only 29 studies good enough to include.

Mixed evidence

Hair loss conditions

  • Malki and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2019. Linked CCCA to variants in the PADI3 gene in patients from Durban and North Carolina. It moved CCCA from 'caused by hot combs' towards an inherited susceptibility.
  • Haskin and Aguh, 2016. Ranked hairstyles by their risk of traction alopecia, giving clinicians something concrete to advise.
  • Dadzie and Salam, 2016. A study of the hair grooming practices of women of African descent in London, one of few sets of UK data.

Limited evidence

Ageing

  • Maymone and colleagues, 2021. Reviewed 69 papers on how hair ages in different populations: greying starts later on average in people of African ancestry, and damage sits closer to the root.

Limited evidence

How to read this literature

Three cautions. First, the big multi-country studies were funded and run by L'Oreal's research division. The measurements are careful, but the questions asked are those useful to a company that sells hair products. Second, 'African', 'Asian' and 'Caucasian' are blunt labels. Africa alone holds more human genetic diversity than the rest of the world together. Third, many clinical claims rest on small studies from a few hospitals in the US and South Africa. UK and Caribbean data are scarce.

When to see a doctor

This page is a reading list, not advice. For a hair or scalp problem, start with the guides and the specialist finder.

Common questions

Who are the main researchers in afro hair science?

Groups led by Nonhlanhla Khumalo and Ncoza Dlova in South Africa, Amy McMichael, Crystal Aguh and Valerie Callender in the US, and Ophelia Dadzie in the UK, alongside industry laboratories.

Sources
