Relaxers and your health

Two large studies link frequent hair relaxer use with uterine cancer. What they measured, how big the risk is, and what they cannot prove.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Two large US studies link frequent, long-term relaxer use with a higher risk of uterine cancer.
  • These are associations. They do not prove relaxers cause cancer, and the studies did not record brands or ingredients.
  • Uterine cancer is uncommon, so the added risk for one person is small. It is still a reason to think about how often you relax.

Strong evidence

The Sister Study, 2022

Researchers at the US National Institutes of Health followed 33,497 women for about 11 years. They estimated that 1.64% of women who never used straighteners would develop uterine cancer by age 70, against 4.05% of those who used them more than four times a year.

Strong evidence

The Black Women's Health Study, 2023

Boston University researchers followed 44,798 Black women. Among those past the menopause, moderate and heavy use was linked with a higher rate of uterine cancer. Among younger women there was no clear link, and across all heavy users the increase was not statistically certain.

Limited evidence

What nobody knows yet

Neither study asked which products women used, so no ingredient has been identified. Relaxers can contain chemicals that interfere with hormones, which gives a plausible mechanism, but that has not been shown in people.

Limited evidence

Practical steps

  • Relax less often, or stretch the time between touch-ups.
  • Protect the scalp: burns and broken skin let more through.
  • Dermatologists advise against chemical straighteners for children.

When to see a doctor

Bleeding after the menopause or unusual bleeding between periods should always be checked, whatever products you use.

Common questions

Do relaxers cause cancer?

Studies show a link between frequent use and uterine cancer, not proof of cause. The absolute risk remains low.

Are no-lye relaxers safer?

The studies did not separate product types, so nobody can say.
