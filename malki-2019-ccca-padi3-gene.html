Research / Scarring hair loss

Variant PADI3 in central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia

Liron Malki, Amy McMichael, Ncoza Dlova, Eli Sprecher, and colleagues · New England Journal of Medicine, 2019

A plain-language summary by melan.in. Not written or endorsed by the authors.

What they did

An international team sequenced the genes of women with CCCA, the most common scarring hair loss in women of African ancestry, looking for shared mutations.

What they found

  • Mutations in PADI3 were associated with CCCA.
  • PADI3 makes a protein that is essential for forming the hair shaft.
  • The finding points to an inherited vulnerability, with hair care practices as a possible trigger.

Why it matters

For decades CCCA was blamed on women's styling choices alone. This study showed that genes are involved, which changes how the condition is explained to patients and their families.

Limits

Not every woman with CCCA carries these mutations, so other causes remain to be found.
