Keloids and raised scars

Keloids are raised scars that grow beyond the original wound and are far more common in darker skin. Who is at risk, piercing advice and treatments.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A keloid is a scar that keeps growing past the edges of the wound.
  • People of African descent are affected many times more often than people with light skin.
  • Cutting a keloid out on its own often brings it back. Combination treatment works better.

Strong evidence

Who gets them

Keloids occur in every population but are most common in people of colour, with estimates of 4% to 16%. People of African descent are affected roughly 5 to 16 times more often than those with light skin. They usually begin after puberty, on average in the early twenties, and they run in families.

Mixed evidence

Piercings and tattoos

Any skin injury can start one: piercings, tattoos, surgery, acne, even a scratch. In one study of people known to form keloids, about 13% developed one at their first piercing and about 70% at their second. Piercings done before puberty carried a lower risk. If you or close relatives form keloids, think carefully before a new piercing, and avoid it once one has already formed.

Strong evidence

Treatment

A 2025 review of 37 studies in skin of colour found that surgery alone cleared 68% of keloids but about a third came back. Steroid injections, silicone sheets and pressure reduce regrowth, and surgery is usually combined with one of them. Lasers and other procedures carry a risk of pigment change on darker skin and need an experienced specialist.

When to see a doctor

A scar that is growing, itchy or painful. Early treatment works better than waiting.

Common questions

Can keloids be removed permanently?

Sometimes, with combined treatment. Surgery alone often leads to regrowth.

Will I get a keloid from a piercing?

The risk is higher if you or close relatives have had keloids, and higher with each further piercing.
