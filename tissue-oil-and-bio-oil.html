Tissue oil and Bio-Oil

Tissue oils such as Bio-Oil are best-sellers for stretch marks, scars and uneven tone, especially in South Africa. What is in them and what evidence supports.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Tissue oil is a light skincare oil, usually mineral oil with plant oils and vitamins A and E.
  • The evidence that it improves scars and stretch marks is limited and mostly company-funded.
  • It is a pleasant, safe moisturiser for most people. Expect softer skin, not erased marks.

Strong evidence

What it is

Bio-Oil was developed in South Africa, where 'tissue oil' has become the general name for the whole category. The base is mineral oil, thinned with an ingredient that makes it feel dry and absorb fast, plus small amounts of vitamin A, vitamin E and plant extracts such as calendula, lavender and rosemary.

Limited evidence

The evidence

A few small studies, sponsored by the manufacturer, report modest improvement in the appearance of scars and stretch marks over eight to twelve weeks compared with no treatment. Independent, larger trials are lacking. Massage and moisturising with any oil probably account for part of the effect. There is no good evidence that it prevents stretch marks in pregnancy.

Mixed evidence

Using it

  • Massage into damp skin once or twice a day.
  • Fragrance and plant extracts can cause reactions. Patch test, or choose a fragrance-free version.
  • It can clog pores on the face, chest and back.
  • Products with vitamin A are generally considered fine on the skin in pregnancy at cosmetic strengths, but check with your midwife.
  • For raised or keloid scars, silicone gel has better evidence.

When to see a doctor

A scar that is growing, itchy or painful, or a rash where you applied the oil.

Common questions

Does Bio-Oil work on stretch marks?

Small company-funded studies show modest improvement in appearance. It softens skin but does not remove marks.
