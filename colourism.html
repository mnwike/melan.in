Colourism

Colourism is prejudice based on skin shade, inside and outside our communities. What studies show about its effect on wellbeing and on skin bleaching.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Colourism is the favouring of lighter skin over darker skin, often within the same ethnic group.
  • Research links it with poorer body image and mental health.
  • It is one of the forces behind skin bleaching and the pressure to straighten hair.

Strong evidence

What it is

Racism is prejudice between groups. Colourism operates by shade, and can come from one's own community as well as from outside it. It affects Black, South Asian, East Asian and Latin American communities, and it falls hardest on women and girls.

Mixed evidence

What the research shows

A UK study of 516 Black, Asian and other minority-ethnic adults found that colourism from within a person's own community was linked with lower satisfaction with their skin shade, more monitoring of it, and in turn worse body image and more psychological distress. A US study found that Black children with the darkest complexions reported more depressive symptoms. A review of 53 studies of Black women found the subject common and still under-researched.

Mixed evidence

How it reaches the body

Skin lighteners and hair straighteners are often used to meet beauty standards shaped by colourism, and both carry health risks. That is why this library treats colourism as a health topic.

Limited evidence

At home

  • Notice shade-based compliments and jokes, and stop repeating them.
  • Praise children for what their skin and hair are, not for how light or straight they are.
  • Talk openly about where these ideas came from.

When to see a doctor

Persistent low mood, anxiety or distress about appearance deserves support. A GP can refer you, and you can ask for a therapist with relevant cultural understanding.

Common questions

What is the difference between racism and colourism?

Racism is prejudice between racial groups. Colourism is prejudice by skin shade, and often happens within the same group.

Does colourism affect mental health?

Studies link it with poorer body image and more psychological distress.
