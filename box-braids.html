Box braids

Box braids are individual braids in square parts, usually with extensions. Sizes, how long they last, washing, and how to avoid traction at the hairline.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Box braids are a low-maintenance style that can protect hair when done loosely.
  • The knot at the root and the weight of the extension are where the risk comes from.
  • Six to eight weeks, then out, with a rest before the next set.

Limited evidence

Choosing a set

  • Size: larger parts carry more weight per braid but need less tension to hold. Very small braids multiply the pull on fine hair.
  • Length: every extra inch is extra weight on the root.
  • Hair: lighter synthetic fibres weigh less. Some people get an itchy scalp from the coating on synthetic hair. Rinsing it in diluted vinegar first can help.
  • Braider: one who listens when you say it is tight.

Mixed evidence

While they are in

  • Scalp should be comfortable within a day.
  • Avoid high ponytails and buns for the first week.
  • Cleanse the scalp every week or two and moisturise your own hair with a light spray.
  • Sleep in a satin bonnet or scarf.
  • Re-doing only the front rows to 'freshen up' doubles the tension on the hairline.

Strong evidence

Signs to take them out early

Small white bumps around the braids, a shiny or sore hairline, braids hanging by a few hairs, or thinning at the temples. Traction caught early grows back. Caught late it does not.

When to see a doctor

Soreness, bumps or thinning at the hairline or parts.

Common questions

How long should box braids stay in?

Six to eight weeks at most, with a break before re-braiding.

Sources

  • Hair care practices and highly textured hair (Cutis, 2022)
  • Wong et al., Evidence-based care of textured hair in Black women: a review of 29 studies (Cutis, 2025)
