Acne scars and marks on dark skin

Most 'acne scars' on brown and black skin are flat dark marks that fade. How to tell marks from true scars, what treats each, and which procedures are safe.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Flat dark spots after acne are marks, not scars. They fade, slowly, without procedures.
  • True scars are dents or raised lumps. They need procedures to improve.
  • Treating the acne is step one. New spots make new marks faster than any cream fades them.

Strong evidence

Marks or scars?

  • Dark marks: flat, brown to grey-black, smooth to the touch. Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
  • Pitted scars: small dents, ice-pick, boxcar or rolling.
  • Raised scars: firm lumps, sometimes keloids, mostly on the jawline, chest, shoulders and back.

Mixed evidence

Fading dark marks

  • Daily sunscreen, ideally tinted with iron oxides.
  • A retinoid at night: adapalene from a pharmacy, or prescription tretinoin.
  • Azelaic acid or niacinamide, which treat acne and pigment together.
  • Expect three to six months on the face, longer on the body.
  • Do not pick. Do not scrub.

Mixed evidence

Treating true scars

  • Microneedling: among the safer options in darker skin.
  • Subcision and fillers for rolling scars.
  • TCA applied to individual ice-pick scars by a specialist.
  • Lasers: only with devices and settings suited to dark skin, with a test patch.
  • Raised and keloid scars: steroid injections and silicone, not resurfacing.

Strong evidence

What to avoid

Deep peels, aggressive resurfacing lasers, home microneedling rollers and lightening creams from informal sellers. Each can leave darker, lighter or scarred skin that is harder to treat than the original marks.

When to see a doctor

Acne that is leaving scars, deep painful spots, or any raised scar that is growing.

Common questions

How do I get rid of acne scars on dark skin?

Flat dark marks fade with sunscreen, a retinoid and azelaic acid. Pitted scars need procedures such as microneedling.
