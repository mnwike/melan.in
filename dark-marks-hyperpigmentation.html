Dark marks after spots, cuts and rashes

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation explained: why dark marks linger on melanin-rich skin, which ingredients have evidence, and what to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Dark marks are the skin's pigment response to inflammation. They are more common and last longer on darker skin.
  • Treat the cause first: no new spots or rashes means no new marks.
  • Sun protection and patience do most of the work. Marks can take months, sometimes years, to fade.

Strong evidence

What dark marks are

The medical name is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. After acne, eczema, a cut, a burn or an ingrown hair, pigment cells in the area release extra melanin. The mark sits exactly where the inflammation was.

It can happen in any skin type, but it is more frequent and more severe in darker skin. In one study, about 65% of African-American patients with acne developed dark marks. Pigment problems are among the most common reasons Black and brown patients see a dermatologist.

Strong evidence

What helps

  • Treat the underlying problem early, whether that is acne, eczema or razor bumps.
  • Use sunscreen every day. Tinted sunscreens containing iron oxides also block visible light, which darkens marks on deeper skin tones.
  • Ingredients with evidence: azelaic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, retinoids, kojic acid, arbutin and liquorice extract.
  • Introduce one product at a time. Irritation creates new marks.

Strong evidence

Hydroquinone

Hydroquinone is an effective pigment-fading medicine and dermatologists do prescribe it. In the UK it is banned from cosmetics and legal only on prescription, because unsupervised use can damage the skin. A cream sold over the counter that lists hydroquinone is illegal.

Mixed evidence

What to avoid

  • Picking or squeezing spots.
  • Harsh scrubs and strong peels at home.
  • Skin-lightening creams bought without a prescription.

When to see a doctor

A mark that grows, changes shape, bleeds or appears on a palm, sole or under a nail. Marks that have not improved after several months of care.

Common questions

How long do dark marks take to fade?

Usually months, and sometimes years, even with treatment. Marks nearer the surface fade faster than deeper ones.

Does sunscreen help dark marks on dark skin?

Yes. Sun protection is part of first-line treatment, and tinted sunscreens with iron oxides add protection against visible light.
