Cornrows and braided hairstyles

Cornrows are braids worked flat to the scalp, with thousands of years of African history. Styles, how long they last, washing, and avoiding tension.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Cornrows are among the oldest hairstyles on record and remain the most searched braid style in Nigeria and South Africa.
  • Done at a comfortable tension they are one of the lower-risk braided styles.
  • Two to four weeks is long enough. They loosen and collect build-up after that.

Mixed evidence

A short history

Braids worked in rows against the scalp appear in Saharan rock art and ancient sculpture, and patterns have signalled age, status, faith and community across Africa. Enslaved Africans carried the practice to the Americas and the Caribbean, where the name 'cornrows' or 'canerows' arose. Today they are worn alone, under wigs, and as the base for sew-ins and crochet styles.

Strong evidence

Styles

  • Straight backs and all-backs.
  • Feed-in or stitch braids, with extension hair added for size.
  • Fulani and tribal patterns with a centre braid and beads.
  • Ghana weaving and shuku, worn widely in West Africa.
  • Short hair and low cuts for men can be cornrowed from about two inches.

Mixed evidence

Care

  • Comfortable from the first day. Tight cornrows at the front are a leading cause of receding hairlines.
  • Vary the direction of the rows between installs so the same hairs are not pulled each time.
  • Cleanse the scalp weekly along the parts.
  • Under wigs, take them down every few weeks and wash.
  • Children: looser, larger, and out sooner.

When to see a doctor

A widening parting, a hairline moving back, or pimples and soreness along the rows.

Common questions

How long do cornrows last?

Two to four weeks. Longer than that and build-up and loosening start to cause problems.
