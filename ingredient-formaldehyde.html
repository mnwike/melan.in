Ingredients / Hair-smoothing active

Formaldehyde and methylene glycol

Locks hair straight in 'Brazilian' and keratin smoothing treatments. Released as gas when the hair is heated.

General information from cosmetic science. Not yet clinically reviewed.

Well established

What the science says

A known cause of cancer when breathed in, and a strong skin, eye and airway irritant and allergen. Glyoxylic acid is used in 'formaldehyde-free' versions and has been linked in case reports with kidney injury.

Not permitted in UK cosmetics

Not permitted as an ingredient in UK and EU cosmetics. A proposed US ban on its use in hair straighteners has been repeatedly delayed.

Cancer evidence

Classed as a known human carcinogen. The risk comes from breathing the gas, which is released when treated hair is blow-dried or flat-ironed.

Other known downsides

Burning eyes, nose and throat, breathing problems, scalp burns, hair loss and allergy.

Hot combs, silk presses and heat

How it appears on a label

Look for: formaldehyde, methylene glycol, formalin, glyoxylic acid.
