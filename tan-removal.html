Tan removal on brown skin

A tan fades as skin renews itself over four to six weeks. What speeds it up safely on brown skin, why bleach, lemon and scrubs backfire, and how to prevent it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A tan is extra melanin made in response to sun. It fades as the outer skin sheds, usually within four to six weeks.
  • Nothing removes a tan overnight. Scrubs, bleach and lemon irritate and often leave skin darker.
  • Sunscreen is the only reliable tan prevention.

Strong evidence

What a tan is

Ultraviolet light damages skin cells, and pigment cells respond by making and spreading more melanin to shield them. Brown skin tans quickly and deeply. As the outer cells are shed, the colour returns to your baseline. Tan lines on the feet, arms and neck last longest because those areas keep getting sun.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Time, and strict sun protection while it fades.
  • Daily moisturiser, which helps skin shed evenly.
  • Gentle chemical exfoliation with lactic or glycolic acid a few times a week.
  • Niacinamide, vitamin C or azelaic acid for patchy areas.
  • For stubborn uneven tone that is really melasma, see a dermatologist.

Mixed evidence

What backfires

  • Lemon juice, baking soda and toothpaste: irritate and sensitise skin to sun.
  • Bleach creams and 'de-tan' packs: often contain peroxide or undisclosed lighteners.
  • Hard scrubbing: causes inflammation and new dark marks.
  • Multani mitti and besan packs: mildly exfoliating and drying. They do not remove pigment.

Strong evidence

Prevention

Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or more on exposed skin, reapplied every two hours outdoors, plus shade, sleeves and a hat. A tinted sunscreen with iron oxides also blocks the visible light that darkens brown skin.

When to see a doctor

Darkening that does not fade after two months, patchy brown areas on the cheeks, forehead or upper lip, or skin changes after a bleaching product.

Common questions

How long does a tan take to fade?

Usually four to six weeks with sun protection, longer on feet and hands.
