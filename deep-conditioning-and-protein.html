Deep conditioning and protein treatments

What deep conditioners and protein treatments do, how to tell which your afro or curly hair needs, how often to use them, and whether heat helps.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Conditioners coat and smooth the hair. They do not repair it permanently.
  • Soft, limp, stretchy hair may need protein. Stiff, dry, snapping hair needs moisture.
  • 'Protein overload' is not a measured condition, but stiffness after protein is real. Adjust by feel.

Mixed evidence

What deep conditioning does

A deep conditioner is a conditioner with more oils, fatty alcohols and conditioning agents, left on longer. Positively charged ingredients cling to damaged, negatively charged areas of the hair, smoothing the cuticle and reducing friction. Less friction means fewer knots and less breakage, which matters most in tightly coiled hair.

Mixed evidence

What protein treatments do

Hydrolysed proteins are proteins broken into small pieces. They sit in gaps in a damaged cuticle and form a film, which makes hair feel stronger for a few washes. They help most after bleaching, colouring, relaxing and heat.

Limited evidence

Which do you need?

  • Stretch test: wet a strand and pull gently. If it stretches a lot and does not spring back, or feels gummy, try protein.
  • If it barely stretches and snaps, or feels rough and straw-like, use moisture.
  • Coloured, relaxed or heat-styled hair usually benefits from protein every four to six weeks.
  • Untreated natural hair may rarely need it.

Limited evidence

How to do it

  • After shampoo, apply to damp hair in sections, concentrating on the ends.
  • Leave for the time on the label, usually fifteen to thirty minutes. Longer adds little.
  • A plastic cap and gentle warmth help some ingredients spread, especially on low-porosity hair.
  • Rinse with cool or lukewarm water.
  • Weekly or fortnightly suits most afro-textured hair.
  • Leaving conditioner in overnight can leave hair over-soft and weak.

When to see a doctor

Hair that keeps breaking despite good care, breaks close to the scalp, or comes with scalp soreness or thinning.

Common questions

How often should I deep condition natural hair?

Weekly or every two weeks suits most people.

How do I know if my hair needs protein?

Wet hair that stretches a long way and feels gummy often benefits from protein.
