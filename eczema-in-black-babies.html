Eczema in Black and brown babies

Eczema in Black and brown babies looks brown, purple or grey, bumpy and dry, not red. How to recognise it, treat it, and prevent light and dark marks.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • On dark skin eczema is brown, purple or grey, often with tiny bumps like goosebumps. Red is hard to see.
  • It often shows on the outsides of arms and legs, not only in the creases.
  • Because 'redness' is missed, eczema in Black children is often judged milder than it is.

Mixed evidence

What to look for

  • Dry, rough patches that are darker than the skin around them, or ashy and grey.
  • Small firm bumps around the hair follicles, especially on the tummy, back and limbs.
  • Rubbing and scratching, poor sleep, and skin that feels thicker where it is rubbed.
  • In babies: cheeks, scalp, and the fronts of the arms and legs. In older children: creases, but also knees and elbows.

Mixed evidence

Why it gets under-treated

Scoring systems for eczema lean on redness. In richly pigmented skin inflammation looks violet, grey or dark brown, so the same flare scores as milder. US data suggest Black children are more likely to have eczema and more likely to have it severely. If your child is scratching and not sleeping, say so plainly. Itch and sleep count as much as colour.

Strong evidence

Treatment

  • Moisturise every day, generously, with a plain fragrance-free emollient, even when the skin looks good.
  • Wash with the emollient or a soap substitute. Short lukewarm baths.
  • Use the steroid cream your GP prescribes on flares, for the days advised. Used correctly on eczema it does not lighten skin permanently.
  • Shea butter and oils are fine for many children as moisturisers, but they do not treat a flare. Patch test first: some babies react.
  • Avoid olive oil on baby skin. Small studies suggest it can weaken the skin barrier.

Mixed evidence

Light and dark marks

After a flare, skin may be darker or lighter for months. Parents often fear the steroid cream caused the pale patches. Far more often it is the eczema. The best protection against marks is treating flares early, so the inflammation is brief.

When to see a doctor

Weeping, crusting or yellow fluid, small punched-out sores (this can be the cold sore virus and is urgent), a baby who is not feeding or is unusually sleepy, or eczema not controlled after two weeks of treatment.

Common questions

What does eczema look like on a Black baby?

Dry patches that are darker, purple or grey rather than red, often with tiny bumps, on the cheeks, scalp, arms and legs.

Will steroid cream lighten my baby's skin?

Used as prescribed for flares, lasting lightening is uncommon. Pale patches after eczema are usually from the eczema itself.
