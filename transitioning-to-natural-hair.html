Going natural: transitioning and the big chop

Stopping relaxer and growing out natural hair. Big chop or slow transition, how to stop breakage where the two textures meet, and what to expect.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Relaxed hair cannot be turned back. It has to grow out and be cut off.
  • The point where new growth meets relaxed hair is the weakest part of the strand.
  • A big chop is quick. Transitioning keeps length but needs gentler handling for longer.

Mixed evidence

Two routes

  • Big chop: cut off all relaxed hair once there is enough new growth to be comfortable, often after two to four months.
  • Transition: keep the relaxed ends and trim a little every six to eight weeks until they are gone, which can take a year or two.
  • Neither is better. It depends on how you feel about short hair and how much time you have for care.

Mixed evidence

Protecting the line of demarcation

  • Detangle only when hair is wet and full of conditioner, with fingers or a wide-tooth comb, from the ends up.
  • Deep condition regularly. Add a protein treatment every four to six weeks if hair feels mushy or stretchy.
  • Keep heat low. Straightening the new growth to match the ends causes heat damage.
  • Low-tension styles: twists, loose braids, buns, wigs with a break between installs.
  • Trim as you go, so the weak ends do not tangle and tear the new growth.

Limited evidence

What to expect

  • New growth may look dry and undefined at first. The curl pattern shows properly once the relaxed ends are gone.
  • The scalp often feels better within months of stopping relaxer.
  • Products that suited relaxed hair may not suit natural hair. Expect to change them.
  • Texture can differ across the head. That is normal.

Mixed evidence

Why people stop

Some stop for the health of their hair and scalp, some because of research linking frequent relaxer use with fibroids and some hormone-related cancers, and some for reasons of identity. The research shows association, not proof of cause, and is covered in our relaxer guide.

When to see a doctor

Thinning at the crown, a tender scalp, or breakage that does not improve after the relaxed hair has gone.

Common questions

Do I have to big chop to go natural?

No. You can grow the relaxer out and trim it gradually.

How long does transitioning take?

Usually one to two years, depending on length and how much you trim.
