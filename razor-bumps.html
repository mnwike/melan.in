Razor bumps and ingrown hairs

Why curly hair grows back into the skin after shaving, how to shave to prevent razor bumps, and when to see a doctor. With the research behind it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Razor bumps happen when a cut hair curls back into the skin. Tightly curled hair makes this far more likely.
  • Technique matters more than products: leave a little length, wet the hair first, and do not stretch the skin.
  • The bumps often leave dark marks, so preventing them protects your skin tone too.

Strong evidence

Why it happens

Doctors call it pseudofolliculitis barbae. In tightly curled hair the follicle itself is curved, so a freshly cut, sharp hair tip can grow sideways or downwards and pierce the skin next to it. The body treats that hair as a splinter and the area becomes inflamed.

It is most common in men of African descent who shave, but it can affect anyone with curly hair on any shaved or plucked area, including the legs, underarms and bikini line. A variant in one hair keratin gene raises the chance about sixfold.

In one study of people with razor bumps, nine in ten also reported dark marks.

Strong evidence

How to shave if you get razor bumps

  • Pause shaving for around four weeks if you can. This lets trapped hairs grow out.
  • Use electric clippers that leave about 1 mm of stubble. A hair that short is rarely sharp enough to re-enter the skin.
  • If you use a razor, wet and soften the hair first. Dry shaving leaves sharp, slanted tips.
  • Shave in the direction the hair grows and do not pull the skin tight. A hair cut under tension springs back below the surface.
  • Multi-blade razors lift the hair before cutting it, which also leaves it below the surface. A single blade or a guarded razor is kinder.

Mixed evidence

Treatments a doctor can offer

If changing technique is not enough, a GP or dermatologist can prescribe creams that calm inflammation or slow regrowth. Laser hair removal can help when bumps keep returning. Researchers note that few new treatments have appeared in recent years, so prevention carries most of the weight.

When to see a doctor

Painful lumps, pus, bumps that scar or thicken, or bumps on the back of the neck that keep growing.

Common questions

Do razor bumps go away on their own?

Often, if you stop shaving for about four weeks so the trapped hairs can grow out. The dark marks they leave can take longer to fade.

Is waxing or plucking better than shaving?

Not usually. Plucking can also trigger the same reaction, because the new hair has to grow back through a curved follicle.
