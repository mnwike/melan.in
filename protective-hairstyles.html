Protective hairstyles: which ones protect

Braids, twists, wigs and weaves are called protective styles, but tight or heavy ones cause hair loss. How to choose and how long to keep them in.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A style protects only if it reduces handling without adding tension.
  • Research ranks braids, locs, weaves and extensions as the highest-risk styles for hair loss when they pull.
  • Loose, light, and out on time: those three rules matter more than the style you pick.

Strong evidence

What the evidence says

A Johns Hopkins review of 19 studies sorted styles by risk of traction alopecia. Loose buns and hair worn down were lowest risk. Braids, locs, weaves and extensions were highest, most of all on relaxed hair. The reviewers stressed that many of these styles make hair easier to manage, so the answer is to wear them safely.

Strong evidence

Rules that keep a style protective

  • No pain, bumps or headaches after installing.
  • Leave the hairline out of tight braiding where you can.
  • Braids out after two to three months at most. Weaves and extensions out after six to eight weeks.
  • Keep washing and moisturising the scalp and hair while the style is in.
  • Take a break between installs.

Limited evidence

Lower-tension options

  • Loose twists and chunky braids.
  • Wigs worn over loose cornrows, without glue on the hairline.
  • Buns and puffs that are not pulled tight.

When to see a doctor

A hairline that is moving back, or bumps and soreness that persist after the style is removed.

Common questions

How long should I keep braids in?

No more than two to three months, and less if they feel tight or heavy.

Are wigs a protective style?

They can be, if the hair underneath is braided loosely and no glue is used on the hairline.
