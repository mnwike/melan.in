Salicylic acid for Black and brown skin

Salicylic acid unblocks pores and smooths bumps. How to use it on brown and black skin for acne, razor bumps and rough skin without causing dark marks.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Salicylic acid works inside oily pores, so it suits blackheads, spots and ingrown hairs.
  • On darker skin the main risk is irritation, which leaves dark marks. Start low and slow.
  • It helps marks fade only indirectly, by preventing the next spot.

Strong evidence

What it is

Salicylic acid is a beta hydroxy acid related to aspirin. It dissolves in oil, so it gets into pores and loosens the plug of dead cells and sebum. It also calms inflammation a little. Products range from 0.5% to 2% for the face, with higher strengths for warts, corns and thick scalp scale.

Mixed evidence

What it helps

  • Blackheads, whiteheads and mild acne.
  • Razor bumps and ingrown hairs on the beard area, bikini line and legs.
  • Keratosis pilaris and 'strawberry legs'.
  • Scalp scale in dandruff and psoriasis, as a shampoo.
  • Rough skin on feet and elbows, at higher strengths.

Mixed evidence

How to use it on darker skin

  • Begin with a 0.5 to 2% cleanser or leave-on product two or three nights a week.
  • Increase only if there is no stinging, peeling or tightness.
  • Moisturise after, and wear sunscreen in the morning.
  • Do not layer it with retinoids, glycolic acid or benzoyl peroxide on the same night at first.
  • Do not use it on broken, freshly shaved or irritated skin.

Strong evidence

Who should avoid it

  • People allergic to aspirin.
  • Large areas of skin in young children.
  • In pregnancy, small amounts in a face wash are generally considered acceptable, but avoid peels and large areas and ask your midwife.

When to see a doctor

Spots that scar, deep painful lumps, or acne that has not improved after three months of pharmacy treatment.

Common questions

Is salicylic acid safe for dark skin?

Yes at low strengths. Irritation is the risk, because irritated dark skin leaves marks, so build up slowly.
