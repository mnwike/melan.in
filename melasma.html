Melasma

Melasma causes brown or grey-brown patches on the face. What triggers it, why tinted sunscreen matters, and which treatments have evidence.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Melasma is a long-term pigment condition that comes and goes. There is no permanent cure yet.
  • Light is the main trigger, including visible light that ordinary sunscreen does not block.
  • Tinted sunscreen with iron oxides is the base of every treatment plan.

Strong evidence

What it is

Melasma shows as symmetrical brown or grey-brown patches, usually on the cheeks, forehead and upper lip. It affects people with darker skin more often. Pregnancy and some medicines can set it off, and those cases often settle once the trigger is gone. For most people it is chronic, with flares in sunnier months.

Strong evidence

Why your sunscreen may not be enough

Melasma pigment can be switched on by UVB, UVA and short-wavelength visible light. Ordinary broad-spectrum sunscreens block UV only. Tinted sunscreens contain iron oxides, which also block visible light, and studies find they prevent relapses better than untinted ones. Visible light passes through windows, so protection matters indoors near daylight too.

Strong evidence

Treatments

Creams that slow pigment production are the main treatment. Hydroquinone and a triple-combination cream (hydroquinone, a retinoid and a mild steroid) are the reference medicines, and both need a prescription and a time limit. Peels, lasers and microneedling exist, but on darker skin they carry a real risk of making pigment worse and belong with an experienced dermatologist.

When to see a doctor

Before starting any prescription-strength fading cream, and if patches appear suddenly or alongside other symptoms.

Common questions

Can melasma be cured?

Not permanently at present. It can be faded and kept under control with daily light protection and treatment.

What is the best sunscreen for melasma?

A broad-spectrum tinted sunscreen that contains iron oxides, in a shade you will wear every day.
