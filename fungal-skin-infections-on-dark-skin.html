Fungal skin infections on dark skin

Ringworm, jock itch, athlete's foot, tinea versicolor and yeast rashes on brown and black skin: how each looks, how to treat it, and the steroid cream trap.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Fungal infections thrive in warmth and sweat and are common in humid climates.
  • On dark skin they look brown, grey or purple, or leave lighter patches, more than red.
  • Antifungal cream treats most. Steroid creams, alone or in mixed 'triple' creams, make them spread.

Mixed evidence

The main types

  • Ringworm of the body: a ring with a raised, scaly edge.
  • Jock itch: an itchy, darker rash in the groin and inner thighs, sparing the scrotum.
  • Athlete's foot: peeling and itching between the toes or on the soles.
  • Tinea versicolor: light or dark finely scaly patches on the chest and back.
  • Yeast (candida) rash: sore, moist rash in skin folds, with small spots at the edge.
  • Nail fungus: thick, discoloured, crumbly nails.

Strong evidence

Treatment

  • Terbinafine or clotrimazole cream from a pharmacy, for two to four weeks.
  • Continue for one to two weeks after it clears.
  • Keep the area dry. Loose cotton clothing and a separate towel.
  • Antifungal shampoo as a body wash for tinea versicolor.
  • Tablets, prescribed, for scalp, nail or widespread infection.

Strong evidence

The steroid cream trap

Creams combining a strong steroid with an antifungal and antibiotic are sold cheaply over the counter in India and across Africa. They calm the itch quickly while the fungus spreads and becomes resistant, and they thin and lighten the skin. Hard-to-treat ringworm has become a serious problem in South Asia for this reason. Use a plain antifungal.

When to see a doctor

No improvement after two weeks of antifungal cream, large or many patches, scalp or nail involvement, diabetes, or a weak immune system.

Common questions

What does a fungal infection look like on dark skin?

A scaly patch that is brown, grey or purple, often with a raised edge, or lighter scaly patches on the chest and back.
