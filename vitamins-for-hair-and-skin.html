Vitamins for hair and skin: what works

Biotin, vitamin D, iron, collagen and 'hair, skin and nails' supplements: which have evidence, who needs them, and the lab-test risk of biotin.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Supplements fix deficiencies. They do little for people who are not deficient.
  • For darker skin in the UK, vitamin D is the one supplement with official backing.
  • High-dose biotin has thin evidence and can distort blood tests, including one used to diagnose heart attacks.

Strong evidence

The biotin problem

Reviews find no clinical trials showing that biotin on its own improves hair in healthy people eating a normal diet. In every published case where it helped, the person had an underlying deficiency or disorder. In 2017, and again in 2019, the US Food and Drug Administration warned that high-dose biotin can give false results on laboratory tests, including thyroid, vitamin D and pregnancy hormone tests and the troponin test used for heart attacks.

Mixed evidence

Test before you supplement

Doctors assessing hair loss usually check iron stores, thyroid function, vitamin D and sometimes zinc and B12. A supplement makes sense when one of those is low. Tell whoever takes your blood about any supplements, and stop biotin for a few days beforehand if they advise it.

Mixed evidence

What does more than a pill

  • For skin: sunscreen, moisturiser, not smoking.
  • For hair: low-tension styles, less heat, and treating scalp problems early.
  • For both: enough protein, and a diet that covers iron and vitamin D.

When to see a doctor

Sudden shedding, tiredness with hair loss, or heavy periods: ask for a blood test before buying supplements.

Common questions

Does biotin help hair grow?

Only if you are deficient, which is rare. Trials in healthy people show no benefit.

Which vitamin is best for Black skin?

Vitamin D is the one with official UK backing, because darker skin makes less of it from weak sunlight.
