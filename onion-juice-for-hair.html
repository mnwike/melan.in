Onion juice and onion oil for hair

Onion juice and onion hair oil are among the most searched hair remedies in India. The one study behind them, how people use it, and the downsides.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • One small study in 2002 tested onion juice, and only in alopecia areata, a patchy immune hair loss.
  • There is no trial for ordinary thinning, hair fall or growth speed.
  • It can irritate the scalp, and the smell lingers.

Mixed evidence

Where the idea comes from

In a study from Iraq published in 2002, people with patchy alopecia areata applied raw onion juice twice a day and were compared with people who applied tap water. More of the onion group regrew hair by six weeks. The study was small, was not blinded, and alopecia areata often regrows by itself. It has not been repeated properly.

Limited evidence

What it cannot tell us

  • Nothing about pattern hair loss, traction alopecia, breakage or shedding after illness.
  • Nothing about bottled onion oils, which contain a small amount of extract in a carrier oil.
  • The sulphur in onions is often given as the reason it works. That is a theory, not a finding.

Limited evidence

If you try it

  • Patch test behind the ear for a day first.
  • Apply strained juice to the scalp for fifteen to thirty minutes, then shampoo.
  • Stop if it burns, itches or the scalp turns flaky.
  • Keep it away from the eyes.
  • A bottled onion oil is mainly a hair oil. Expect softer hair, not new hair.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does onion juice regrow hair?

One small study found regrowth in patchy alopecia areata. There is no evidence for other types of hair loss.
