Retinol and retinoids for dark skin

Retinoids fade dark marks and treat acne in Black and brown skin, but irritation can cause new marks. Which to choose and how to start safely.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Retinoids are among the best-evidenced treatments for dark marks and acne in skin of colour.
  • The risk is irritation, which can itself leave marks. Start low and slow.
  • Not for use in pregnancy. Always with sunscreen.

Strong evidence

What they are

Retinoids are the vitamin A family. Retinol is the weaker, shop-bought form. Adapalene is sold in pharmacies for acne. Tretinoin and others need a prescription. A clinical review of trials in patients with skin of colour concluded that topical retinoids should be considered a first-line treatment for acne with hyperpigmentation.

Mixed evidence

How to start

  • Pick one product. Adapalene 0.1% or a low-strength retinol.
  • A pea-sized amount for the whole face, at night, two or three times a week.
  • Moisturiser before or after to buffer it.
  • Increase to nightly over six to eight weeks if your skin stays calm.
  • Sunscreen every morning.

Mixed evidence

Is darker skin more easily irritated?

Not necessarily. In pooled trial data, patients with darker skin types were no more prone to irritation than those with lighter skin. What differs is the consequence: irritation in darker skin is more likely to leave a mark. In one comparison, adapalene irritated less than tretinoin.

Mixed evidence

What to expect

  • Weeks one to four: dryness and flaking are common. Ease off if it stings or burns.
  • Three months: clearer skin and fading marks.
  • It is a long-term treatment, not a quick fix.

When to see a doctor

Burning, swelling or new dark patches after starting a retinoid, or if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

Common questions

Is retinol good for black skin?

Yes. Retinoids treat acne and fade dark marks, provided you start gently to avoid irritation.

Which retinoid is best for hyperpigmentation?

Prescription tretinoin and pharmacy adapalene have the most evidence. Adapalene tends to irritate less.
