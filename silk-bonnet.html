Do silk bonnets work?

What a silk or satin bonnet does for curly and afro hair at night, what the evidence supports, and how to choose between silk and satin.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A bonnet reduces rubbing between hair and pillow and stops cotton drawing moisture out.
  • The evidence is mostly expert opinion and fabric tests, not trials on hair. The logic is sound and the risk is nil.
  • Silk or satin both work. Fit matters more than fabric.

Limited evidence

What it does

Friction lifts and wears the cuticle, the outer layer of the hair. Curly and coily hair is drier and more prone to breakage, so it has more to lose. A smooth surface lets hair slide. Dermatologist Dr Janiene Luke notes there is some evidence that silk absorbs less moisture than cotton.

Limited evidence

Silk or satin?

Silk is a natural fibre. Satin is a weave, usually polyester, and costs much less. Both give a smooth surface. Nobody has shown in a proper trial that one protects hair better than the other.

Limited evidence

Getting the most from it

  • Choose a band that stays on without pressing on the hairline. A tight band can thin the edges.
  • Wash it weekly: it collects product and oil.
  • A silk or satin pillowcase is a good backup for children and for anyone who loses the bonnet in the night.

When to see a doctor

A bonnet will not fix hair loss. See a doctor for thinning, bald patches or a sore scalp.

Common questions

Is a silk bonnet better than satin?

No trial has shown a difference for hair. Both are smooth; satin is cheaper.

Should children wear a bonnet?

It can help keep styles and moisture in. A satin pillowcase is easier for young children.
