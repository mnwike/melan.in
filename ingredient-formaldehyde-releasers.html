Ingredients / Preservative

Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives

Releases small amounts of formaldehyde to stop microbes.

General information from cosmetic science. Not yet clinically reviewed.

Well established

What the science says

Formaldehyde is a known skin allergen. These preservatives are legal within limits and can trigger reactions in sensitised people.

Restricted

Permitted within set limits; must carry a warning above a threshold.

Cancer evidence

They release formaldehyde, a known human carcinogen when breathed in. The amounts from a preserved cream or shampoo are small, and the cancer evidence comes from much higher airborne exposure.

Other known downsides

Contact allergy and irritation, especially in people already sensitised to formaldehyde.

How it appears on a label

Look for: dmdm hydantoin, imidazolidinyl urea, diazolidinyl urea, quaternium-15, bronopol, hydroxymethylglycinate.
