How hair differs between populations

What research has measured about hair in people of African, European and Asian ancestry: shape, thickness, density, growth, strength, greying and hair loss.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • The chemistry of hair is the same in everyone. The shape is what differs.
  • On average: East Asian hair is thickest and straightest, European hair is densest, African hair is most coiled and most fragile.
  • Curl type predicts how hair behaves far better than ancestry does.

Strong evidence

Read this first

The studies behind this page use labels such as 'African', 'Asian' and 'Caucasian'. Those are rough social groupings, not biological types. Each group contains huge variety, the ranges overlap, and many studies measured only a few dozen people in one city. Read every figure here as an average from a sample, never as a rule about a person.

Strong evidence

The same material

Chemical analysis finds the same keratin proteins and amino acids in hair from people of African, Asian and European ancestry. No group has 'stronger' or 'weaker' hair as a substance. The fibre's geometry does the work.

Strong evidence

Shape and thickness

In the studies, East Asian hair has the largest and roundest cross-section and is the straightest. European hair is thinner and round to oval, ranging from straight to curly. African hair is flattened and elliptical, with the tightest curl and twists along its length. South Asian hair is usually described as straight to wavy and relatively thick, but it has been studied far less.

Mixed evidence

Density and growth

A study of 511 volunteers found European participants had the most hairs per square centimetre, African participants the fewest and the slowest growth, and Asian participants fast growth with lower density. A later study of 2,249 young adults in 24 ethnic groups confirmed lower density and slower growth in African hair overall, while stressing that scalp area, sex and geography shift the numbers too. Coiled hair looks fuller than its density suggests because it stands away from the scalp.

Mixed evidence

Strength and damage

Coiled, flattened fibres have weak points at each bend, so they break at lower force and knot more. In straight hair, wear accumulates at the old ends. In African hair, damage is found nearer the root. This is why length retention, not growth, is the usual challenge.

Limited evidence

Oil and dryness

Scalp oil runs easily down a straight fibre and poorly along a coil. So straight hair gets greasy at the root within days, while tightly coiled hair is dry along its length even on an oily scalp. Wash and moisturising routines differ for that mechanical reason.

Limited evidence

Greying and ageing

A review of 69 papers put the average start of greying in the mid-thirties for people of European ancestry, the late thirties for Asian ancestry and the mid-forties for African ancestry.

Mixed evidence

Hair loss patterns

  • Male and female pattern thinning occurs everywhere. In the 511-person study it was most common in European men and least in Asian men.
  • Traction alopecia and CCCA are seen mostly in women of African ancestry, where hair fragility, styling and inherited susceptibility meet.
  • Razor bumps and acne keloidalis follow tightly curled hair, whatever a person's ancestry.
  • Alopecia areata, the patchy autoimmune kind, affects all groups.

Limited evidence

What is not known

Mixed-heritage hair, South Asian, Middle Eastern, Latin American and Pacific hair are thinly covered. The largest studies were run by industry laboratories. Few separated the effect of grooming from the effect of the fibre.

When to see a doctor

Sudden, patchy, painful or scarring hair loss in anyone, of any hair type.

Common questions

Is African hair weaker than European hair?

The material is the same. Its coiled, flattened shape creates weak points, so it breaks at lower force.

Which hair grows fastest?

In the published studies East Asian hair grew fastest on average and African hair slowest, with wide overlap.
