Keratosis pilaris on dark skin

Keratosis pilaris causes rough bumps on the upper arms and thighs. On dark skin the bumps are brown or skin-coloured. What smooths them.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Keratosis pilaris is harmless, inherited and very common. It is not acne and not an infection.
  • On dark skin the bumps are skin-coloured, brown or grey, sometimes with a dark dot.
  • It can be smoothed, not cured. It returns when treatment stops and often fades with age.

Strong evidence

What it is

Each bump is a hair follicle plugged with keratin, the protein that makes up the outer skin. It gives a sandpaper feel on the backs of the upper arms, the thighs, buttocks and sometimes the cheeks. It runs in families, is worse in winter and in dry skin, and is more common in people with eczema.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Moisturiser with urea 10%, lactic acid or salicylic acid, daily.
  • Short, lukewarm showers and a soap-free wash.
  • Gentle chemical exfoliation. Not scrubs, loofahs or picking.
  • A retinoid cream for stubborn areas, on advice.
  • Sunscreen on exposed arms to stop the dots darkening.

Mixed evidence

On darker skin

The redness described in most guides is replaced by brown or grey dots, and scratching or scrubbing leaves more pigment behind. Laser hair removal can help when coiled hairs are trapped in the plugs, using a laser suited to dark skin.

When to see a doctor

Bumps that are painful, filled with pus, spreading quickly or leaving scars.

Common questions

How do you get rid of keratosis pilaris?

It cannot be cured, but a daily urea or lactic acid moisturiser smooths it.
