When your child's hair is different from yours

For parents, step-parents, adoptive and foster carers and grandparents caring for a child whose curls are new to them. Where to start and who to ask.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • You do not need to have this hair to care for it well. You need a routine and a willingness to ask.
  • Mixed-heritage children often have several curl patterns on one head. Treat the hair in front of you, not a label.
  • How you talk about your child's hair matters as much as what you put on it.

Limited evidence

Who this is for

A white mother of a mixed-heritage daughter. A Black father whose son's looser curls behave nothing like his own. A South Asian stepmother, an adoptive or foster carer, a grandparent doing the school run. The details differ, and the starting point is the same.

Limited evidence

First steps

  • Learn the basic routine in our parent's guide: wash, condition, detangle wet, style loosely.
  • Find a salon or barber that specialises in textured hair and book a visit. Ask them to show you, not only to do it.
  • Ask relatives or friends who share your child's hair type. Most people are glad to be asked.
  • Give it time. Wash day is slow at first and gets quicker.

Limited evidence

Words matter

Children notice how adults describe their hair. Words like difficult, wild or unmanageable stay with them. Carers' organisations describe hair care as a visible act of love and a link to a child's heritage, particularly for children in foster and adoptive families. Let your child choose styles, and make sure they see hair like theirs in books and on screen.

Limited evidence

For foster and adoptive carers

Ask the child's social worker or birth family what routine and styles they are used to. Keeping a familiar style can be steadying during a move. Budget for the right products and salon visits as an ordinary cost of care.

When to see a doctor

The same signs as for any child: patches of hair loss, a scaly or sore scalp, or thinning at the hairline.

Common questions

How do I look after mixed-race hair?

Keep it moisturised, detangle it wet with conditioner, style it loosely, and learn from a stylist who specialises in textured hair.

What products should I buy first?

A gentle shampoo, a rich conditioner, a leave-in or detangling spray and a wide-tooth comb. Add more only if you find a need.
