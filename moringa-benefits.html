Moringa for skin and hair

Moringa leaves are nutritious and moringa oil is a stable skin and hair oil. What the research supports, what it does not, and who should be careful.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Moringa leaves are a good source of iron, vitamin A, vitamin C and protein.
  • No human trial shows that moringa grows hair, clears skin or fades marks.
  • Its value is as food where diets lack those nutrients, and as a pleasant oil.

Strong evidence

What it is

Moringa oleifera, the drumstick tree, is native to South Asia and grown widely across Africa and the Caribbean. The leaves are eaten fresh or as powder, the pods are cooked, and the seeds are pressed for oil, sometimes called ben oil.

Limited evidence

What the evidence shows

  • Leaves: nutritious. Useful against deficiency, which is itself a cause of hair shedding and dull skin.
  • Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects in laboratory and animal studies.
  • Small human studies on blood sugar and cholesterol, with mixed results.
  • Skin and hair: no good human trials.
  • Seed oil: high in oleic acid, resists going rancid, light on skin and hair.

Mixed evidence

Using it safely

  • Treat leaf powder as a food: a teaspoon or two a day.
  • It can lower blood sugar and blood pressure. Check with a doctor if you take medicines for either, or for thyroid disease.
  • Avoid root, bark and concentrated extracts in pregnancy.
  • Buy from suppliers that test for heavy metals and contamination.

When to see a doctor

Hair loss or tiredness that continues: ask for iron, vitamin D and thyroid tests instead of relying on supplements.

Common questions

Is moringa good for hair growth?

Only indirectly, by correcting a poor diet. No trial shows it grows hair.
