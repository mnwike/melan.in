Vitamin D for people with dark skin

UK health advice is for people with dark skin to consider a daily 10 microgram vitamin D supplement all year. Doses by age and why melanin matters.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Melanin filters the sunlight skin uses to make vitamin D.
  • In the UK, nobody can make vitamin D from sunlight between October and early March.
  • Official advice for people with dark skin: consider 10 micrograms a day, all year.

Strong evidence

Why darker skin needs more sun

Skin makes vitamin D when sunlight reaches it. Darker skin needs longer in the sun to make the same amount, and low vitamin D levels are more common in people with dark skin living at northern latitudes.

Strong evidence

UK doses by age

  • Babies under one year: 8.5 to 10 micrograms a day.
  • Children aged one to four: 10 micrograms a day.
  • Everyone aged four and over: 10 micrograms a day in autumn and winter.
  • People with dark skin, including African, African-Caribbean and South Asian backgrounds: consider 10 micrograms a day throughout the year.

Strong evidence

Safety

Ten micrograms is 400 international units. More is not better. The NHS publishes safe upper limits, and a pharmacist can check your dose against other medicines. Families on low incomes may qualify for free vitamins through the Healthy Start scheme.

When to see a doctor

Bone pain, muscle weakness, or a child with delayed walking or bowed legs.

Common questions

Should Black people take vitamin D in the UK?

UK health advice is to consider a daily 10 microgram supplement all year.

How much vitamin D should a child take?

Babies under one need 8.5 to 10 micrograms a day and children aged one to four need 10, under UK guidance.
