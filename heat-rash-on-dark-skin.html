Heat rash and prickly heat on dark skin

Heat rash is blocked sweat. On dark skin it shows as tiny skin-coloured, dark or clear bumps that prickle. How to cool it, and what to avoid in humid weather.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Heat rash is trapped sweat. It is harmless and clears when the skin cools.
  • On dark skin look for tiny raised bumps, clear, skin-coloured or slightly darker, that prickle or itch.
  • Thick creams and oils in hot, humid weather make it worse.

Mixed evidence

What it looks like

Clusters of pinhead bumps or tiny clear blisters, on the neck, chest, back, groin, under the breasts and in skin folds. Babies get it on the face and nappy area. The prickling, stinging itch is the giveaway. It is one of the most searched skin problems in South Africa, the Caribbean and India, and catches out people from cooler countries on holiday.

Strong evidence

Relief

  • Cool the skin: a cool shower, a fan, loose cotton clothes.
  • Calamine lotion, or an antihistamine for itch.
  • Avoid heavy butters, petroleum jelly and oils on affected areas until it settles.
  • Do not scratch. Scratched heat rash can become infected and leave dark marks.
  • It clears in a few days once you stay cool.

Limited evidence

Preventing it

In heat and humidity, switch to a light lotion or gel moisturiser, change out of sweaty clothes promptly, and avoid dressing babies in too many layers. Talc is no longer recommended, especially for babies.

When to see a doctor

Bumps that fill with pus, spreading soreness, fever, or signs of heat exhaustion such as dizziness, headache, cramps and feeling sick.

Common questions

What does heat rash look like on black skin?

Tiny clear, skin-coloured or dark bumps in clusters that prickle or itch, in sweaty areas.
