Colours and make-up for brown and black skin

How to find your undertone, match foundation on brown and black skin, avoid an ashy cast, and choose clothing and hair colours that suit deep skin tones.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Undertone matters more than depth. Brown and black skin can be warm, cool, neutral or olive.
  • Ashy foundation is usually the wrong undertone or too light, not too little product.
  • No colour is off limits. Contrast and saturation are what make a colour work on deep skin.

Limited evidence

Finding your undertone

  • Warm: golden, yellow or red tones. Gold jewellery tends to flatter.
  • Cool: blue or red-blue tones. Silver tends to flatter.
  • Neutral or olive: a mix, sometimes with a green cast.
  • Look at your chest and jawline in daylight, not your wrist veins, which are hard to read on dark skin.
  • Many people are two shades: a lighter centre of the face and a deeper perimeter.

Limited evidence

Foundation and concealer

  • Match to the jaw and neck in daylight, and check a photo taken with flash.
  • Grey or ashy means too cool, too light, or a formula with too much white pigment.
  • Orange or peach corrector under concealer cancels dark circles on deep skin.
  • Mineral sunscreen underneath can cause a white cast. Use a tinted or chemical-filter sunscreen.
  • Shade ranges have improved a great deal. Brands that test on deep skin tones are worth favouring.

Limited evidence

Clothing and hair colour

Deep skin carries saturated colour beautifully: cobalt, emerald, fuchsia, orange, white, yellow and jewel tones. Pastels and neutrals work when they contrast with your skin, and can wash out when they match it. For hair, warm browns, copper, burgundy and honey blondes are common choices, though bleaching afro-textured hair to blonde weakens it considerably. Always patch test hair dye.

When to see a doctor

A rash, swelling or burning after make-up or hair dye.

Common questions

What colours look good on brown skin?

Saturated and jewel tones, white, and any shade that contrasts with your skin. Undertone guides the rest.
