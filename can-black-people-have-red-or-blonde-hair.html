Guides / The big conversations

Can Black people have red, blonde or straight hair?

Yes. Red, blonde and straight hair all occur naturally in people of African and Melanesian ancestry. The genetics, and when a colour change is a health sign.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Yes to all three. Natural red, blonde and loosely curled or straight hair occur in Black people.
  • The genes involved are sometimes shared with Europeans and sometimes entirely separate.
  • Hair that lightens during childhood illness or poor nutrition is a medical sign, not genetics.

Mixed evidence

Red hair

Red hair comes from variants in a gene called MC1R that shift pigment from brown-black towards red-yellow. Those variants are most common in northern Europe but exist in African populations too, and red or auburn hair appears in families across the Caribbean, West and southern Africa, with and without known European ancestry. A form of albinism common in southern Africa also gives red-bronze skin and ginger hair.

Strong evidence

Blonde hair

Around one in ten people in the Solomon Islands, who have some of the darkest skin outside Africa, have naturally blonde hair. In 2012 researchers traced it to a variant in a gene called TYRP1 that is not found in Europeans. Blonde hair evolved there independently. Blonde and light brown hair also occur in parts of Africa, in Aboriginal Australians, and with albinism.

Mixed evidence

Straight and loosely curled hair

Africa holds more genetic diversity than the rest of the world put together, and hair there ranges from tight coils to loose curls and waves, for example in parts of the Horn of Africa. Curl is shaped by many genes, so two parents with coily hair can have a child with looser curls.

Strong evidence

When colour change is a health sign

Hair that turns reddish or pale in a child who is unwell or underfed can be a sign of severe protein deficiency. Sudden patches of white hair can follow vitiligo or alopecia areata. Those are reasons to see a doctor.

When to see a doctor

A child whose hair is lightening along with poor growth or swelling, or new white patches of hair or skin.

Common questions

Can Black people have natural blonde hair?

Yes. In the Solomon Islands it comes from a gene variant not found in Europeans.

Can Black people have natural red hair?

Yes. Variants in the MC1R gene, and a form of albinism, both produce red hair in people of African ancestry.
