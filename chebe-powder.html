Chebe powder

Chebe powder comes from Chad and is used for length, not growth. What is in it, how Basara women use it, the evidence, and how to try it safely.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Chebe does not make hair grow. It is used to stop hair breaking, so length is kept.
  • The method matters as much as the powder: hair is coated, braided and left alone.
  • It goes on the lengths, never the scalp.

Strong evidence

What it is

Chebe is a powder made from the seeds of a shrub that grows in Chad, mixed with cloves, resin and other plants. Basara Arab women in Chad mix it with oil or butter, coat the lengths of their hair, braid it, and repeat every few days without washing it out for long periods. Many have hair to the waist.

Limited evidence

Why it seems to work

No clinical study exists. The likely explanation is mechanical. The paste coats each strand, the hair stays braided and is not combed or heat-styled, and it stays moisturised under the coating. Those three things reduce breakage in tightly coiled hair whatever the coating is made of.

Limited evidence

Trying it

  • Mix the powder with an oil or butter into a paste, or buy a chebe butter or oil.
  • Apply to damp lengths in sections, then braid or twist.
  • Keep it off the scalp. The gritty powder can irritate and block follicles.
  • It is messy and stains fabric.
  • Clove and fragrance can cause allergic rashes. Patch test.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does chebe powder grow hair?

No. It helps hair keep its length by reducing breakage.
