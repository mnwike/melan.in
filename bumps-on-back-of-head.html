Bumps on the back of the head and neck

Hard bumps at the back of the scalp after haircuts are usually acne keloidalis nuchae. What it is, why Black men get it, and why early treatment matters.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Firm bumps where the hair meets the back of the neck are usually acne keloidalis nuchae, or AKN.
  • It is not an infection and you cannot catch it from a barber's clippers.
  • Treated early it can be controlled. Left alone, bumps can join into scars with permanent hair loss.

Strong evidence

What it is

AKN is a long-term inflammation of the hair follicles on the back of the scalp and neck. It starts as small, itchy bumps and can harden into raised scars, sometimes with pus, tufts of hair and bald patches. Despite the name it is not acne, and the scars are not true keloids.

Strong evidence

Who gets it

Mostly teenage and adult men of African or Afro-Caribbean descent. Studies put it at between 0.5% and 13.6% of African American men, with similar figures in Nigeria, South Africa and West Africa. Men are affected about twenty times as often as women. It usually starts after puberty and is rare after 55.

Mixed evidence

What sets it off

  • Close haircuts and skin fades at the nape, especially frequent ones.
  • Rubbing: shirt collars, helmets, hard hats, durags and chains.
  • Picking, scratching or trying to shave the bumps off.

The American Academy of Dermatology is clear that dirty clippers do not cause it. But active bumps bleed during haircuts, so each client should get clean equipment.

Mixed evidence

What to do now

  • Ask your barber to leave the nape longer: a low taper instead of a skin fade, and no razor or edger over the bumps.
  • Stop the rubbing: looser collars, a satin lining in caps and helmets.
  • Do not pick, squeeze or apply acids, oils or home remedies. Reviews report that these leave bigger scars.
  • See a doctor early. Steroid creams or injections, antibiotics and retinoids can settle early disease. Large scars need laser or surgery.

When to see a doctor

Any firm bumps at the nape that last more than a few weeks, or bumps that are painful, weeping or leaving bald patches. Early treatment prevents scarring.

Common questions

Why do I get bumps on the back of my head after a haircut?

Close cuts at the nape irritate curved hair follicles and can trigger acne keloidalis nuchae, a long-term inflammation that is common in Black men.

Did I catch it from the barber's clippers?

No. It is not contagious. Clean clippers still matter because the bumps can bleed.
