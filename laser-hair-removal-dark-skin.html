Laser hair removal on dark skin

Laser hair removal can be safe on Black and brown skin with the right laser. Which lasers suit dark skin, the risks, and questions to ask a clinic.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • It can be safe on dark skin, with the right machine and an experienced operator.
  • The laser to ask for is a long-pulsed Nd:YAG at 1064 nanometres.
  • The wrong laser, or IPL, can burn dark skin and leave lasting marks.

Strong evidence

Why the wavelength matters

Lasers remove hair by heating the pigment in the follicle. Dark skin has pigment in the surface layer too, which can soak up the energy and burn. The Nd:YAG laser's longer wavelength reaches deeper and is absorbed less by pigment at the surface, which is why medical references list it for all skin types, including the darkest.

Mixed evidence

Does it work?

In a 2025 study of 55 Sudanese women with brown to deep brown skin, low-energy Nd:YAG treatment reduced hair with no adverse events. Expect a course of sessions, and reduction more than permanent removal.

Strong evidence

The risks

  • Burns, blisters and dark or light patches if the wrong device or settings are used.
  • Paradoxical hair growth: more hair, not less, near the treated area. It is uncommon, and reported most with IPL.
  • Flare-ups if you have active eczema or a recent tan in the area.

Mixed evidence

A medical use too

Laser hair removal is a recognised treatment for stubborn razor bumps and for bumps at the back of the scalp, because removing the hair removes the cause.

Mixed evidence

Questions to ask a clinic

  • Which laser do you use on skin like mine? You want to hear 'Nd:YAG'.
  • How many patients with my skin tone do you treat?
  • Will you do a test patch and wait before a full session?
  • Who supervises, and what do you do if I burn?

When to see a doctor

Blistering, crusting or a patch that turns lighter or darker after treatment. See a GP and stop further sessions.

Common questions

Is laser hair removal safe for black skin?

Yes, with a long-pulsed Nd:YAG laser and an operator experienced with dark skin.

What is paradoxical hypertrichosis?

Extra hair growth near a lasered area. It is uncommon and reported most often with IPL.
