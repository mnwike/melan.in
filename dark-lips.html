Dark lips

Darker lips are normal in brown and black skin. When darkening is new, the causes include smoking, lip products, sun and licking. What helps and what to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Naturally brown, purple-brown or two-toned lips are normal in darker skin.
  • Lips that have become darker usually have a cause: smoking, a product, sun, or irritation.
  • Lip lightening creams and scrubs tend to make things worse.

Strong evidence

Normal variation

Lips contain pigment cells like the rest of the skin. In brown and black skin the lips, gums and inside of the mouth are often naturally darker, and a darker upper lip with a pinker lower lip is common. None of that needs treatment.

Mixed evidence

Why lips darken

  • Smoking, including shisha: the commonest cause of new darkening of lips and gums.
  • Allergy or irritation from lipstick, balm, toothpaste or mouthwash.
  • Sun. Lips have little natural protection.
  • Lip licking and biting.
  • Some medicines, including hydroxychloroquine and minocycline.
  • Rarely, hormone conditions or iron and B12 deficiency.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Lip balm with SPF 30 during the day, plain petroleum jelly at night.
  • Stop smoking. Colour often lightens over months.
  • Switch to fragrance-free, flavour-free lip products for six weeks to test for a reaction.
  • Do not scrub, and avoid lemon and lightening creams on lips.

When to see a doctor

New dark spots on the lips or inside the mouth, darkening with tiredness, weight loss or dizziness, or a patch that is growing, raised or bleeding.

Common questions

Why are my lips dark?

It is often natural in darker skin. New darkening is most often from smoking, a lip product, sun or licking.
