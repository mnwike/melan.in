Glycolic acid for Black and brown skin

Glycolic acid is used for dark spots, dull skin and, on social media, dark underarms. What it does, the strengths that are safe on darker skin, and the risks.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Glycolic acid removes the surface layer of dead cells, so skin looks brighter and marks fade a little faster.
  • It is the acid most likely to irritate. On dark skin, irritation causes new dark marks.
  • Using it as a deodorant on dark underarms has no trial behind it and often backfires.

Strong evidence

What it is

Glycolic acid is the smallest alpha hydroxy acid, originally from sugar cane. Because the molecule is small it gets into skin quickly, which is why it works and why it stings. Home products are usually 5 to 10%. Clinic peels are 20 to 70% and are a medical procedure.

Mixed evidence

On darker skin

  • Start at 5 to 8%, once or twice a week at night.
  • Lactic acid and mandelic acid are larger molecules and gentler. Many dermatologists prefer mandelic acid for brown and black skin.
  • Stinging that lasts more than a minute, redness, darkening or peeling means stop.
  • Sunscreen every morning. Acids make skin more sensitive to the sun.
  • Never before or after waxing, threading or shaving the same area.

Limited evidence

Dark underarms

The idea of wiping glycolic toner under the arms spread on social media. It lowers the skin's pH, which may reduce odour for some people. Underarm skin is thin, folded and often freshly shaved, so burns and darker patches are common. Dark underarms are usually caused by friction, shaving, deodorant irritation or a condition called acanthosis nigricans, which is linked to insulin resistance. An acid treats none of these causes.

Mixed evidence

Peels

Superficial glycolic peels can help melasma and marks on darker skin when done by someone experienced with skin of colour, at low strength, with skin prepared beforehand. Done badly they cause burns and long-lasting pigment change.

When to see a doctor

A burn, blistering, or a patch that turns darker or lighter after an acid. Velvety dark skin in the armpits, neck or groin: ask for a blood sugar test.

Common questions

Can I use glycolic acid on dark skin?

Yes, at low strength and slowly. Mandelic or lactic acid is gentler and often a better first choice.

Does glycolic acid lighten underarms?

There is no trial showing it does, and irritation can make them darker.
