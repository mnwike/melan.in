Albinism

Albinism is an inherited difference in melanin. How it affects skin, hair and eyesight, why sun protection is vital, and the myths that put people at risk.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Albinism is inherited. A child is born with it when both parents carry the gene, usually without knowing.
  • It reduces melanin in skin, hair and eyes, which affects eyesight and sun protection.
  • With sun protection and eye care, people with albinism live full, healthy lives.

Strong evidence

What it is

People with albinism make little or no melanin. Skin is very pale, hair is white, yellow, ginger or light brown, and eyes are light and sensitive to glare. The commonest type in people of African ancestry is called OCA2. It occurs worldwide and is more frequent in parts of sub-Saharan Africa than in Europe.

Strong evidence

Eyes

Melanin is needed for the eye and its nerve connections to develop, so almost everyone with albinism has reduced vision that glasses only partly correct, along with light sensitivity and often involuntary eye movements. Early eye checks, tinted lenses, large print and classroom support make a great difference to a child's schooling.

Strong evidence

Skin and sun

  • Skin burns very quickly and the lifetime risk of skin cancer is high, especially near the equator.
  • High-SPF sunscreen every day, reapplied, plus hats, sleeves and shade.
  • Regular skin checks. Any sore that does not heal needs a doctor promptly.
  • Vitamin D may need to come from a supplement.

Mixed evidence

Myths and safety

Albinism is not contagious, not a curse and not caused by anything the mother did. In some regions people with albinism face discrimination and violence rooted in superstition. Accurate information is protection, which is why this page exists. Albinism is also not vitiligo, which is acquired later in life.

When to see a doctor

Any skin sore or lump that does not heal within a few weeks, and regular eye and skin reviews from infancy.

Common questions

What causes albinism?

Inherited gene variants that reduce melanin. Both parents carry the gene.
