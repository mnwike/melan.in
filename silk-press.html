Silk press on natural hair

A silk press straightens natural hair with heat and no chemicals. How it is done, how long it lasts, how often is safe, and how to tell if curls are damaged.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A silk press uses a blow dryer and flat iron to straighten natural hair. No relaxer is involved.
  • It is temporary. Water or humidity brings the curl back.
  • Too hot or too often, and the curl pattern can be permanently loosened. That is heat damage.

Strong evidence

How it is done

Hair is clarified, deep conditioned, coated with heat protectant, blow-dried straight, then passed through a flat iron in small sections. The result is smooth, light hair that moves. It lasts one to three weeks if kept dry and wrapped at night.

Mixed evidence

Keeping your curls

  • One pass of the iron per section, at the lowest temperature that works. Fine or coloured hair needs less heat.
  • Always use heat protectant on fully dry hair.
  • No more than every six to eight weeks, and less is better.
  • No touch-ups with the iron between washes.
  • Wrap in silk or satin at night. Avoid steam, sweat and rain.

Mixed evidence

Spotting heat damage

After washing, damaged sections hang straight or limp where the rest coils. They feel rough and break easily. Heat-damaged hair does not recover. Protein treatments can help it hold together, and the damaged ends are trimmed over time.

When to see a doctor

Scalp burns, or hair breaking at the root after heat styling.

Common questions

Does a silk press damage natural hair?

It can if the heat is too high or it is done too often. Occasional presses with protectant are tolerated by most hair.
