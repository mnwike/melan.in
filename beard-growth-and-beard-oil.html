Beard growth and beard oil for Black men

What decides beard growth, whether beard oil or minoxidil helps, and how to care for a coily beard without ingrown hairs, dandruff or dry skin underneath.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Beard density is set by genes and hormones. No oil makes follicles appear.
  • Beard oil softens hair and moisturises the skin underneath. That is its real job.
  • Coily beards need the same care as coily hair: wash, condition, moisturise, comb gently.

Strong evidence

What decides growth

Facial hair follicles respond to testosterone and its stronger form, DHT, and how sensitive yours are is inherited. Most beards fill in through the twenties. Patchy growth on the cheeks is common in men of every ancestry and is not a deficiency.

Limited evidence

Does anything grow a beard?

  • Beard oils and 'growth' serums: no evidence they add hairs.
  • Minoxidil: small studies suggest it can thicken beard hair. It is not licensed for this, irritates some skin, and the gain may be lost on stopping.
  • Biotin and supplements: no benefit unless deficient.
  • Time: give it eight to twelve weeks without trimming before judging.
  • Smooth, sharply bordered bald patches in a beard are a medical condition, alopecia areata, and are treatable.

Mixed evidence

Looking after a coily beard

  • Wash two or three times a week with a gentle cleanser. Skin flakes under beards too.
  • Condition, then apply a light oil or butter to damp hair.
  • Comb with a wide-tooth comb or pick, from the ends.
  • Line up with a trimmer, not a razor, to avoid bumps on the neck.
  • An anti-dandruff shampoo once a week treats beard dandruff.

When to see a doctor

Round bald patches in the beard, painful bumps or pus, or scaly, itchy skin under the beard that does not settle.

Common questions

Does beard oil help growth?

No. It conditions the hair and skin. Growth depends on genes and hormones.
