Premature grey hair

Grey hair before 30 is usually inherited. The few correctable causes, which tests are worth asking for, and what does and does not bring colour back.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Greying is mostly genetic. When it starts runs in families.
  • 'Premature' means before about 20 in white people, 25 in Asian people and 30 in Black people.
  • A few causes are correctable: low vitamin B12, thyroid disease, and smoking.

Strong evidence

Why hair greys

Each follicle has pigment cells that load melanin into the growing hair. With age those cells run down and the hair grows in without colour. The timing is largely inherited. On average greying begins in the mid-thirties in people of European ancestry, the late thirties in Asian ancestry and the mid-forties in African ancestry.

Mixed evidence

Causes worth checking

  • Vitamin B12 deficiency, more likely on vegan or vegetarian diets and with some stomach medicines.
  • Thyroid disease.
  • Vitiligo and alopecia areata, which can turn patches white.
  • Smoking, linked to earlier greying in several studies.
  • Low iron, copper or vitamin D have been reported in some studies. The evidence is weaker.
  • Stress can speed greying. A small study found a few hairs regained colour when stress eased.

Mixed evidence

What works

  • Correcting a real deficiency can restore colour in new growth.
  • Otherwise, no oil, herb, shampoo or supplement reverses grey. That includes curry leaves, amla and onion.
  • Hair dye, henna, or leaving it grey are the options. Patch test dyes every time.
  • Grey afro-textured hair is often drier and more wiry. It needs more moisture.

When to see a doctor

Greying in your teens or early twenties, patches of white hair, or greying with tiredness, numbness or weight change.

Common questions

Can premature grey hair be reversed?

Only when it is caused by a correctable problem such as vitamin B12 deficiency or thyroid disease.
