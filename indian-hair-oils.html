Bhringraj, amla, onion and curry leaf for hair

Bhringraj oil, amla, onion oil and curry leaves are the leading hair remedies in India. What each is, what the research shows, and how to oil hair without harm.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • These remedies have centuries of use and very little human research.
  • Onion juice has one small trial, in patchy alopecia areata. The rest have animal or laboratory studies only.
  • Regular oiling does reduce breakage. Whichever herb is in the oil may matter less than the habit.

Strong evidence

What each one is

  • Bhringraj: an Ayurvedic herb, false daisy, infused into sesame or coconut oil.
  • Amla: Indian gooseberry, rich in vitamin C, used as oil or powder.
  • Onion oil: onion extract in a carrier oil, sold for hair fall.
  • Curry leaves: boiled in coconut oil, said to prevent greying.

Limited evidence

What the research shows

Bhringraj and amla extracts have lengthened the growth phase of hair in mice and in cell studies. Nobody has shown the same in people. A small 2002 trial found that raw onion juice on the scalp regrew hair in some people with alopecia areata, an autoimmune patchy hair loss, more than tap water did. That is not the same as the commercial oils, or as general hair fall. Nothing shows that curry leaves reverse grey hair.

Limited evidence

Oiling well

  • Warm a little oil and massage the scalp for a few minutes. Massage itself may help.
  • Leave for an hour, or overnight with a covered pillow, then wash out.
  • Once or twice a week is plenty.
  • Oil on an itchy, flaky scalp usually makes it worse. Treat the dandruff first.
  • Heavy fragrance and mustard oil are common causes of scalp rashes.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does bhringraj oil regrow hair?

It has only been tested in animals. There is no human trial.
