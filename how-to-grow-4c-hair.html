How to grow 4c hair

4c hair grows at a similar rate to other hair. The challenge is keeping the length. Shrinkage, breakage, and the routine that lets tightly coiled hair get long.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Your hair is growing. If it stays the same length, it is breaking at the ends as fast as it grows.
  • Shrinkage hides most of the length of tightly coiled hair. That is normal and healthy.
  • Growth 'in a week' is not possible. Retention over months is.

Strong evidence

Growth and shrinkage

Scalp hair grows roughly a centimetre a month, a little less on average in tightly coiled hair. 4c hair can shrink to a quarter of its stretched length, so six inches of growth may look like two. Measure progress by stretching a section, not by looking in the mirror.

Mixed evidence

Why length is lost

  • Dry combing and rough detangling.
  • Ends rubbing on collars, scarves and cotton pillowcases.
  • Heat, bleach and relaxer overlap.
  • Styles that are too tight or left in too long.
  • Single-strand knots that snap when combed.

Limited evidence

A retention routine

  • Wash every one to two weeks and condition every time.
  • Detangle only when wet and coated in conditioner, fingers first, ends to roots.
  • Moisturise with a water-based leave-in, then seal with oil or butter.
  • Keep hair in low-tension twists, braids or buns most of the time.
  • Sleep on satin. Trim split ends a few times a year.
  • Eat enough protein and check iron if shedding is heavy.

When to see a doctor

Hair that is thinning at the roots, patches, or breakage that continues despite gentle care.

Common questions

How fast does 4c hair grow?

About a centimetre a month, slightly less on average than straight hair. Breakage, not growth, limits length.
