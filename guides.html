Guides, grounded in the research

Every guide gives the short answer, what the science says and how sure it is, what helps, when to see a doctor, and the papers behind it.

Strong evidence: large studies or official reviews · Mixed: small or conflicting studies · Limited: expert opinion, little data

Skin

Razor bumps and ingrown hairsRazor bumps happen when a cut hair curls back into the skin. Tightly curled hair makes this far more likely.Dark marks after spots, cuts and rashesDark marks are the skin's pigment response to inflammation. They are more common and last longer on darker skin.MelasmaMelasma is a long-term pigment condition that comes and goes. There is no permanent cure yet.Dry and ashy skinAshiness is dry, flaking surface skin. It shows more on darker tones because the flakes look grey against the skin.Eczema on darker skinOn darker skin, eczema usually looks dark brown, purple or grey, not red.Sun, sunscreen and skin cancerMelanin gives real protection, so skin cancer is less common in darker skin.Dark underarms and a dark neckDark, thickened, velvety skin in the folds is called acanthosis nigricans. It is not dirt and scrubbing will not remove it.Keloids and raised scarsA keloid is a scar that keeps growing past the edges of the wound.Dark skin in a cold climateWhat changes for melanin-rich skin and hair far from the equator.

Hair and scalp

Thinning edges and traction alopeciaTraction alopecia is hair loss from repeated pulling on the root.Hair loss from the crown (CCCA)CCCA is the most common scarring hair loss in women of African descent.Itchy, flaky scalpPersistent flaking is usually seborrhoeic dermatitis, a reaction to a yeast that lives on everyone's scalp.4C hair: what it is and how to care for it4C hair has the tightest pattern on the hair type chart: small zigzag bends more than round curls.Hair type chart: 1 to 4C explainedThe familiar chart runs from type 1, straight, to type 4, coily, with A to C for how tight the pattern is.Hair porosity: low and high porosity explainedPorosity is how easily water moves in and out of a strand. It depends on the state of the cuticle.Do silk bonnets work?A bonnet reduces rubbing between hair and pillow and stops cotton drawing moisture out.Protective hairstyles: which ones protectA style protects only if it reduces handling without adding tension.Hair breakage: causes and fixesBreakage is hair snapping along its length. Hair loss is hair leaving the follicle. They need different answers.

Children's hair

A parent's guide to curly and coily hairCurly and coily hair is drier and more fragile than straight hair. Moisture and gentle handling solve most problems.When your child's hair is different from yoursYou do not need to have this hair to care for it well. You need a routine and a willingness to ask.Braids, extensions and tension in childrenChildren can get traction alopecia, the same hair loss from pulling that affects adults.Scalp ringworm in childrenScalp ringworm is a fungal infection, not a worm and not a sign of poor hygiene.

Products and safety

Relaxers and your healthTwo large US studies link frequent, long-term relaxer use with a higher risk of uterine cancer.Vitamin D for people with dark skinMelanin filters the sunlight skin uses to make vitamin D.Shea butter for skin: what it can and cannot doShea butter is an emollient: it softens skin and slows water loss. That part is well accepted.
