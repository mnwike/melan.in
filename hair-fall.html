Hair fall: causes and what works

Hair fall has a short list of causes. How to tell shedding from breakage and from pattern loss, which blood tests matter, and which treatments have evidence.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Losing 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal. More than that for weeks is hair fall worth looking into.
  • Sudden shedding usually follows a trigger about three months earlier: illness, childbirth, stress, crash dieting.
  • Low iron, thyroid problems and pattern hair loss are the common treatable causes.

Strong evidence

Three different problems

  • Shedding: whole hairs with a tiny white bulb, from all over the scalp. Often temporary.
  • Breakage: short snapped pieces with no bulb. A care and styling problem.
  • Pattern loss: slow thinning at the crown, parting or temples over years, with no extra hair in the brush.

Mixed evidence

Common causes

  • Illness, fever, surgery, childbirth or a major stress, about three months before.
  • Low iron, common in women with heavy periods and in vegetarian diets.
  • Thyroid disease, low vitamin D, very low protein or rapid weight loss.
  • Some medicines.
  • Tight styles, heat and chemical treatments.
  • Hard water and shampoo are often blamed and are rarely the cause.

Mixed evidence

What works

  • Find and fix the trigger. Shedding after illness or childbirth recovers by itself in six to nine months.
  • Ask for blood tests: full blood count, ferritin, thyroid, vitamin D.
  • Minoxidil is the proven treatment for pattern hair loss in men and women.
  • Biotin helps only people who are deficient, which is rare, and it interferes with blood tests.
  • Oils condition hair. None has been shown to stop hair fall.

When to see a doctor

Bald patches, a sore, scaly or scarred scalp, hair fall with tiredness, weight change or irregular periods, or shedding that continues past six months.

Common questions

How do I stop hair fall?

Find the cause first. Check iron and thyroid, ease tight styles, and use minoxidil for pattern loss.
