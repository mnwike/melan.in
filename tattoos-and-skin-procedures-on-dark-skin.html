Tattoos, peels and microneedling on dark skin

Tattoos, chemical peels and microneedling all work on Black and brown skin with the right approach. Which colours show, keloid risk, and which peels are safe.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Tattoos show well on dark skin. Bold lines and strong contrast age best.
  • Anyone prone to keloids should think carefully before a tattoo, piercing or deep peel.
  • Superficial peels and microneedling are generally safe on darker skin in experienced hands. Deep peels are not.

Limited evidence

Tattoos

  • Black, deep red, dark green and blue show most clearly. Pale colours and white are muted by the melanin above the ink.
  • Ask to see healed work on skin like yours, not fresh photos.
  • A colour test of a few dots lets you see how inks heal.
  • Overworking the skin causes scarring. A skilled artist uses a lighter hand.
  • Keloid history in you or your family: avoid tattoos on the chest, shoulders and ears, or avoid them altogether.

Mixed evidence

Chemical peels

  • Superficial peels with glycolic, salicylic, lactic or mandelic acid can help acne and dark marks.
  • Medium and deep peels carry a high risk of lasting dark or light patches in brown and black skin.
  • Skin should be prepared for some weeks beforehand and protected from sun afterwards.
  • Home peel kits above low strengths are a common cause of burns.

Mixed evidence

Microneedling and lasers

Microneedling does not rely on heat or target pigment, so it is one of the safer options for acne scars in darker skin. Lasers need the right wavelength and settings: ask which device will be used and how often the practitioner treats skin like yours. A test patch is reasonable to request for any of these.

When to see a doctor

A raised, growing or itchy scar after a tattoo or procedure, signs of infection, or new dark or white patches.

Common questions

Do tattoos show up on dark skin?

Yes. Black and strongly saturated colours with bold lines show best.

Sources

  • Structural and functional differences in skin of colour (Clinical and Experimental Dermatology, 2022)
  • Garg and McMichael, Limitations of Fitzpatrick skin type as a proxy for skin color and race (Cutis, 2026)
