Vitiligo

Vitiligo causes white patches where pigment cells stop working. Who gets it, how it is treated today, what does not work, and living with it in darker skin.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Vitiligo affects about one person in a hundred, in every ethnic group equally.
  • It is far more visible on brown and black skin, which is why it weighs more heavily there.
  • It is not contagious, and treatment can bring colour back, especially on the face.

Strong evidence

What happens

The immune system attacks melanocytes, the cells that make pigment. The skin left behind is milk-white with a sharp edge. Common sites are around the eyes and mouth, hands, wrists, armpits and genitals, and hair in a patch may turn white. It often runs with thyroid disease and other autoimmune conditions.

Mixed evidence

Treatment

  • Steroid creams and creams called calcineurin inhibitors, started early.
  • A cream that blocks an immune signal, ruxolitinib, is licensed for some patients.
  • Narrowband ultraviolet light therapy over several months.
  • Face and neck respond best. Hands, feet and lips respond least.
  • Sunscreen on white patches, which burn easily.
  • Skin camouflage, which can be prescribed, and cover creams matched to deep skin tones.

Strong evidence

What does not work

No diet, herb, oil or supplement has been shown to reverse vitiligo. Combining milk and fish, eating sour foods, or other food rules do not cause it. Be wary of expensive clinics promising a cure.

Mixed evidence

Living with it

In many African, Caribbean and South Asian communities vitiligo has been confused with leprosy or seen as a curse, and people report stares, questions and difficulty with marriage and work. That burden is recognised by doctors as part of the condition. Ask for psychological support if you need it. Many people also choose not to cover their patches at all.

When to see a doctor

New or spreading white patches. Early treatment works better, so ask for a dermatology referral.

Common questions

Can vitiligo be cured?

There is no permanent cure, but creams and light therapy can restore colour in many people, best on the face.
