Pigmentation on the face

Facial pigmentation in brown skin is usually melasma, marks after acne, or sun tan. How to tell which you have, the treatments that work, and what to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Three causes account for most facial pigmentation in brown skin: melasma, marks after spots or rashes, and sun.
  • All three get worse in sunlight, including through windows, so sunscreen is the base of every treatment.
  • Expect three months for visible change. Faster products are usually unsafe ones.

Mixed evidence

Which kind is it?

  • Melasma: symmetrical brown or grey-brown patches on cheeks, forehead, nose and upper lip.
  • Post-inflammatory marks: spots of colour exactly where acne, a rash or a scratch was.
  • Tan and sun spots: on exposed areas, sharper lines at clothing edges.
  • Around the eyes and mouth: often inherited.
  • Greyish patches after a cosmetic or hair dye: a reaction called pigmented contact dermatitis.
  • Dark, rough skin on the forehead and temples in someone using a lightening cream: possible ochronosis.

Mixed evidence

Treatment that works

  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen every day, ideally tinted.
  • Azelaic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C or a retinoid.
  • Prescription creams for melasma, used in courses.
  • Tranexamic acid for stubborn melasma, under a doctor.
  • Superficial peels by someone experienced with brown skin.

Strong evidence

What to avoid

Fairness and whitening creams with undeclared steroids or hydroquinone, facial bleach, lemon, and harsh scrubs. They are the commonest reason pigmentation in South Asian and African skin becomes permanent.

When to see a doctor

Pigmentation that is spreading quickly, patches that are grey-blue, or any change after using a whitening product.

Common questions

How do I remove pigmentation from my face?

Identify the type, use daily sunscreen, and add azelaic acid, niacinamide or a prescribed cream. It takes about three months.
