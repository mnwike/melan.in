Braid and plait styles: a guide

Twelve families of braided and twisted styles, with how long each takes, how long to keep it in, and how hard it pulls on the scalp.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Every style here can be worn safely. What matters is how tight, how heavy and how long.
  • Pain, bumps at the hairline or a headache after braiding mean it is too tight. Take it out.
  • Lighter and looser beats smaller and longer. Give the scalp a break between installs.

The styles

Goddess and boho braidsBox or knotless braids with loose curly strands left out. The most searched style in the UK. The loose curls tangle, so they need more upkeep.Time: 4 to 7 hours · Keep in: 4 to 6 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Medium · About 41,000 UK searches a month
Locs, faux locs and butterfly locsHair matted into rope-like strands, or wrapped extensions that imitate them. Sisterlocks are very small locs made with a tool. Faux locs can be heavy.Time: Faux: 4 to 8 hours · Keep in: Faux: 4 to 8 weeks. Real locs are permanent. · Pull on the scalp: Low for real locs; medium to high for faux · About 22,700 UK searches a month
Cornrows (canerows)Plaits made flat against the scalp in rows. The base for many other styles. Quick, cheap and good for sport and school.Time: 30 minutes to 2 hours · Keep in: 1 to 2 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Low if loose · About 19,000 UK searches a month
Fulani, tribal and lemonade braidsCornrows in patterns, often with a centre plait, side plaits and beads. Named for the Fula people of West Africa. Fine cornrows at the hairline carry the risk.Time: 3 to 6 hours · Keep in: 2 to 4 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Medium · About 13,150 UK searches a month
Knotless braidsBox braids started with your own hair, with extensions fed in gradually. No knot at the root, so less pull and weight on the scalp.Time: 4 to 8 hours · Keep in: 4 to 6 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Lower than box braids · About 11,000 UK searches a month
Box braidsIndividual plaits on square partings, usually with extensions. Smaller and longer means heavier. Ask for medium size and shoulder length for less pull.Time: 4 to 8 hours · Keep in: 4 to 8 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Medium to high · About 8,800 UK searches a month
French curl braidsBraids that finish in long, loose bouncy curls. Light on the head, but the curled ends tangle.Time: 4 to 7 hours · Keep in: 4 to 6 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Medium · About 6,400 UK searches a month
Twists (two-strand, Senegalese, passion, marley)Two strands wound round each other, with or without extensions. The easiest style to learn at home and quick to take out.Time: 1 to 6 hours · Keep in: 2 to 6 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Low to medium · About 4,350 UK searches a month
Stitch and feed-in braidsCornrows with extension hair fed in, with crisp horizontal partings. Neat lines need firm tension. Ask the braider to ease off at the edges.Time: 1 to 3 hours · Keep in: 1 to 3 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Medium · About 3,000 UK searches a month
Bantu knotsSections twisted and coiled into small knots. Named after the Bantu-speaking peoples of Africa. Take them down for a curly 'knot-out'.Time: 30 to 60 minutes · Keep in: A few days · Pull on the scalp: Low if not wound tight · About 2,100 UK searches a month
Crochet braidsExtension hair looped onto cornrows with a crochet hook. Fast and gentle if the base cornrows are loose.Time: 1 to 3 hours · Keep in: 4 to 6 weeks · Pull on the scalp: Low to medium · About 1,600 UK searches a month
Afro puffs and bunsHair gathered into one or two soft puffs. Use a satin scrunchie or a cut stocking, not a tight elastic.Time: 5 minutes · Keep in: A day · Pull on the scalp: Low with a soft band · About 440 UK searches a month

Diagrams are simplified. Times and wear lengths are typical ranges and vary with hair length, braid size and the braider. Search figures are UK monthly estimates from Ahrefs, November 2026, grouped by style family.

Strong evidence

Why braids are worth it

  • Less daily combing and handling, which is when fragile coiled hair breaks.
  • Time: a style that lasts weeks saves hours.
  • Heritage: these styles carry centuries of African history and are worth wearing with pride.

Dermatologists at Johns Hopkins who reviewed 19 studies ranked styles by risk of hair loss. Loose, low-weight styles sat at the low end. Braids, locs and extensions sat at the high end when tight or heavy, and most of all on relaxed hair.

Make any style safer

  • Ask for loose at the hairline, and speak up in the chair.
  • Choose medium size and moderate length to cut weight.
  • Keep the scalp clean: wash or cleanse it every one to two weeks.
  • Take styles out on time. Hair left in too long mats at the root.
  • Rest for a week or two between installs.

When to see a doctor

Thinning edges, a widening parting, or small bumps and soreness along the hairline. Early traction hair loss can recover. Late hair loss is permanent.
