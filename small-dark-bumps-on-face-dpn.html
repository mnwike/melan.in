Small dark bumps on the face (DPN)

Dermatosis papulosa nigra, or DPN, is the cluster of small dark bumps common on the cheeks of Black adults. It is harmless. What it is and removal risks.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Small, smooth, dark bumps on the cheeks and around the eyes are usually DPN.
  • They are harmless and never turn into cancer.
  • They can be removed, but removal can leave light or dark marks on darker skin.

Strong evidence

What it is

Dermatosis papulosa nigra is a harmless skin growth, a relative of the common 'seborrhoeic wart'. The bumps are brown to black, one to five millimetres across, and usually sit on the cheeks, temples and eyelids. They tend to increase slowly with age.

Strong evidence

Who gets it

Mostly people with darker skin. Studies put it at between about one in ten and one in three Black adults, and it is also seen in Asian and Hispanic people. It runs in families: more than half of people with it have a relative who does.

Strong evidence

Do you need to do anything?

No. Most people who see a doctor about it want reassurance, and no treatment is needed. They are not moles, not warts you can catch, and not caused by anything you did.

Mixed evidence

If you want them removed

A dermatologist can snip, freeze, burn or laser them. In darker skin each method carries a risk of leaving a lighter or darker patch, so it should be done by someone experienced with skin of colour, starting with a test area. Do not use wart removers, acids or thread at home.

When to see a doctor

A single dark spot that is new, changing, bleeding or looks different from the others, or a sudden crop of many bumps.

Common questions

What are the small black dots on my face?

In Black adults they are most often dermatosis papulosa nigra, a harmless growth.

Can DPN be removed?

Yes, by a dermatologist, but removal can leave light or dark marks on darker skin.
