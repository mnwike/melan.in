Guides / Lotions and moisturisers

After-sun lotion

After-sun lotion is a light moisturiser with cooling ingredients. What it does for sun-exposed brown and black skin, what to use instead, and its limits.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • After-sun cools and moisturises. It does not repair sun damage or extend a tan.
  • Any light, fragrance-free lotion or aloe gel does the same job.
  • For darker skin the aim after sun is to prevent dryness and patchy darkening.

Strong evidence

What is in it

Most after-sun products are water, glycerin and a light oil, with aloe vera, menthol or alcohol for a cooling feel. Some add vitamin E or panthenol. None reverses DNA damage that has already happened.

Mixed evidence

After a day in the sun

  • Cool shower, pat dry.
  • Light lotion or aloe gel on damp skin. Repeat that evening and the next day.
  • Avoid thick butters and petroleum jelly on hot skin: they hold heat in.
  • Drink water.
  • Skip products with alcohol or strong fragrance on sore skin.

Mixed evidence

Preventing patchy tone

Darker skin responds to sun by darkening unevenly, most on the face, shoulders and shins. Moisturising afterwards helps the skin shed evenly. Sunscreen before and during exposure does far more than anything applied afterwards.

When to see a doctor

Blisters, fever, chills, headache or feeling faint after sun exposure.

Common questions

Do I need after-sun lotion?

No. A light moisturiser or aloe gel works as well.
