Guides / Hair and scalp

Itchy, flaky scalp

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is one of the most common scalp problems in Black people. How often to wash coiled hair, what works, and what makes it worse.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Persistent flaking is usually seborrhoeic dermatitis, a reaction to a yeast that lives on everyone's scalp.
  • Oiling the scalp to hide flakes tends to make it worse.
  • Washing every one to two weeks with a medicated shampoo, then conditioning, suits most coiled hair.

Strong evidence

What it is

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is among the most common skin diagnoses in Black patients. It causes scale and itch on the scalp, and sometimes around the nose, eyebrows and ears. On darker skin it can leave lighter patches.

Mixed evidence

Washing and treatment

Standard advice is to wash often, which does not suit hair that dries and breaks easily. Dermatologists who treat textured hair suggest washing the scalp every one to two weeks, using an anti-dandruff shampoo on the scalp and following with a moisturising conditioner on the lengths. Very infrequent washing, such as once a month, makes the condition worse.

Pomades and oils on the scalp cover the flakes but can feed the problem and delay proper treatment.

Strong evidence

In children

In a school-age child, a scaly scalp may be scalp ringworm, a fungal infection that needs prescribed treatment. See the guide below before assuming it is dandruff.

When to see a doctor

Scaling with hair loss or broken hairs, sore or weeping patches, or flaking that persists after a month of medicated shampoo.

Common questions

How often should I wash afro hair if I have dandruff?

About every one to two weeks, with a medicated shampoo on the scalp and conditioner on the lengths.

Does oiling the scalp help dandruff?

No. It hides flakes for a while but tends to make seborrhoeic dermatitis worse.
