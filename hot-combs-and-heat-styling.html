Hot combs, silk presses and heat

How hot combs, flat irons and silk presses straighten afro hair, why heat damage is permanent, and how dermatologists advise using heat more safely.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Heat straightens by breaking the hair's temporary bonds. Water puts them back, which is why a press reverts in rain.
  • Too much heat changes the hair for good. 'Heat-trained' hair is heat-damaged hair.
  • Lower heat, less often, on clean dry hair, is the dermatologists' advice.

Strong evidence

How pressing works

A hot comb or flat iron temporarily disrupts the hydrogen bonds inside the hair shaft. Moisture reverses it, so people repeat it every week or two. A silk press is the same process done with a blow-dry and flat iron for a smoother finish.

Mixed evidence

What goes wrong

  • Heat damage is cumulative. Curls that no longer spring back after washing have been permanently altered, and the only fix is to grow them out.
  • Burns to the scalp, ears and neck, especially with stove-heated combs whose temperature you cannot see.
  • The scarring hair loss now called CCCA was first named 'hot comb alopecia'. Heat is no longer thought to be the only cause, and genes play a part.

Mixed evidence

Using heat more safely

  • Press less often.
  • Use the lowest setting that works, and one slow pass instead of several.
  • Straighten only clean, fully dry hair.
  • Use a heat protectant, and trim split ends regularly.
  • Keep hot tools off the scalp and away from children's hair where you can.

When to see a doctor

A scalp burn that blisters, or thinning and tenderness at the crown.

Common questions

Is a hot comb bad for your hair?

Used occasionally at moderate heat on clean, dry hair, the risk is low. Frequent or very hot pressing causes permanent damage.

Can heat damage be reversed?

No. Damaged lengths have to grow out and be trimmed.
