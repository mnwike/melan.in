Hydroquinone cream

Hydroquinone is prescription-only in the UK and banned from cosmetics. What it treats, how doctors use it in short courses, the risks, and legal alternatives.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • In the UK hydroquinone is a prescription-only medicine at any strength. It is banned from shop cosmetics.
  • Used under a prescriber for a few months, it is one of the best-studied treatments for melasma and dark marks.
  • Used for years, or in illegal creams, it can cause a permanent blue-black darkening called ochronosis.

Strong evidence

What it is

Hydroquinone slows the enzyme that makes melanin, so new skin carries less pigment. It fades patches. It does not 'bleach' existing skin, and the pigment returns if the cause, usually sun or inflammation, continues.

Strong evidence

The law in the UK

You cannot legally buy hydroquinone over the counter or online without a prescription in the UK, at any strength. The EU, Australia and Japan take the same line, and the US moved it to prescription-only too. Creams sold in shops, markets or online that contain it are illegal, and tested samples have also contained strong steroids or mercury. Trading Standards prosecutes sellers.

Mixed evidence

How prescribers use it

  • Usually 4%, on the dark patches only, once a day.
  • In a course, commonly up to three or four months, then a break.
  • Often combined with a retinoid and a mild steroid for melasma.
  • Always with daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, or the pigment comes straight back.
  • Reviewed by the prescriber, who stops it if nothing has changed by about three months.

Strong evidence

The risks

  • Irritation, redness and stinging. In darker skin irritation can itself cause new dark marks.
  • A lighter 'halo' if it is spread onto normal skin.
  • Ochronosis: a blue-black or grey-brown darkening that is very hard to treat. It is linked mainly to long, unsupervised use, and was described most in South Africa where strong creams were used for years.

Mixed evidence

Alternatives you can buy

Azelaic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, kojic acid, tranexamic acid serums and retinoids all have some evidence for dark marks, with gentler effects. Tinted sunscreen containing iron oxides protects against the visible light that drives melasma. None of these works quickly. Expect three months.

When to see a doctor

Dark patches you want treated: start with a GP or dermatologist. See one urgently if skin you have been lightening turns blue-black, thins, shows stretch marks or spots, or if you have used an unlabelled cream for months.

Common questions

Is hydroquinone legal in the UK?

Only on prescription. It is banned from cosmetics, so any cream sold without a prescription is illegal.

How long can you use hydroquinone?

Prescribers usually limit it to a course of a few months, then a break, to lower the risk of ochronosis.
