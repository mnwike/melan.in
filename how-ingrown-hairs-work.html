How ingrown hairs work

What an ingrown hair is, the two ways a curly hair gets trapped after shaving, why it leaves bumps and dark marks, and how to stop the cycle.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • An ingrown hair is a hair that has grown into the skin instead of out of it.
  • Curly hair cut to a sharp point does this in two ways: curling back in from outside, or piercing its own follicle under the surface.
  • The skin treats the hair like a splinter. That reaction is the bump.

Strong evidence

The two routes in

Curly and coily hair grows from a curved follicle. Shaving cuts each hair to a sharp tip. As a curved hair regrows, the tip can arc over and re-enter the skin a few millimetres from where it came out. Doctors call this extrafollicular penetration. The second route starts with a very close shave: the hair is cut below the surface, so the sharp tip pierces the wall of the follicle before it ever reaches daylight. This is transfollicular penetration.

Strong evidence

Why it becomes a bump

Once the tip is in living skin, the body mounts a foreign-body reaction, as it would to a splinter. That gives a firm, sore bump, sometimes with pus. In darker skin the inflammation often leaves a dark mark, and repeated bumps can scar or form keloids.

Strong evidence

What makes it worse

  • Shaving very close: multi-blade razors, pulling the skin taut, shaving against the grain.
  • Plucking or waxing, which can leave a sharp fragment under the skin.
  • Tight collars and helmet straps that rub the neck and jaw.

Mixed evidence

Breaking the cycle

  • Leave a little length: clippers or a guarded trimmer that leaves about a millimetre of stubble.
  • If you use a razor, shave with the grain, do not stretch the skin, and use one pass.
  • Free a trapped hair gently with a clean needle or toothbrush. Do not pluck it.
  • Growing a beard solves the problem for many men after a few weeks, once the hairs are too long to re-enter.

Mixed evidence

Not only men, not only faces

The same thing happens on the bikini line, underarms, legs and scalp, and on the chin and neck of women who remove facial hair. The fixes are the same.

When to see a doctor

Bumps that are painful, spreading or leaving scars, or a job that requires a clean shave you cannot manage without flare-ups.

Common questions

Why do I get ingrown hairs after shaving?

A close shave leaves curly hair with a sharp tip that either curls back into the skin or pierces its follicle below the surface.

Should I pull out an ingrown hair?

No. Lift the tip free and leave the hair in place. Plucking restarts the cycle.
