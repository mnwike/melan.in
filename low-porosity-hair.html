Low porosity hair

Low porosity hair has a tight cuticle that resists water and product. The signs, why the float test fails, and the products and routine that suit it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Low porosity hair takes a long time to get wet and a long time to dry.
  • Products tend to sit on top as a film or white residue.
  • Lighter products, warmth and clean hair help it take moisture in.

Mixed evidence

What it means

Porosity describes how easily water moves in and out of the hair through its outer layer, the cuticle. In low porosity hair the cuticle scales lie flat and tight. It is often healthy, undamaged hair. Porosity is a useful shorthand, though the science behind consumer porosity typing is thinner than its popularity suggests.

Limited evidence

Signs

  • Water beads on the hair before soaking in.
  • Hair takes hours to air dry.
  • Butters and heavy creams leave it greasy or coated.
  • Protein treatments make it stiff.
  • The glass-of-water float test is unreliable, because oil and product on the strand change the result.

Limited evidence

What suits it

  • Clarify every few weeks to remove build-up.
  • Apply conditioner with warmth: a warm towel, steamer or heat cap.
  • Choose lightweight, water-based leave-ins and milks.
  • Light oils such as jojoba, grapeseed or argan over castor and shea.
  • Apply products to damp, not dripping, hair.
  • Go easy on protein.

When to see a doctor

Porosity is a care question, not a medical one. See a doctor for hair loss or scalp symptoms.

Common questions

How do I know if I have low porosity hair?

It is slow to wet, slow to dry, and products sit on the surface.
