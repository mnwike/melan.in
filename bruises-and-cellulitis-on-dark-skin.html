Bruises and cellulitis on Black and brown skin

Bruises and skin infections are harder to see on dark skin. How to recognise them by touch, warmth and swelling, and when cellulitis needs same-day care.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • On dark skin a bruise looks dark purple, brown or black from the start. The red and yellow stages are hard to see.
  • Cellulitis is a skin infection. Look for warmth, swelling, pain, tightness and shine more than redness.
  • Spreading warmth with fever or feeling unwell needs help the same day.

Mixed evidence

Bruises

A bruise is blood under the skin. On brown and black skin it appears as a darker area, purple-black or deep brown, and is tender or slightly swollen. Comparing with the same place on the other side of the body helps. Bruises on dark skin are missed in clinical and safeguarding examinations for exactly this reason, so it is fair to point one out and ask for it to be recorded.

Strong evidence

Cellulitis

  • An area that is hot, swollen, tight and painful, usually on a lower leg, foot, arm or face.
  • The colour may be darker brown, purple or grey, and the skin may look shiny.
  • It spreads over hours to days.
  • Fever, shivering or feeling unwell may follow.
  • Draw around the edge with a pen and note the time, to see if it is spreading.

Strong evidence

Treatment and after

Cellulitis needs antibiotics. Raise the limb, drink fluids and finish the course. Athlete's foot, cracked heels and eczema are common entry points, so treat those to stop it coming back. The skin often stays darker for months afterwards.

When to see a doctor

Same day: a hot, swollen, painful area that is spreading. Call 999 for a very high or very low temperature, confusion, a fast heartbeat, or purple patches that do not fade when pressed.

Common questions

What does cellulitis look like on black skin?

A hot, swollen, painful area that looks darker, purple or grey and may be shiny. Warmth and spreading are the key signs.
