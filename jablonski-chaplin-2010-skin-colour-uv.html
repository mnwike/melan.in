Research / Evolution of skin colour

Human skin pigmentation as an adaptation to UV radiation

Nina Jablonski, George Chaplin · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2010

A plain-language summary by melan.in. Not written or endorsed by the authors.

What they did

The authors compared maps of ultraviolet radiation measured by satellite with the skin colour of long-settled populations around the world.

What they found

  • Skin colour follows the strength of ultraviolet light. The deepest tones evolved where UV is strongest. The authors argue this protected the body's folate.
  • As people settled where UVB is weak, skin lightened so it could still make vitamin D.
  • Light skin evolved more than once, by different genetic routes, in separate populations.

Why it matters

It explains skin colour as a response to sunlight, with no ranking of people. It also explains a modern health issue: darker-skinned people living far from the equator make less vitamin D.

Limits

It describes populations over thousands of years. It says nothing about any one person's health.
