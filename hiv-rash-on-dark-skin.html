HIV rash on Black and brown skin

An early HIV rash on dark skin looks like purple or dark brown flat spots and bumps, with flu-like illness. Why a rash cannot diagnose HIV, and how to test.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A rash alone cannot tell you whether you have HIV. Only a test can.
  • Early HIV can cause a flu-like illness with a rash two to six weeks after infection. Many people have no symptoms at all.
  • If you may have been exposed in the last 72 hours, emergency medicine called PEP can prevent infection.

Limited evidence

What an early rash looks like

A widespread rash of flat patches and small raised bumps, mainly on the chest, back and face, sometimes the palms and soles. On dark skin it looks purple, dark brown or darker than the surrounding skin, and is easier to feel than see. It usually comes with fever, sore throat, swollen glands, tiredness and aching, and fades within a couple of weeks. Many other viruses and medicines cause an identical rash.

Strong evidence

What to do

  • Get tested. Tests are free and confidential at sexual health clinics, and home test kits are available.
  • Modern tests are reliable from about four to six weeks after exposure. Clinics advise on timing.
  • Within 72 hours of a possible exposure, go to a sexual health clinic or A&E and ask for PEP.
  • If you are HIV negative and at ongoing risk, ask about PrEP, a daily or event-based tablet that prevents HIV.

Strong evidence

If you have HIV

HIV is a manageable long-term condition. Treatment lets people live a normal lifespan, and someone whose virus is undetectable on treatment cannot pass it on through sex. Skin problems are common in untreated HIV, including severe dandruff, shingles and itchy bumps, and most improve once treatment starts.

When to see a doctor

Possible exposure in the last 72 hours: go now for PEP. A rash with fever, sore throat and swollen glands a few weeks after a risk: get tested.

Common questions

What does an HIV rash look like on dark skin?

Flat purple or dark brown spots and small bumps on the trunk and face, with flu-like symptoms. It cannot be told apart from other viral rashes without a test.
