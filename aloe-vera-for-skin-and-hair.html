Aloe vera for skin and hair

Aloe vera gel soothes and lightly hydrates. The evidence for burns, acne, dark marks and hair growth, and how to use fresh aloe safely on skin and scalp.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Aloe vera gel is a light humectant that soothes irritated skin. That much is well supported.
  • It has modest evidence for minor burns and mild acne, and almost none for dark marks or hair growth.
  • Fresh leaf contains a yellow latex that irritates. Rinse it off before using the gel.

Strong evidence

What it is

The clear gel inside aloe leaves is mostly water with sugars that hold moisture. It is used on skin and hair across the Caribbean, Africa and India. It feels cooling because it evaporates.

Mixed evidence

The evidence

  • Minor burns and sunburn: may speed healing slightly and eases discomfort.
  • Acne: small trials suggest a benefit when combined with standard treatment.
  • Dark marks: a compound called aloesin slows pigment production in laboratory studies. Human evidence is thin.
  • Hair growth and dandruff: no good trials. It can calm an itchy scalp.
  • It is a mild moisturiser. Seal it with a cream or oil or it dries off.

Limited evidence

Using it safely

  • From the leaf: stand the cut leaf upright to drain the yellow sap, then rinse the gel.
  • Patch test. Allergy is uncommon but real.
  • Bought gels vary. Avoid those with alcohol and heavy fragrance.
  • As a hair gel or pre-wash, it rinses out easily and suits low-porosity hair.
  • Drinking aloe latex is a laxative and is not recommended.

When to see a doctor

A burn larger than your hand, blistering, or a rash after applying aloe.

Common questions

Is aloe vera good for your skin?

Yes, as a soothing light moisturiser. Claims about fading dark marks and growing hair are not well supported.
