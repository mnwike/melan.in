Glycerin for skin

Glycerin is the most common moisturising ingredient there is. What it does for dry and ashy skin, how to use it, and the truth about glycerin and rose water.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Glycerin pulls water into the outer layer of skin and holds it there. It is cheap, safe and well studied.
  • It works best inside a lotion, with something to seal it in.
  • Used neat it is sticky and can dry skin in cold, dry air.

Strong evidence

What it does

Glycerin, also called glycerol, is a humectant: it attracts water. In the skin it keeps the outer layer flexible and helps the barrier repair itself. It appears near the top of the ingredient list of most good moisturisers.

Limited evidence

Glycerin and rose water

Mixing glycerin with rose water is a long-standing home recipe in African, Caribbean and South Asian households. Diluted like this it is a pleasant, light moisturiser. Rose water adds scent and nothing else, and home mixes have no preservative, so make small amounts and keep them a week or two at most. In a British winter, follow it with a cream or butter or the water it attracts will evaporate.

Mixed evidence

How to use it

  • Easiest: choose a lotion with glycerin in the first five ingredients.
  • If using pure glycerin, dilute it: roughly one part to three or four parts water.
  • Apply to damp skin and seal with a cream, butter or petroleum jelly.
  • It is fine for the face and for sensitive skin.

Strong evidence

What it will not do

Glycerin does not lighten skin or fade dark marks, whatever a recipe video says. Skin that is well moisturised reflects light better and looks more even, which is where that idea comes from.

When to see a doctor

Stinging, redness or a rash after use. That is more likely the rose water or fragrance than the glycerin.

Common questions

Is glycerin good for skin?

Yes. It is one of the best-studied moisturising ingredients and suits almost everyone.

Does glycerin lighten skin?

No. It moisturises. Well-moisturised skin can look more even, but pigment is unchanged.
