Body lotion for Black and brown skin

How to choose a body lotion for Black and brown skin: the three ingredient types that matter, why dryness shows as ash, and what the label will not tell you.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Darker skin does not need a different lotion. Dryness simply shows more on it, as grey 'ash'.
  • A good lotion has three things: something that pulls water in, something that smooths, something that seals.
  • Thickness matters more than brand. In a cold, dry climate most lotions sold in pumps are too thin.

Strong evidence

Why dry skin shows as ash

The outer layer of skin is made of flat, dead cells. When it dries, the edges of those cells lift and scatter light, which looks white. On pale skin that is invisible. On brown and black skin it shows as a grey, dusty film, most on shins, knees, elbows and hands. Some studies also report lower levels of the barrier fats called ceramides in Black skin, though that research is small and disputed.

Mixed evidence

The three jobs of a moisturiser

  • Humectants pull water into the outer layer: glycerin, urea, hyaluronic acid, lactic acid.
  • Emollients fill the gaps between cells so skin feels smooth: shea butter, cocoa butter, plant oils, ceramides.
  • Occlusives seal water in: petroleum jelly, mineral oil, dimethicone, waxes.

A lotion with only oils and butters smooths and seals but adds no water. One with only humectants can leave skin drier in cold air. You want all three.

Limited evidence

Reading the label

  • Ingredients are listed from most to least. Water first is normal.
  • Look for glycerin or urea in the first five or six.
  • Look for a butter, oil or petrolatum high up, not at the end.
  • Fragrance is the commonest cause of reactions. Fragrance-free is safest for eczema-prone skin.
  • 'For dark skin' on the front is marketing. The ingredient list on the back is what does the work.

Strong evidence

How to use it so it works

  • Apply within a few minutes of washing, on slightly damp skin.
  • Use more than you think: a full handful for both legs.
  • Shins, knees, elbows and hands need a second layer in winter.
  • Short, warm showers. Long hot ones strip the skin's own oils.

Strong evidence

What to be wary of

Body lotions sold for 'toning', 'brightening' or 'even tone' sometimes contain illegal lightening agents or strong steroids, especially when bought from market stalls or online sellers. If a body lotion changes your skin colour, stop using it.

When to see a doctor

Dry skin that cracks, bleeds, weeps or itches enough to wake you, or dryness that does not improve after two weeks of regular moisturising.

Common questions

What is the best body lotion for Black skin?

One that contains a humectant such as glycerin or urea, an emollient such as shea butter, and an occlusive such as petrolatum, and that is thick enough for your climate.

Do Black people need a special lotion?

No. The skin's needs are the same. Dryness is more visible on darker skin, so richer products tend to suit better.
