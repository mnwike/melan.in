Sunburn on Black and brown skin

Black and brown skin can burn. It shows as heat, tightness, tenderness and darkening, then peeling. How to treat it and why it leaves uneven tone.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Yes, dark skin can sunburn. It takes longer and shows differently.
  • Expect skin that is hot, tight, sore and darker than usual, then itching and peeling.
  • The lasting effect is patchy darkening, not just a few sore days.

Mixed evidence

How it shows

Without visible redness, the signs are felt more than seen: warmth to the touch, tenderness, tightness, and a deeper or slightly purple tone. Peeling follows after a few days. Shoulders, nose, forehead, scalp partings and the tops of feet are the usual places. People who have moved from a very sunny country sometimes burn for the first time on a snowy or beach holiday.

Strong evidence

What to do

  • Get out of the sun and cool the skin with a cool shower or damp cloth.
  • Moisturise with aloe vera or a plain lotion.
  • Drink water. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain if you can.
  • Do not pick peeling skin.
  • Keep the area covered and out of the sun until healed.

Mixed evidence

Why it matters for dark skin

A burn triggers extra pigment, so the area can stay darker for weeks or months, and existing dark marks deepen. Repeated burning also adds to the small but real risk of skin cancer. Sunscreen of SPF 30 or more, shade in the middle of the day and a hat prevent all of it.

When to see a doctor

Blistering, swelling, chills, dizziness, headache or feeling sick after sun exposure, or sunburn in a baby or young child.

Common questions

Can Black people get sunburn?

Yes. It feels hot, tight and sore, and the skin darkens and later peels.
