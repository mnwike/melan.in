Dark circles under the eyes

Dark circles are common and largely inherited in South Asian, Middle Eastern and Black skin. The four causes, how to tell which you have, and what helps each.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • In brown and black skin, dark circles are most often inherited extra pigment around the eyes.
  • Hollowing, visible blood vessels, and rubbing from allergies or eczema are the other causes.
  • No cream removes inherited dark circles. Some treatments soften them.

Mixed evidence

Four causes

  • Pigment: brown colour that stays when you stretch the skin. Runs in families, common in South Asian skin.
  • Hollows: a shadow from the tear trough that deepens with age or weight loss. It disappears under direct light.
  • Vessels: a blue or purple tone through thin skin, worse when tired.
  • Inflammation: darkening from rubbing itchy eyes, eczema or hay fever.

Limited evidence

What helps

  • Sunscreen and sunglasses, every day. Sun darkens the area.
  • Treat allergies and eczema so you stop rubbing.
  • Gentle eye creams with niacinamide, vitamin C, caffeine or a low-strength retinoid: small improvements over months.
  • Colour-correcting concealer in peach or orange tones for deep skin.
  • Fillers and lasers for some people, from a practitioner experienced with skin of colour. Both carry real risks around the eye.

Strong evidence

What does not

Sleep and water help puffiness, not inherited pigment. Lemon, potato and bleaching creams irritate the thinnest skin on the body and commonly make dark circles worse. Lightening creams with steroids near the eye can raise pressure in the eye.

When to see a doctor

Darkening around one eye only, swelling, or dark circles that appear suddenly with tiredness, weight change or feeling unwell.

Common questions

Why do I have dark circles?

In brown and black skin it is usually inherited pigment around the eyes, sometimes with hollowing or rubbing from allergies.
