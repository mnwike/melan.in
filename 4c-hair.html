4C hair: what it is and how to care for it

4C is the most tightly coiled hair type. What makes it different, why it shrinks and breaks, and a simple routine backed by hair science.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • 4C hair has the tightest pattern on the hair type chart: small zigzag bends more than round curls.
  • Every bend is a weak point and blocks scalp oil from travelling down, so it is drier and more fragile.
  • It needs moisture, gentle detangling and low tension. It does not need to be changed.

Mixed evidence

What 4C means

The label comes from the salon hair typing system created by stylist Andre Walker in 1997, where type 4 is coily hair and the letter C marks the tightest pattern. Scientists describe the same hair by its geometry: highly curved, flattened in cross-section and coiled along its length.

Strong evidence

What the measurements show

Studies of afro-textured hair by Geneviève Loussouarn found it grows at about 256 micrometres a day, a little slower than European hair, and sits less densely on the scalp, around 190 hairs per square centimetre against about 227. The tight coil hides length, which is why hair that is growing can look as if it is not.

Mixed evidence

A simple routine

  • Wash the scalp every one to two weeks and condition every time.
  • Detangle wet, in sections, with conditioner in. Combing dry hair breaks it.
  • Keep styles loose and rotate them.
  • Protect it at night with a satin or silk bonnet or pillowcase.
  • Trim damaged ends.

Mixed evidence

What 4C does not tell you

Curl type says nothing about how your hair takes up water or how much of it you have. Two people with 4C hair can need different products. Use type as a starting point and watch how your own hair responds.

When to see a doctor

Bald patches, a receding hairline, a sore or scaly scalp, or sudden shedding.

Common questions

Does 4C hair grow slower?

Slightly, on average, and its tight coil hides length. It does grow.

Why is 4C hair so dry?

Scalp oil cannot travel easily down a tightly coiled strand, so the lengths get less natural lubrication.
