Batana oil for hair

Batana oil is sold as a hair growth miracle. What it is, where it comes from, what evidence exists, and what it can honestly do for afro and curly hair.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • There are no clinical studies of batana oil for hair growth. None.
  • It is a rich, dark palm oil that softens and seals hair well.
  • Much of what is sold online as batana is diluted or is a different oil.

Strong evidence

What it is

Batana oil is pressed from the nut of the American oil palm, and is traditionally made by the Miskito people of Honduras, who use it on hair and skin. It is a thick, brown, smoky-smelling fat, solid when cool. It went viral on social media as a regrowth treatment.

Limited evidence

What the evidence says

No trial has tested batana oil for hair growth, thinning or hair loss. The claims come from tradition and from sellers. What can be said is that it is high in oleic and linoleic acids, like many plant oils, so it coats the hair, reduces friction and helps hair hold moisture. Hair that breaks less looks longer and fuller. That is a real benefit. It is a different thing from growing new hair.

Limited evidence

Using it sensibly

  • Use it as a pre-wash treatment or a sealant on lengths and ends.
  • A little goes far. It is heavy and can weigh down fine or low-porosity hair.
  • Wash it out properly. Heavy oil left on the scalp can worsen flaking.
  • Patch test first.
  • Buy from a seller who names the source. Bright yellow, odourless 'batana' is unlikely to be the real thing.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does batana oil regrow hair?

There is no clinical evidence that it does. It conditions hair and may reduce breakage.
