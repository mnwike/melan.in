Tranexamic acid for melasma and dark marks

Tranexamic acid is used as a serum and, on prescription, as tablets for stubborn melasma. How it works, the evidence, and who must avoid the tablets.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Tranexamic acid is a medicine for heavy bleeding that also reduces pigment.
  • Serums are mild and low-risk. Tablets work better for melasma but need a doctor and screening.
  • Melasma tends to return when treatment stops, so sun protection remains essential.

Mixed evidence

How it works

Sunlight and hormones trigger a chain of signals between skin cells and pigment cells. Tranexamic acid interrupts part of that chain, so less melanin is made. It was noticed by chance in the 1970s when patients taking it for other reasons saw their melasma fade.

Mixed evidence

Serums and creams

  • Usually 2 to 5%, once or twice a day.
  • Small trials show modest improvement in melasma and dark marks over eight to twelve weeks.
  • Well tolerated, so it suits skin that reacts to stronger treatments.
  • Combine with sunscreen, and with niacinamide or azelaic acid if you wish.

Strong evidence

Tablets

  • Prescribed off-licence by dermatologists for melasma that has not responded to creams.
  • Low doses for a few months.
  • Not suitable if you have had a blood clot, stroke or heart disease, have a clotting tendency, smoke heavily, or take combined hormonal contraception, because of a possible clot risk.
  • Never buy the tablets online for skin lightening.

When to see a doctor

Leg pain or swelling, chest pain, breathlessness or sudden changes in vision while taking the tablets: seek urgent help.

Common questions

Does tranexamic acid help melasma?

Yes. Tablets have the stronger evidence but need a prescription and screening. Serums give a milder effect.
