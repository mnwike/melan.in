Wigs, lace fronts and scalp health

Wigs can protect natural hair or wear away the hairline. Lace front glue, bands and combs, washing the hair underneath, and signs your edges need a break.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A wig is only protective if the hair and scalp underneath are still being looked after.
  • Glue, tight bands and combs at the hairline are the commonest cause of thinning edges in wig wearers.
  • Take it off at night whenever you can, and wash your own scalp every one to two weeks.

Mixed evidence

How wigs damage hair

  • Adhesive pulling out fine hairline hairs on removal.
  • Allergic reactions to glue, causing an itchy, weeping hairline.
  • Elastic bands and combs pressing on the same spot every day.
  • Tight cornrows underneath.
  • A scalp left unwashed for weeks, leading to flaking, odour and sometimes infection.
  • Cotton wig caps drawing moisture out of the hair.

Limited evidence

Wearing them well

  • Prefer glueless wigs with adjustable straps, or wear the band behind the hairline.
  • If you use adhesive, remove it with the proper solvent and patience. Never peel.
  • Patch test glue behind the ear 48 hours before first use.
  • Satin or silk-lined caps.
  • Braid loosely underneath and vary the pattern.
  • Give your hairline wig-free days every week.

Limited evidence

Caring for what is underneath

Cleanse the scalp, condition, and moisturise your own hair on the same schedule as if you wore no wig. Let hair dry completely before putting a wig back on. Check your hairline monthly in good light and with a photo.

When to see a doctor

A hairline moving back, a smooth shiny band of skin, or itching, blisters and weeping where glue is applied.

Common questions

Do wigs damage your hairline?

They can, through glue, tight bands and neglecting the hair underneath. Glueless wigs and rest days reduce the risk.
