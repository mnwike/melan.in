Research / Vitamin D

Vitamin D and health

Scientific Advisory Committee on Nutrition · UK government report, 2016

A plain-language summary by melan.in. Not written or endorsed by the authors.

What they did

The UK government's independent nutrition advisers reviewed the evidence on vitamin D, sunlight and health to set recommended intakes.

What they found

  • Everyone aged four and over needs 10 micrograms of vitamin D a day.
  • Sunlight in the UK is too weak from October to early March to make it in the skin.
  • People with dark skin may not make enough even in summer and should consider a supplement all year.

Why it matters

It is the basis of the NHS advice on vitamin D, and it names people with dark skin as a group who should consider year-round supplements.

Limits

It sets intakes for bone and muscle health. It does not support the wider claims sometimes made for vitamin D.
