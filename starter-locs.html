Starter locs

How to start locs: comb coils, two-strand twists, braids, interlocking and freeform. The stages, how to wash, how often to retwist, and protecting the hairline.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Locs form when coiled hair is left to mat in sections. Any tightly curled hair will loc.
  • The method you start with shapes how they look for the first year.
  • You can and should wash starter locs. Over-tight, over-frequent retwisting is the real risk.

Strong evidence

Ways to start

  • Comb coils: neat and round, fragile when wet early on.
  • Two-strand twists: sturdier, suit longer hair.
  • Braids or plaits: hold best, keep a braid pattern for longer.
  • Interlocking: tightened with a tool, good for fine or active hair.
  • Freeform: wash and let the hair section itself.

Mixed evidence

The stages

Starter, about the first three to six months, when they unravel easily. Budding, when the ends swell and frizz. Teen, when they shrink and stick out. Mature, from around a year or two, when they are firm and hang. Frizz and shrinkage in the middle stages are normal.

Mixed evidence

Care

  • Wash every one to two weeks with a residue-free shampoo. A stocking cap helps early on.
  • Dry fully. Damp locs smell and can grow mildew inside.
  • Retwist every four to six weeks, and never to the point of pain.
  • Avoid heavy waxes and thick butters that build up inside the loc.
  • Thinning at the root or a receding hairline means too much tension or weight.

When to see a doctor

A hairline that is receding, locs thinning at the root, or a sore, flaky scalp.

Common questions

Can you wash starter locs?

Yes. Wash gently every week or two and dry them completely.
