Dark underarms and a dark neck

Dark, velvety skin on the neck or underarms is often acanthosis nigricans, which can signal insulin resistance. What it means and when to get tested.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Dark, thickened, velvety skin in the folds is called acanthosis nigricans. It is not dirt and scrubbing will not remove it.
  • It is often linked with insulin resistance, which can lead to type 2 diabetes.
  • On darker skin it sometimes appears without insulin resistance, so a simple blood test is the way to know.

Strong evidence

What it looks like

Brown to black patches with a soft, velvety feel, most often on the back and sides of the neck, the armpits and the groin. People with darker skin get it on the neck more often, and on the face almost exclusively.

Strong evidence

What it can mean

High insulin levels drive skin cells to multiply, which thickens and darkens the skin. That is why it is common in people with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome and pre-diabetes. In people with light skin it nearly always signals insulin resistance. In darker-skinned people it can occur without it, so the patch alone does not give the answer.

Mixed evidence

What helps

Treatment is aimed at the cause. Where insulin resistance is present, healthier eating, more activity and weight loss improve it and the skin often lightens. Ordinary friction, shaving and deodorant irritation can also darken underarms, and those improve with gentler hair removal and fragrance-free products. Lightening creams do not treat the cause.

When to see a doctor

Velvety dark patches on the neck or in the folds, especially in a child or teenager, or with tiredness, thirst or irregular periods. Ask about a blood sugar test. Patches that appear suddenly and spread fast in an adult need prompt assessment.

Common questions

Why is my neck darker than the rest of my skin?

A dark, velvety neck is often acanthosis nigricans, which can be linked to high insulin levels. A GP can check with a blood test.

How do I get rid of dark underarms?

Find the cause first. Treating insulin resistance, or reducing friction and irritation, works better than lightening creams.
