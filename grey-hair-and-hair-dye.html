Going grey, and what hair dye does

When grey hair usually starts in people of African and Asian descent, what counts as premature, how hair and beard dyes work, and their risks.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Grey hair starts later, on average, in people of African descent: around the mid-forties.
  • Grey before 30 counts as premature in people of African descent, and before 25 in Asian people.
  • Permanent dyes are a leading cause of allergy. Patch test every time, including beard dye.

Strong evidence

Why hair goes grey

Pigment cells in the follicle slow down and stop with age. When it begins is largely inherited. A common rule of thumb is that by 50, half of people have about half their hair grey. Grey usually shows first at the temples, then the crown, with beard and body hair later.

Mixed evidence

When early grey deserves a check

Premature greying is usually just family pattern. It has also been linked with vitamin B12 deficiency, thyroid problems, vitiligo and smoking, so grey hair in your teens or twenties is a reasonable thing to mention to a GP.

Mixed evidence

How grey hair behaves

Hair ages in other ways too. In Black hair, damage tends to show nearer the root. Grey strands are often drier and more wiry, and reviews note that ageing hair copes less well with chemical treatment. Condition more, and be gentler with heat and relaxers.

Mixed evidence

Dye: the options

  • Temporary and semi-permanent: coat the hair, wash out over weeks, least damage.
  • Permanent: opens the hair with ammonia and peroxide and forms colour inside. Covers grey best, dries hair most.
  • Going lighter needs bleach, which weakens coily hair quickly.
  • Beard dyes are permanent dyes for the face. Skin there is thinner, and reactions can be severe.

Mixed evidence

The risks

Allergy is the main one. The dye chemical PPD is among the strongest allergens in cosmetics, and a reaction can leave dark marks on darker skin. On cancer, one large US study of women found permanent dye associated with more breast cancer, most of all in Black women. There is no comparable study in men, so the honest answer for men is that nobody knows.

When to see a doctor

Swelling, blistering or a weeping rash after dye. Swelling of the face or trouble breathing is an emergency.

Common questions

At what age do Black people go grey?

On average around the mid-forties, about ten years later than white Europeans.

Is beard dye safe?

It carries the same allergy risk as hair dye, on more sensitive skin. Patch test 48 hours before every use.
