Guides / Trichology: hair science

The science of the hair fibre

How a hair is built, how it grows, and what measurably differs between tightly coiled, wavy and straight hair: shape, growth rate, density, lipids and strength.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • All human hair is made of the same keratin proteins. What differs is shape.
  • Coiled hair comes from a curved follicle and has a flattened cross-section, which makes it break more easily.
  • Measured averages differ between populations, but the ranges overlap and curl is better measured than guessed from ancestry.

Strong evidence

How a hair is built

Each fibre has three parts. The cuticle is the outer layer of overlapping scales, like roof tiles, that protects the hair and gives shine when it lies flat. The cortex is the bulk: bundles of keratin, held by strong sulphur bonds and weaker hydrogen bonds, which carries the strength, the stretch and the pigment. Some hairs have a hollow central medulla. Water breaks the hydrogen bonds, which is why wet hair can be reshaped. Relaxers break the sulphur bonds, which is why that change is permanent.

Strong evidence

How it grows

Each follicle cycles on its own: a growth phase lasting years, a brief transition, a resting phase of a few months, then the hair sheds and a new one starts. Losing 50 to 100 hairs a day is ordinary. A shock to the body can push many follicles into rest at once, causing heavy shedding about three months later.

Strong evidence

Where curl comes from

Curl is set in the follicle. A straight follicle with a round opening makes a straight, round fibre. A curved follicle makes an oval or flattened fibre that coils as it grows, and the two sides of the cortex are built slightly differently. Studies of people of African descent describe a cross-section that is more elliptical, even kidney-shaped, with twists along the length.

Mixed evidence

Why coiled hair breaks

  • Every bend and twist concentrates stress when the hair is pulled.
  • The flattened shape gives a smaller cross-section to carry the load at the narrow points.
  • Coils catch on each other and form knots.
  • Scalp oil spreads poorly along a coil, so lengths are drier and less flexible.
  • Damage tends to sit nearer the root than in straight hair, where it sits at the old ends.

Strong evidence

A better way to classify hair

Researchers measuring hair from about 2,400 people in many countries found they could sort it into eight curl types using simple measurements of curve and wave, with no reference to ethnicity at all. Every broad population contains several of the eight types. That is the scientific basis for describing hair by its curl, not by race.

When to see a doctor

Hair that breaks at the root or comes out in handfuls, or sudden change in texture with other symptoms. Fibre science explains breakage. It does not explain patchy or scarring loss.

Common questions

Is afro hair made of something different?

No. All human hair is the same keratin. Afro-textured hair differs in shape: a curved follicle and a flattened, coiled fibre.

Does afro hair grow more slowly?

On average in the studies, somewhat. Breakage, not growth, is the usual reason length is hard to keep.
