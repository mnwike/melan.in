Cancer and hair and skin products: the evidence

What studies have found about cancer and relaxers, hair dye, formaldehyde smoothing treatments, talc and lightening creams, and how sure each finding is.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • The strongest signal is for frequent, long-term use of chemical hair straighteners and womb cancer.
  • Formaldehyde, released by some smoothing treatments, is an established cause of cancer.
  • Most other fears, such as parabens, are not backed by evidence. 'Linked to' does not mean 'causes'.

Permanent hair dyeBreast cancerIn the same study, permanent dye use went with a 45% higher rate in Black women, and 60% for those dyeing every five to eight weeks, against 8% in white women. Semi-permanent and temporary dye showed little or no increase.One cohort, in which every woman had a sister with breast cancer. Needs confirming elsewhere.
Smoothing treatments that release formaldehyde ('Brazilian' or keratin treatments)Cancers linked to breathing formaldehydeFormaldehyde is classed as a known human carcinogen. Some smoothing products release it as gas when heated. A US ban was proposed and has been repeatedly delayed.The hazard is established. Salon workers, who breathe it most often, carry most of the risk.
Talc powdersOvarian cancerIn 2024 the WHO's cancer agency classed talc as 'probably carcinogenic' (Group 2A), on limited evidence in humans. It could not rule out asbestos contamination as the explanation in the human studies.Probable hazard, uncertain size. Simple to avoid.
Skin-lightening creamsMostly not cancerThe proven harms are mercury poisoning, skin thinning from steroids and permanent darkening from hydroquinone. Hydroquinone is not classifiable as a human carcinogen on current evidence (Group 3).The danger is real but it is not chiefly a cancer risk.
ParabensBreast cancerA widely shared worry. No study has shown that parabens in cosmetics cause breast cancer. Some longer-chain parabens are restricted in the UK and EU as a precaution.No established link.

Strong evidence

How to read this evidence

  • An association means the two things were found together in a group of people. It does not show that one caused the other.
  • These studies ask people to recall products used over decades. They cannot tell which brand or ingredient mattered.
  • The WHO's cancer agency (IARC) grades how strong the evidence is that something can cause cancer, not how big the risk is. Group 1 includes sunlight, alcohol and processed meat.
  • Dose matters. The raised rates were in frequent, long-term users.

Strong evidence

Why this falls harder on Black women

Black women use straighteners and permanent dyes more, start younger, and are sold different products. In the Sister Study most straightener users were Black women, so any real risk falls on them disproportionately.

Mixed evidence

What you can do

  • Cut down first: fewer relaxer touch-ups and less frequent permanent dye lower your exposure, whatever the true risk turns out to be.
  • Avoid smoothing treatments that list formaldehyde, formalin or methylene glycol, and be wary of 'formaldehyde-free' ones that fume when heated.
  • Skip talc powder in the genital area.
  • Look up ingredients in our glossary, where each one has a note on cancer evidence and known harms.
  • Do not relax or dye a child's hair.

Limited evidence

What nobody knows yet

Which ingredients, if any, drive the straightener findings. Whether stopping lowers the risk. Whether products sold in the UK and Africa carry the same risk as those in the US studies. Honest answers need studies that have not been done.

When to see a doctor

Bleeding after the menopause, bleeding between periods, or a new breast lump. These need checking whatever products you use.

Common questions

Do hair relaxers cause cancer?

Frequent, long-term use is associated with a higher rate of womb cancer in two large studies. That is a warning sign, not proof of cause.

Does hair dye cause cancer?

One large study found permanent dye associated with more breast cancer, especially in Black women. Other research is mixed.

Are parabens carcinogenic?

No study has established that parabens in cosmetics cause cancer.
