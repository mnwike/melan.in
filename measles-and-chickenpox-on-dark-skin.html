Measles and chickenpox on Black and brown skin

The measles rash is hard to see on dark skin. The signs that matter more than colour, how chickenpox looks, and how to prevent the marks they leave.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • On dark skin a measles rash may be barely visible. Feel for rough bumps and watch for the other symptoms.
  • High fever, cough, runny nose and sore red eyes come first. The rash follows after a few days.
  • Chickenpox blisters are easy to see on any skin, and they commonly leave dark marks or small scars.

Mixed evidence

Measles

  • Starts like a bad cold with a high temperature, cough and red, watery eyes.
  • Small white spots inside the cheeks may appear.
  • The rash starts on the face and behind the ears, then spreads down.
  • On brown and black skin it can look darker than the skin, purple-brown, or hardly show. It feels rough or bumpy.
  • Measles is very infectious and can be serious. Phone ahead before going to a surgery or hospital.

Strong evidence

Chickenpox

  • Itchy spots that become fluid-filled blisters, then crust.
  • New crops appear over several days, so spots are at different stages.
  • On dark skin the spots may be the same colour as the skin or darker before they blister.
  • Calamine, cooling gels and antihistamines ease itching. Do not give ibuprofen.

Mixed evidence

Marks afterwards

Both rashes can leave dark or light marks on darker skin, and scratched chickenpox can leave pitted scars or keloids. Keep nails short, use mittens on babies, and keep healed skin out of strong sun. Marks fade over months. Vaccination prevents measles, and a chickenpox vaccine is now part of the UK childhood schedule.

When to see a doctor

Call for urgent advice for difficulty breathing, a rash that does not fade when pressed, a seizure, drowsiness, a baby under three months with a fever, or anyone pregnant or with a weak immune system who has been exposed.

Common questions

What does measles look like on black skin?

The rash may look purple-brown or barely show. It feels rough, and follows fever, cough and red eyes.
