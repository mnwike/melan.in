Guides / Lotions and moisturisers

Body oil

Body oils add shine and seal in moisture but do not hydrate. Which oils suit brown and black skin, how to use them so ash does not return, and which to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Oil seals and softens. It contains no water, so used alone on dry skin the ash comes back.
  • Apply to damp skin straight after a shower, or over a lotion.
  • Lighter oils suit the body. Fragranced and essential oils are the usual cause of reactions.

Mixed evidence

Which oil

  • Jojoba, squalane, grapeseed, sunflower: light, absorb quickly.
  • Sweet almond and apricot kernel: medium, good all-rounders. Avoid with nut allergy.
  • Coconut: rich, can clog pores.
  • Olive oil: not recommended on babies or eczema, as it may weaken the skin barrier.
  • Mineral oil and baby oil: excellent sealants, fragrance aside.
  • 'Dry oils' contain silicones that make them feel weightless.

Mixed evidence

Using it

  • Three or four drops per limb on damp skin.
  • Or mix a few drops into your lotion.
  • Wait a few minutes before dressing. Oil marks clothes.
  • For glow, add a thin layer to shoulders, collarbones and shins.
  • Oil on a wet shower floor is a slip hazard.

Strong evidence

Sun and oil

Oil does not protect from the sun and does not help a tan. On some skin, citrus oils such as bergamot cause dark patches after sun exposure. If you want glow outdoors, put oil over sunscreen, not instead of it.

When to see a doctor

A rash after a new oil, or spots on the chest and back.

Common questions

Which body oil is best for Black skin?

A light, fragrance-free oil such as jojoba or squalane, applied to damp skin or over lotion.
