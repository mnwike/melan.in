Multani mitti (fuller's earth)

Multani mitti, or fuller's earth, is a clay used across South Asia for oily skin and tan. What it actually does, who should avoid it, and how often to use it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Multani mitti is an absorbent clay. It soaks up oil and leaves skin feeling clean and tight.
  • It suits oily skin, used about once a week. It dries out normal and dry skin.
  • It does not lighten skin, remove tan or treat pigmentation.

Strong evidence

What it is

Fuller's earth is a natural clay rich in aluminium and magnesium silicates, named in South Asia after Multan in Pakistan. It was used historically to clean wool. Mixed with water or rose water it is among the most searched skincare items in India.

Limited evidence

What it does and does not do

Clays absorb oil and can make pores look smaller for a few hours. The cooling, tightening feeling as it dries is water evaporating. There are no clinical trials showing that it treats acne, fades marks or removes tan. Any brightness afterwards is from oil removal and mild exfoliation.

Limited evidence

Using it

  • Once a week for oily skin. Less, or not at all, for dry or sensitive skin.
  • Mix with water. Skip lemon juice, which irritates.
  • Rinse off before it cracks completely.
  • Moisturise afterwards.
  • Do not use on eczema or broken skin.
  • Buy cosmetic-grade clay. Unregulated clays can carry contaminants.

When to see a doctor

Redness, burning or a rash after use, or acne that is leaving dark marks.

Common questions

Is multani mitti good for skin?

For oily skin, used weekly, it absorbs oil. It can dry out other skin types and has no proven effect on pigmentation.
