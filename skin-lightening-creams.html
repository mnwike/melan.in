Skin bleaching: what is in the creams

Hydroquinone, mercury and strong steroids are banned from UK cosmetics yet still sold. What they do to the body and how to check and report a product.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Many skin-lightening creams sold informally in the UK contain ingredients that are illegal in cosmetics.
  • The three to know are hydroquinone, mercury and strong steroids.
  • Never use a lightening product on a child.

Strong evidence

What Trading Standards keep finding

In 2026 the Chartered Trading Standards Institute reported illegal lightening products on sale in butchers, specialist food shops, small grocers and online. When London Trading Standards tested samples, one in five contained hydroquinone and one in six contained high levels of mercury. Councils have prosecuted sellers and seized thousands of products.

Strong evidence

What the ingredients do

  • Hydroquinone: a pigment-blocking medicine, legal only on prescription. Long unsupervised use can cause permanent blue-black darkening.
  • Mercury: blocks melanin and is absorbed through the skin. Linked to kidney damage and nerve problems. International law bans products above one part per million.
  • Strong steroids, such as clobetasol and betamethasone: thin the skin, cause stretch marks and acne, and can affect the whole body.

Strong evidence

How to check a product

  • No ingredient list, or no UK or EU address on the pack, is a warning sign.
  • Look for hydroquinone, mercury, mercurous chloride, calomel, clobetasol or betamethasone.
  • Be wary of very low prices and under-the-counter sales.
  • Paste the ingredients into our checker.

Mixed evidence

Why people use them

People do not bleach in a vacuum. Lighter skin is still rewarded in families, workplaces and media. Research on colourism links that pressure to poorer body image and mental health. Understanding it matters more than judging it.

When to see a doctor

If you have used a lightening cream and notice thinning skin, stretch marks, dark blue-black patches, tremor or numbness. Take the product with you. To report a seller in the UK, call the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133.

Common questions

Is hydroquinone legal in the UK?

Only on prescription. It is banned from cosmetic products.

How do I know if my cream contains mercury?

Check the label for mercury, mercurous chloride or calomel. Many illegal products do not list it, so a missing ingredient list is itself a warning.
