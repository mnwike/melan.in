Lotion for ashy skin

Ashy skin is dry skin showing as a grey film on darker skin. Which lotions fix it, why knees, elbows and legs are worst, and how to keep it away all day.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Ash is dryness made visible. It is not dirt and it is not a skin condition.
  • It needs water plus a seal: a humectant lotion, then something heavier on top in the worst places.
  • If it is back by lunchtime, the lotion is too thin or went on to bone-dry skin.

Mixed evidence

Why knees, elbows and shins

These areas have few oil glands, thicker outer skin, and constant bending and rubbing from clothes. They dry first and show it most. Shins are also what tights, trousers and central heating attack all winter.

Mixed evidence

What to put on

  • A lotion or cream with glycerin or urea as the daily base.
  • For rough knees and elbows, 10% urea or a lactic acid lotion softens the thick layer.
  • Then seal the worst spots with shea butter, cocoa butter or petroleum jelly.
  • Oil alone gives shine for an hour and then the ash returns, because oil adds no water.

Limited evidence

Keeping it away all day

  • Moisturise straight after washing, while skin is damp.
  • Carry a small tin of balm or a hand cream for knees, elbows and knuckles.
  • Wash with a soap-free cleanser. Bar soap and hot water undo the lotion.
  • Drink enough, but water alone will not fix ash. It is a surface problem.

Mixed evidence

When it is more than dryness

Ash that itches a great deal, comes in round patches, or sits with dark, thickened skin may be eczema. Fine fish-like scaling on the shins from childhood can be an inherited dry skin condition called ichthyosis. Both are treatable.

When to see a doctor

Itchy, cracked or bleeding skin, round scaly patches, or dryness from childhood that never clears.

Common questions

What causes ashy skin?

Dry outer skin cells lifting and scattering light. It shows as grey on darker skin.

What is the best lotion for ashy skin?

One with glycerin or urea to add water, sealed with a butter or petroleum jelly on knees, elbows and shins.
