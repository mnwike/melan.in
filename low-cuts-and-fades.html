Low cuts, fades and the barbershop

Low cuts and fades are the most common men's styles for afro hair. Clipper hygiene, hairline pushing, bumps at the nape, ringworm, and scalp care.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A low cut is the easiest afro hairstyle to keep healthy, if the clippers are clean.
  • Bumps at the back of the head and along the hairline are the commonest barbershop problem.
  • A hairline 'pushed back' for sharpness, week after week, can stop growing back.

Limited evidence

The cuts

  • Low cut or even cut: one short length all over.
  • Fade: short at the sides and nape, graduating to longer on top. Low, mid or high.
  • Taper: a gentler fade at the temples and nape only.
  • Line-up or shape-up: the hairline edged with trimmers.
  • Waves: a low cut brushed into a pattern.

Mixed evidence

Problems to avoid

  • Nape bumps: caused by close clipping and friction from collars. Ask for a guard at the back, not a bare blade.
  • Hairline recession: repeated edging slightly behind the natural line. Ask the barber to follow your hairline.
  • Scalp ringworm and folliculitis: spread by unclean clippers. Blades should be disinfected between clients.
  • Razor finishing on the neck and cheeks causes ingrown hairs.
  • Hair fibres and enhancement sprays can irritate. Wash them off.

Mixed evidence

Looking after a low cut

  • Wash the scalp two or three times a week.
  • Light moisturiser or oil. A shaved or low-cut scalp shows ash too.
  • Sunscreen or a hat on a very short cut in summer.
  • Brush with a soft brush.
  • See someone early about bumps that are firm or growing.

When to see a doctor

Firm bumps or lumps at the back of the scalp, pus spots after a cut, scaly bald patches, or a hairline that keeps receding.

Common questions

Do fades damage your hairline?

Not by themselves. Edging the hairline back repeatedly can cause permanent recession.
