Baby lotion for Black and brown skin

Choosing a lotion for a Black, brown or mixed-heritage baby: what newborn skin needs, which oils to avoid, eczema signs on dark skin, and what the trials show.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Baby skin is thinner and loses water faster than adult skin. Plain and fragrance-free is best.
  • Most babies do not need lotion every day. Dry or eczema-prone skin does.
  • Olive oil and heavily scented baby products are worth avoiding.

Mixed evidence

What baby skin needs

A newborn's skin barrier is still forming. For the first weeks, water and a little plain emollient on dry patches is enough. Dryness and peeling in the first fortnight are normal. On Black and brown babies, dry skin shows as grey or ashy patches, often on the cheeks, arms and legs.

Limited evidence

Choosing a lotion

  • Fragrance-free and colour-free.
  • A short ingredient list.
  • Ointments and creams moisturise better than thin lotions.
  • Avoid olive oil: small studies suggest it can weaken the skin barrier.
  • Shea butter and coconut oil are used widely. Try a small area first.
  • Emollients make babies slippery and are flammable on fabric. Take care near heat.

Strong evidence

Does daily moisturiser prevent eczema?

It seemed likely, so it was tested. A large UK trial had parents apply emollient daily for the first year of life. It did not prevent eczema, and skin infections were slightly more common in the emollient group. So moisturise a baby whose skin is dry or who has eczema, where it clearly helps. There is no need to do it as prevention.

Mixed evidence

Spotting eczema on darker baby skin

Eczema on dark skin looks brown, purple or grey, with small bumps, more than red. A baby who rubs their face on the cot sheet or scratches when undressed is itchy. Treated early, it leaves fewer light and dark marks.

When to see a doctor

Weeping or crusted skin, a baby who is not feeding or is unusually sleepy, or dry itchy patches that are not improving.

Common questions

What lotion is best for Black babies?

A plain, fragrance-free cream or ointment, used on dry areas. No special product is needed.

Can I use shea butter on my baby?

Many families do. Test a small patch first, and use prescribed treatment for eczema flares.
