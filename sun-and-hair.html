How the sun affects hair

Sunlight breaks down hair protein and bleaches its colour. Why dark hair is better protected, why treated hair is not, and simple ways to shield it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Sunlight damages hair. UVB breaks down protein and UVA and visible light bleach the pigment.
  • Dark hair is better protected than light hair because melanin shields the inside of the strand. The outer cuticle gets no such protection.
  • Bleached, dyed, relaxed or permed hair is damaged faster.

Strong evidence

What the studies show

In laboratory studies every hair type lost more protein after sun or UV lamp exposure, with UVB two to five times more damaging than UVA and visible light. Colour fading is driven mainly by UVA and visible light. A high concentration of melanin protects the fibre as a whole against this photo-oxidation, but not the cuticle, because pigment sits in the cortex. Humidity speeds the damage up.

Mixed evidence

What it means for coiled hair

Afro-textured hair starts out drier and more fragile, and its cuticle is the part melanin does not protect. Sun adds weathering on top: rougher strands, more tangling and breakage at the ends. Hair that has been relaxed, bleached or dyed is more vulnerable still.

Limited evidence

Protecting hair in the sun

  • Cover it: a hat, headwrap or scarf is the only protection with no doubt about it. Line it with satin to avoid friction.
  • Condition after sun, sea or pool, and rinse out salt and chlorine the same day.
  • Wear protective styles loosely on holiday, and keep parted or cornrowed scalp out of strong sun or use sunscreen on it.
  • Be sceptical of 'UV protection' claims on hair products: there is no regulated test or rating for them.

When to see a doctor

A sunburnt, blistered or peeling scalp, or hair loss that follows it.

Common questions

Does the sun damage Black hair?

Yes. Melanin protects the inside of the strand but not the outer cuticle, so sun still roughens and weakens it.

Do UV protection hair sprays work?

There is no regulated standard for them, so claims are hard to check. Covering the hair is more reliable.
