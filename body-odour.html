Body odour

Body odour comes from bacteria breaking down sweat. What reduces it, antiperspirant versus deodorant, dark underarms from products, and when to see a doctor.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Sweat itself barely smells. Odour comes from skin bacteria breaking it down.
  • Antiperspirant reduces sweat. Deodorant only covers or reduces odour. Many people need the first.
  • Fragranced deodorants and shaving are common causes of dark, irritated underarms.

Strong evidence

What helps

  • Wash armpits, groin and feet daily with soap, and dry well.
  • Apply antiperspirant at night to dry skin, when sweat glands are less active.
  • Wear cotton or moisture-wicking fabrics and change after sweating.
  • Wash clothes at a temperature that removes bacteria. Synthetic sportswear holds odour.
  • Trimming underarm hair reduces the surface where bacteria live.
  • An antibacterial wash such as benzoyl peroxide a few times a week helps stubborn odour.

Limited evidence

Home remedies

Lemon juice, baking soda and alum stones are widely used in Nigeria, the Caribbean and South Asia. Lemon and baking soda irritate the skin and can leave underarms darker. Alum is a mild antiperspirant salt and is reasonably gentle. None works better than a standard antiperspirant.

Mixed evidence

Dark underarms

Irritation from fragrance, alcohol-based sprays, shaving and friction leaves pigment behind in darker skin. Switching to a fragrance-free antiperspirant and changing how you remove hair usually helps. Dark, velvety thickened skin is a different condition linked to insulin.

When to see a doctor

A sudden change in body odour, heavy sweating that soaks clothes or happens at night, or odour with a rash, lumps or boils in the armpit or groin.

Common questions

How do I stop body odour?

Wash and dry daily, use antiperspirant at night, and wear breathable fabrics.
