Strawberry legs and bumpy skin on dark skin

Dark dots on the legs and rough bumps on the arms are usually ingrown hairs or keratosis pilaris. How they look on brown and black skin and what smooths them.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • 'Strawberry legs' are dark dots at the hair follicles: trapped hairs, plugs of dead skin, or marks left by shaving.
  • Keratosis pilaris is rough 'chicken skin' on the upper arms and thighs. It is harmless and very common.
  • On dark skin both leave brown dots, so preventing irritation matters as much as treating bumps.

Mixed evidence

What causes the dots

  • Ingrown hairs after shaving or waxing, common with curly hair.
  • Keratosis pilaris: follicles plugged with keratin.
  • Folliculitis: inflamed follicles, sometimes from tight clothes and sweat.
  • Dark marks left behind after any of the above.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Moisturise daily with urea, lactic acid or salicylic acid lotion.
  • Shave with a sharp blade, with gel, in the direction of growth, or switch to an electric trimmer.
  • Exfoliate gently, chemically. Hard scrubs and dry brushing inflame dark skin.
  • Loose clothing after exercise and a prompt shower.
  • Laser hair removal with the right laser for dark skin reduces ingrown hairs long term.

Strong evidence

What to expect

Keratosis pilaris improves while you treat it and returns when you stop. It often eases with age. The brown dots fade over months once the bumps and ingrown hairs are under control. Sunscreen on bare legs speeds that up.

When to see a doctor

Painful, pus-filled or spreading bumps, or bumps that leave raised scars.

Common questions

How do I get rid of strawberry legs on dark skin?

Moisturise with urea or lactic acid, change how you shave, and avoid harsh scrubs. The dots fade over months.
