Women's hair for work

Smart hairstyles for work for Black and brown women: quick natural styles, braids, locs, wigs and pressed hair, how to protect your edges, and your rights.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Any well-kept style is a professional style. You do not have to straighten your hair to look smart.
  • The styles that survive a working week are low-tension and moisturised.
  • The hairline pays for tight buns, heavy braids and glued wigs. Rotate styles.

Limited evidence

Quick styles for natural hair

  • Low bun or puff with a satin scrunchie: five minutes. Keep it loose and move its position from day to day.
  • Twist-out or braid-out: set at night, shake out in the morning, lasts three to five days.
  • Flat twists or a halo twist: tidy for a week.
  • Short cut or TWA: the lowest maintenance of all. Moisturise and go.

Mixed evidence

Longer-wear styles

  • Knotless or medium box braids, tied back loosely: four to six weeks.
  • Cornrows, straight back or in a pattern: one to two weeks.
  • Locs, loose or in an updo.
  • Wigs: choose glueless where you can, wear a satin or mesh cap rather than a tight band, and keep washing and conditioning the hair underneath.

Mixed evidence

If you straighten

A silk press now and then is lower risk than a relaxer, but heat damage adds up. Dermatologists advise less frequent heat, the lowest setting that works, and only on clean, dry hair. Frequent, long-term relaxer use is associated with womb cancer in two large studies, so pressure to straighten for work is a health issue as well as a fairness one.

Strong evidence

Protect your edges

Dermatologists at Johns Hopkins who reviewed the research found that tight ponytails, buns, braids, weaves and extensions carry the highest risk of traction hair loss, most of all on relaxed hair. Pain, bumps or a headache mean the style is too tight.

Limited evidence

Jobs with rules about hair

  • Healthcare and food: hair tied back or covered. Large hairnets, theatre caps and surgical hoods exist for braids, locs and afros. Ask for them.
  • Hard hats and helmets: low buns, cornrows or braids tied at the nape.
  • Customer-facing roles with a uniform standard: the standard must apply evenly to everyone.

Strong evidence

Your rights

In Britain, penalising a hairstyle tied to race can be race discrimination under the Equality Act 2010. Employers who sign the voluntary Halo Code pledge to let staff wear afro hairstyles without restriction or judgement. Campaigners say one in five Black women feels pressure to straighten her hair for work.

When to see a doctor

Thinning at the hairline or parting, a tender or burning scalp, or a widening patch at the crown.

Common questions

Is natural hair professional?

Yes. In Britain, treating afro hair as unprofessional can amount to race discrimination under the Equality Act 2010.

What is the best low-maintenance hairstyle for work?

A loose low bun or puff for day to day, or medium knotless braids if you want four to six weeks without daily styling.
