How to detangle and moisturise 4c hair

A step-by-step method for detangling 4c hair without breakage, and a moisturising routine that lasts: water, leave-in, cream, oil, and protective handling.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Never detangle 4c hair dry. Water and conditioner give the slip that stops it snapping.
  • Fingers first, then a wide-tooth comb, from the ends up to the roots.
  • Moisture is water. Oils and butters only seal it in.

Limited evidence

Detangling, step by step

  • Divide hair into four to eight sections and clip them.
  • Soak one section with water and apply plenty of conditioner.
  • Separate large knots with your fingers.
  • Comb from the ends, working upward a few centimetres at a time.
  • Twist or braid the finished section so it stays detangled.
  • A knot that will not move: add more conditioner and a drop of oil, and work it apart with a pin. Cutting is the last resort.

Limited evidence

Moisturising that lasts

  • Start on damp hair.
  • Leave-in conditioner or a water-based moisturiser first.
  • A cream next, then an oil or butter to seal. Some people prefer oil before cream. Try both.
  • Re-moisturise mid-week with a water-based spray, then reseal the ends.
  • Keep hair stretched in twists or braids to reduce new tangles.
  • Satin bonnet or pillowcase at night.

Mixed evidence

Common mistakes

  • Detangling in a hurry.
  • Small-tooth combs and brushing dry hair.
  • Oil alone on dry hair, which seals dryness in.
  • Skipping trims, so split and knotted ends tangle with everything else.
  • Leaving shed hair in after taking down braids. Finger-detangle before you wash.

When to see a doctor

Breakage at the roots, bald patches, or a sore scalp.

Common questions

How do you moisturise 4c hair?

Apply a water-based leave-in to damp hair, then a cream, then seal with oil or butter.
