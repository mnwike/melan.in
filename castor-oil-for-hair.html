Castor oil and Jamaican black castor oil

Castor oil and Jamaican black castor oil are staples for edges and growth. What they do, what the evidence is, how to use them, and the risks of overdoing it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • There is no clinical evidence that castor oil, black or clear, makes hair grow.
  • It is a very thick sealant that adds shine and reduces breakage when used on lengths.
  • On edges, the gentle massage and leaving them alone probably help more than the oil.

Strong evidence

What it is

Castor oil is pressed from castor beans and is mostly ricinoleic acid, which makes it unusually thick and sticky. Jamaican black castor oil is made by roasting the beans first, then boiling them. The ash makes it dark and slightly alkaline. It is a Caribbean tradition with roots in West Africa, and it is searched for heavily in the US, South Africa and India as well as Jamaica.

Limited evidence

What the evidence says

No trial shows that castor oil of any kind regrows hair or thickens edges. Laboratory work suggests ricinoleic acid has anti-inflammatory effects. There is one rare but real risk: heavy use on long hair has been linked to sudden severe matting, called acute hair felting, that can only be cut out.

Limited evidence

Using it

  • Mix with a lighter oil. Neat castor oil is hard to spread and hard to wash out.
  • A few drops on the ends and lengths as a sealant.
  • On the scalp, small amounts, washed out weekly.
  • For thin edges: stop the tension first. Oil cannot undo a tight style.
  • Keep it away from eyes.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does Jamaican black castor oil grow hair?

There is no clinical evidence that it does. It seals and protects hair, which helps length retention.
