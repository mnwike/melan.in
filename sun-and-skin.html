How the sun affects darker skin

Melanin gives real but partial sun protection. What UV and visible light do to darker skin, from dark marks to vitamin D, and what protection is for.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Melanin is a built-in filter. Deeply pigmented skin has a natural protection estimated at about SPF 13, against about SPF 3 for pale skin.
  • That reduces sunburn and skin cancer. It does not stop the sun darkening marks, deepening melasma or ageing the skin.
  • The same filter slows vitamin D production, which matters in low-sun countries.

Strong evidence

What melanin blocks

In laboratory measurements, deeply pigmented epidermis let through about a quarter of the UVB and a third of the UVA that pale epidermis did. Researchers put its natural protection at around SPF 13.4, compared with 3.3 for light skin. That is real protection, and well short of the SPF 30 dermatologists recommend.

Strong evidence

What the sun still does

  • Pigment: sunlight worsens dark marks and melasma. Visible light, not only UV, drives this in darker skin.
  • Ageing: it shows later than in pale skin and more often as uneven tone than as wrinkles.
  • Cancer: less common, but found later. Melanoma in darker skin usually appears on palms, soles and nails, where the sun is not the cause.
  • Vitamin D: darker skin needs more sun to make the same amount.

Mixed evidence

What that means day to day

  • If you are treating dark marks or melasma, daily tinted sunscreen is part of the treatment.
  • In strong sun, shade, clothing and a hat matter for everyone.
  • In a UK winter the sun is too weak to burn you or to make vitamin D. See the UV map for your city and month.

When to see a doctor

A new or changing dark patch on a palm, sole or nail, or a sore that does not heal.

Common questions

Does melanin protect against the sun?

Yes, partly. Deeply pigmented skin has natural protection estimated at about SPF 13, which lowers but does not remove sun damage.

Can Black skin get sunburn?

Yes. It takes more sun than for pale skin, and it may show as tightness, heat and peeling more than redness.
