Petroleum jelly (Vaseline) for skin

Petroleum jelly is the most effective sealant for dry skin and one of the safest. How to use it on ashy skin, lips, heels and eczema, and where to avoid it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Petroleum jelly seals water into the skin better than any oil or butter.
  • Cosmetic and pharmacy grades are highly refined and safe. Allergy to it is very rare.
  • It adds no water of its own. Put it on damp skin or over a lotion.

Strong evidence

What it does

Petroleum jelly, or petrolatum, forms a film that cuts water loss through the skin dramatically, which lets the outer layer rehydrate and repair. It is the base of most ointments for eczema, and has been a staple in Black and South Asian households for generations for exactly that reason.

Strong evidence

Is it safe?

The concern you may have read is about impurities in poorly refined petroleum products. The white petrolatum sold for skin in the UK, EU and US is purified to pharmaceutical standards. It does not cause cancer and is not absorbed into the body. It is fragrance-free and almost never causes allergy, which makes it one of the safest things you can put on a baby or on broken, eczema-prone skin.

Mixed evidence

Good uses

  • Over moisturiser on shins, knees, elbows and hands in winter.
  • Cracked heels: apply thickly and wear cotton socks overnight.
  • Lips, cuticles and around the nostrils during a cold.
  • Protecting a healing graze or a new tattoo, as advised.
  • 'Slugging': a thin layer over night-time skincare to seal it in. Suits dry skin.

Mixed evidence

Where to avoid it

  • Acne-prone faces, chest and back, where it can trap oil. Avoid slugging over retinoids until your skin tolerates them.
  • Inside the nose in large amounts, long term.
  • Near naked flames or cigarettes: paraffin-based products soak into fabric and make it flammable.
  • On heat rash, where it blocks sweat further.

When to see a doctor

Skin that stays cracked, sore or weeping despite regular use.

Common questions

Is Vaseline good for your skin?

Yes. It is one of the most effective and safest ways to seal moisture into dry skin.
