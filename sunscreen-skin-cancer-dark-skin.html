Sun, sunscreen and skin cancer

Melanin lowers skin cancer risk but does not remove it. Where skin cancer appears on darker skin, why it is found late, and what sunscreen is for.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Melanin gives real protection, so skin cancer is less common in darker skin.
  • When it does occur it is found later, and survival is worse.
  • Check your palms, soles and nails. That is where melanoma most often appears on darker skin, and it is not linked to sun.

Strong evidence

The numbers

In a US cancer registry analysis, five-year melanoma survival was about 66% for Black patients and 90% for white patients. Only 55% of Black patients were diagnosed while the cancer was still localised, against 78% of white patients. The gap comes largely from late diagnosis.

Strong evidence

Where to look

The type more common in darker skin is acral lentiginous melanoma, which grows on the palms, the soles and under the nails. Almost half of melanomas in Black patients in that analysis were on the lower limbs, mostly the foot.

  • A new or changing dark patch on a palm or sole.
  • A dark streak down a fingernail or toenail that was not caused by an injury, or that widens.
  • A sore anywhere that does not heal.

Strong evidence

So what is sunscreen for?

On darker skin the strongest everyday case for sunscreen is pigment: it helps prevent and fade dark marks and melasma. Tinted formulas with iron oxides avoid the white cast and block visible light as well.

When to see a doctor

Any of the signs above. Ask directly for the area to be examined, and mention that melanoma on darker skin often appears on hands, feet and nails.

Common questions

Can Black people get skin cancer?

Yes. It is less common than in white people, but it is often diagnosed later, which makes outcomes worse.

Where does melanoma appear on dark skin?

Most often on the palms, soles and under the nails, places that get little sun.
