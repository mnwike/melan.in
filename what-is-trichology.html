Guides / Trichology: hair science

What is trichology?

Trichology is the study of hair and scalp. What a trichologist does, how they differ from a dermatologist, UK regulation, and how to check someone's register.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Trichology is the study of the hair and scalp. A trichologist is a hair and scalp specialist.
  • Most trichologists are not doctors. They cannot prescribe, order NHS tests, or take a biopsy.
  • In the UK the title is not protected by law. Anyone can use it, so check the register.

Limited evidence

What a trichologist does

A consultation is long, often an hour. It covers your hair history, styling, diet, health and medicines, a close look at the scalp and hair shaft, often with a magnifying camera, and a plan: changes to care and styling, scalp treatments, and a letter to your GP if blood tests or a medical opinion are needed. For breakage, shedding, flaking and early traction, that kind of time and attention is often exactly what was missing.

Strong evidence

Trichologist or dermatologist?

A dermatologist is a medical doctor who then specialised in skin, hair and nails. They can diagnose by biopsy, prescribe, and treat the scarring hair loss conditions where delay costs hair permanently.

A trichologist is trained specifically in hair and scalp but is usually not medically qualified. The Institute of Trichologists says this itself, and describes its members' training as clinical in nature and specific to hair and scalp.

A sensible route: a trichologist for breakage, care, styling damage and a first assessment. A dermatologist, through your GP, for anything painful, scarring, patchy, sudden, or not improving.

Strong evidence

Regulation in the UK

There is no legal requirement to register, and 'trichologist' is not a protected title. The Institute of Trichologists, founded in 1902, keeps a voluntary register, and that register is accredited by the Professional Standards Authority. Its main route is a Level 5 Diploma in Clinical Trichology taking about two and a half years, followed by supervised practice. Other training bodies exist. Letters such as AIT, MIT or FIT show Institute membership.

Limited evidence

Questions to ask

  • Which register are you on, and can I look you up?
  • How much of your work is with afro-textured or curly hair?
  • When do you refer to a GP or dermatologist?
  • Do you sell the products you recommend?
  • What will the full course cost?

Limited evidence

Red flags

  • A guaranteed cure or regrowth.
  • A long, expensive course of in-house products sold at the first visit.
  • Advice to stop prescribed medicine.
  • No mention of a doctor for a painful, shiny or scarred scalp.

When to see a doctor

A sore, burning or tender scalp, smooth shiny bald areas, pustules, sudden patchy loss, or hair loss with other symptoms such as tiredness or weight change. These need a doctor first.

Common questions

Is a trichologist a doctor?

Usually not. Trichologists are trained in hair and scalp but most are not medically qualified and cannot prescribe.

Is trichology regulated in the UK?

The title is not protected by law. The Institute of Trichologists keeps a voluntary, accredited register.
