African, East Asian and South Asian skin and hair compared

How skin and hair compare in people of African, East Asian and South Asian ancestry: pigment marks, melasma, keloids, sensitivity, hair shape and hair loss.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • All three share a tendency to dark marks after inflammation. They differ a great deal in hair.
  • 'Asian' covers more than half of humanity. East Asian and South Asian skin and hair are not the same.
  • South Asian skin and hair are among the least researched, despite the numbers of people.

Strong evidence

Read this first

The studies behind this page use labels such as 'African', 'Asian' and 'Caucasian'. Those are rough social groupings, not biological types. Each group contains huge variety, the ranges overlap, and many studies measured only a few dozen people in one city. Read every figure here as an average from a sample, never as a rule about a person.

Mixed evidence

Pigment and marks

South Asian skin spans a wide range, from light brown to very dark, and in clinics behaves much like African skin when inflamed: acne, eczema and bites leave long-lasting dark marks. East Asian skin is lighter on average but still marks readily, and is prone to brown sun spots. Melasma, the patchy facial darkening driven by hormones and light, is especially common in South Asian, East Asian and South-East Asian women. Dark circles around the eyes are a frequent concern in South Asian skin, largely inherited.

Strong evidence

Sun and vitamin D

Ultraviolet filtering rises with pigment, so it is lowest in fair East Asian skin and highest in dark African and dark South Asian skin. In the UK, vitamin D deficiency is common in both South Asian and Black communities. Covered clothing and time indoors add to the effect of pigment.

Mixed evidence

Barrier and sensitivity

Some studies report a thinner outer layer and more stinging and irritation from products in East Asian participants, alongside the highest ceramide levels. Others find no difference. Black skin has been reported to have the lowest ceramides and more tightly packed layers. Treat all of this as provisional: the studies are small and partly contradictory.

Strong evidence

Scarring

Keloids are most common in people of African ancestry and are also more common in East and South Asian people than in Europeans. Anyone with a family history should think carefully about piercings, tattoos and elective surgery.

Mixed evidence

Hair

East Asian hair has the thickest, roundest fibres, is the straightest, and grew fastest in the published studies, with fewer hairs per square centimetre than European hair. It resists breaking well. African hair has flattened, tightly coiled fibres that break under less force. South Asian hair is typically straight to wavy with a fairly thick fibre and is often worn long.

  • Oiling the scalp and hair is a tradition in both African and South Asian communities. On coiled hair it helps the dry lengths. On a flaky scalp of any type, heavy oil can feed the yeast involved in dandruff.
  • Traction alopecia affects any hair pulled tight for years. It is described in Black women with braids, and in Sikh men and boys from tightly tied hair under a turban or patka.
  • Pattern baldness was least common in East Asian men in the 511-person study.
  • Greying began on average in the late thirties in Asian samples and the mid-forties in African samples.

Mixed evidence

Shared pressures

Skin lightening products are marketed heavily to African, South Asian and East Asian communities alike, and the same illegal ingredients turn up in all three markets. Colourism is not one community's problem.

When to see a doctor

Facial darkening that is spreading, any skin that changes after using a lightening cream, a growing scar, or hair loss along the hairline.

Common questions

Is South Asian skin similar to Black skin?

In how it responds to inflammation, yes: both commonly develop lasting dark marks. Hair is usually very different.

Which hair type is strongest?

In published tests East Asian hair, with the thickest and roundest fibre, resisted breaking best.
