Rosacea on Black and brown skin

Rosacea is often missed in dark skin because the redness is hard to see. The signs to look for, common triggers, treatment, and what to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Rosacea does occur in Black and brown skin. It is under-diagnosed because textbooks describe redness.
  • Look for warmth, stinging, a dusky or purple-brown flush, and spots without blackheads.
  • Steroid creams on the face make it worse.

Mixed evidence

Signs on darker skin

  • A feeling of heat or burning in the cheeks, nose or forehead.
  • Skin that stings with ordinary skincare.
  • A dusky brown or violet tone across the centre of the face.
  • Small bumps and pus spots with no blackheads, often called 'acne that will not respond'.
  • Dry, gritty or watery eyes.
  • Thickening of the skin of the nose in some men.

Strong evidence

Triggers

  • Sun and heat.
  • Hot drinks, alcohol, spicy food.
  • Exercise and strong emotion.
  • Harsh scrubs, fragranced products, and steroid creams.

Strong evidence

Treatment

Prescription creams and gels such as ivermectin, metronidazole or azelaic acid, and sometimes a course of antibiotic tablets, control the bumps. Azelaic acid also helps fade the dark marks. Gentle cleansing, plain moisturiser and daily sunscreen matter as much as the medicine. If you have been using a steroid or lightening cream on your face, tell the doctor.

When to see a doctor

Eye pain, blurred vision or very red eyes need prompt review. See a GP for facial bumps that do not respond to acne treatment.

Common questions

Can Black people get rosacea?

Yes. It is less common but often missed, because redness is harder to see on dark skin.
