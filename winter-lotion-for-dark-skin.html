Winter lotion for Black and brown skin

Cold air and heating dry skin fast, and it shows on darker skin. What to look for in a winter lotion, when to switch, and the routine that stops ash and itch.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Winter air holds little water and heating holds less. Skin loses moisture faster than in summer.
  • Switch from a lotion to a cream or butter when the heating goes on.
  • Itchy skin in winter is nearly always dryness. Thicker moisturiser fixes most of it.

Strong evidence

What changes in winter

Cold outdoor air and heated indoor air are both dry. Water leaves the skin faster, the outer layer stiffens and cracks, and nerve endings near the surface start to itch. People who grew up in humid climates often meet this for the first time after moving somewhere cold.

Mixed evidence

What a winter lotion needs

  • More oil and less water: a cream or butter in a tub, not a thin lotion in a pump.
  • Glycerin or urea to hold water in.
  • Petrolatum, shea butter or dimethicone to seal it.
  • No fragrance if your skin is already itchy or cracked.
  • No alcohol near the top of the ingredient list.

Limited evidence

A winter routine

  • Shower warm, not hot, and keep it short.
  • Pat dry and moisturise within a few minutes.
  • Second layer on shins, hands, knees and elbows.
  • Hand cream after every wash. Gloves outdoors.
  • A humidifier or a bowl of water near the radiator helps a little.
  • Lip balm with petrolatum or lanolin.

Strong evidence

Vitamin D

Winter in the UK brings no useful sunlight for making vitamin D, and darker skin makes less of it at any time. UK guidance is for everyone to consider a supplement from October to March, and for people with dark skin to consider one all year. That is separate from moisturising, but it is the other half of looking after dark skin in winter.

When to see a doctor

Cracks that bleed, itching that stops you sleeping, or round coin-shaped itchy patches on the legs.

Common questions

Why is my skin so dry and itchy in winter?

Cold air and indoor heating are both very dry, so skin loses water faster. Thicker moisturiser on damp skin usually settles it.

Should I change lotion in winter?

Yes. Most people need a cream or body butter in winter where a light lotion was enough in summer.
