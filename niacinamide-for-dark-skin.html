Niacinamide for Black and brown skin

Niacinamide is vitamin B3. It calms inflammation, controls oil and helps fade dark marks with little irritation, which makes it well suited to darker skin.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Niacinamide is one of the gentlest ingredients with evidence for uneven tone.
  • It reduces the transfer of pigment to skin cells, calms redness and oil, and supports the skin barrier.
  • 2 to 5% is enough. Very high strengths irritate more and add little.

Mixed evidence

What it does

Niacinamide, also called nicotinamide, is a form of vitamin B3. In studies it has reduced dark marks and blotchiness over eight to twelve weeks, lowered oil production, and improved the skin's barrier. It does not stop melanin being made. It slows the hand-over of pigment packets to surrounding skin cells, which is one reason it is gentle.

Mixed evidence

Why it suits darker skin

The main risk with pigment treatments in brown and black skin is irritation, which itself causes dark marks. Niacinamide rarely irritates, can be used morning and night, and combines safely with sunscreen, retinoids, azelaic acid and vitamin C.

Limited evidence

Using it

  • A serum or moisturiser with 2 to 5%, once or twice a day.
  • Under sunscreen in the morning.
  • Flushing or stinging usually means the strength is too high.
  • Expect eight to twelve weeks for marks.
  • It is an add-on. Acne still needs acne treatment, and melasma still needs sun protection.

When to see a doctor

Dark patches that are spreading, or marks that have not improved after three months of consistent treatment.

Common questions

Is niacinamide good for dark skin?

Yes. It helps fade dark marks with a low risk of irritation.
