Knotless braids

Knotless braids start with your own hair and feed extensions in, so there is no knot at the root. How they compare with box braids, and edge care.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Knotless braids put less pull on the scalp than box braids because there is no knot at the root.
  • They still carry weight. Long, small or heavy braids can cause traction whichever method is used.
  • Keep them in for six to eight weeks at most.

Strong evidence

How they are done

The braider begins each braid with your own hair, then feeds in small pieces of extension hair a little way down. The braid lies flat against the scalp and moves freely from day one. Box braids begin with the extension knotted at the root, which is quicker and tighter.

Mixed evidence

Scalp health

  • They should not hurt. Pain, bumps or a headache mean too tight: ask for them to be loosened or redone.
  • Choose medium or large over micro, and shoulder length over waist length.
  • Leave the fine hairs at the hairline out.
  • Wash the scalp every one to two weeks with diluted shampoo.
  • Dry thoroughly. Damp braids smell and can grow mildew.

Limited evidence

Taking them out

Remove within eight weeks. Cut below your own hair length, unravel gently, and detangle with conditioner before washing, or the shed hair that has collected will mat. Give your hair at least a week or two before the next install.

When to see a doctor

Pain that lasts beyond a day, pimples or crusting along the parts, or a hairline that is moving back.

Common questions

Are knotless braids better for your hair?

They put less tension on the roots than box braids, but size, length and how long you keep them in matter as much.

Sources

  • Wong et al., Evidence-based care of textured hair in Black women: a review of 29 studies (Cutis, 2025)
