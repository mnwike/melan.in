Eczema on darker skin

Eczema rarely looks red on darker skin. How to recognise it in adults and children, why it is often missed, and what treatment involves.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • On darker skin, eczema usually looks dark brown, purple or grey, not red.
  • It is more common and more often severe in people of African descent, and it is often under-recognised.
  • The marks it leaves can upset people more than the eczema itself. They do fade once the eczema is controlled.

Strong evidence

What to look for

  • Patches that are darker than the surrounding skin, purple-brown or grey.
  • Small rough bumps around hair follicles, often on the trunk, arms and neck.
  • Thickened, leathery skin where it has been scratched.
  • Lighter or darker patches left behind as it heals.

Strong evidence

Why it gets missed

Textbook descriptions and severity scores rely on redness, which is hard to see on deeper tones, so the eczema is judged milder than it is. Black children have around six times the risk of severe eczema compared with white children, yet their symptoms are more likely to be underestimated. If you feel a clinician has not grasped how bad it is, say how much it itches and how it affects sleep.

Strong evidence

Treatment

Treatment is the same in every skin tone: regular moisturisers, avoiding triggers, and prescribed anti-inflammatory creams during flares. Treating flares early also limits the pigment changes afterwards.

When to see a doctor

Any child with a persistent itchy rash, skin that weeps or crusts, or eczema that disturbs sleep.

Common questions

What colour is eczema on Black skin?

Usually dark brown, purple or grey. Redness is often hard to see.

Will the light or dark patches go away?

In most cases they fade slowly once the eczema itself is under control.
