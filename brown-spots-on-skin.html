Brown spots on the skin

Brown spots on darker skin are usually marks from old inflammation, sun spots, or harmless growths. How to tell them apart, and the few that need checking.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Most brown spots on brown and black skin are marks left by spots, bites, scratches or rashes.
  • Flat spots that appear on sun-exposed skin with age are sun spots. Raised, stuck-on ones are harmless growths.
  • A new spot on a palm, sole or under a nail, or one that changes, needs checking.

Mixed evidence

The common kinds

  • Post-inflammatory marks: flat, where something was inflamed. Fade over months.
  • Sun spots (lentigines): flat, even brown, on the face, hands and forearms from middle age.
  • Melasma: larger patches on the cheeks, forehead and upper lip.
  • Dermatosis papulosa nigra: small raised dark bumps on the face and neck.
  • Seborrhoeic keratoses: rough, waxy, stuck-on growths on the trunk.
  • Freckles and moles.
  • Shin spots in diabetes: brown, slightly sunken patches on the shins.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Sunscreen, which stops all of them darkening.
  • Marks and melasma: azelaic acid, niacinamide, retinoids, or prescription creams.
  • Sun spots: the same creams, or careful freezing or laser, with a risk of lighter marks in dark skin.
  • Raised growths: left alone, or removed by a dermatologist. Creams do not remove them.

Strong evidence

The ones to check

  • A spot that is growing, changing colour, uneven in shape, or bleeding.
  • Any new dark patch on a palm, sole, under a nail, or inside the mouth.
  • A spot that looks unlike all your others.
  • Many new spots appearing over weeks.

When to see a doctor

Any spot that changes, bleeds or will not heal, and any new dark patch on palms, soles or nails.

Common questions

What causes brown spots on the skin?

On darker skin, most often marks left by inflammation. Sun spots and harmless growths become common with age.
