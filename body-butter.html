Guides / Lotions and moisturisers

Body butter

Body butter is a thick, low-water moisturiser. Which butters suit dry and ashy brown and black skin, how to use them, and who should avoid them.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Body butter seals moisture in. It is the right choice for very dry skin and cold weather.
  • It contains little or no water, so apply it to damp skin or over a lotion.
  • Keep heavy butters off acne-prone areas.

Strong evidence

What it is

A body butter is mostly plant butters and oils, such as shea, cocoa, mango or kokum, with little or no water. Shop versions are often emulsions with some water and are closer to a rich cream. Whipped butters have air beaten in to make them lighter to spread.

Mixed evidence

Choosing one

  • Shea: soft, widely tolerated, some evidence for eczema-prone skin.
  • Cocoa: firmer, richer, more likely to clog pores.
  • Mango and kokum: lighter, less greasy.
  • Unscented if skin is sensitive. Essential oils and fragrance are the usual cause of reactions.
  • Homemade butters have no preservative. Keep water out and use clean hands.

Mixed evidence

Using it

  • On damp skin after a shower, or over a glycerin lotion.
  • Warm a small amount between the palms first.
  • Concentrate on shins, knees, elbows, hands and feet.
  • Winter and dry climates: daily. Hot, humid weather: switch to a lotion to avoid heat rash.

When to see a doctor

Spots on the chest or back after starting a butter, or a rash where you applied it.

Common questions

Is body butter better than lotion for dark skin?

For very dry or ashy skin and in winter, yes. In heat or on oily skin, a lotion suits better.
