Coconut oil for hair and skin

Coconut oil is one of the few oils shown to get inside the hair shaft. How to use it on curly, coily and South Asian hair, and who it does not suit.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Coconut oil is one of the best-studied hair oils. It reduces protein loss when used before washing.
  • It can make low-porosity or protein-sensitive hair feel stiff and brittle.
  • On the face it clogs pores for many people.

Mixed evidence

Why it is different

Most oils sit on the surface of hair. Coconut oil is mostly lauric acid, a small, straight molecule that can pass into the hair shaft. A widely cited laboratory study found that coconut oil applied before or after washing reduced the protein that hair loses, where mineral oil and sunflower oil did not. That supports the South Asian and Caribbean tradition of oiling before a wash.

Limited evidence

Using it on hair

  • Best as a pre-wash treatment: apply to lengths 30 minutes or more before shampoo.
  • A small amount as a sealant on ends.
  • It solidifies below about 24 degrees, which can leave hair hard in cold weather.
  • If hair feels stiff, dry or snaps more, stop. Some hair does not get on with it.
  • Wash it off the scalp. Left on, it can worsen dandruff.

Mixed evidence

Using it on skin

Coconut oil is a decent body moisturiser and has some evidence in eczema. It is also among the more pore-clogging oils, so it suits legs and arms better than a spot-prone face, chest or back.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Is coconut oil good for hair?

Yes for many people, especially before washing, where it reduces protein loss. Some hair goes stiff with it.
