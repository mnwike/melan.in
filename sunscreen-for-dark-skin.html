Sunscreen for dark skin: how to choose

How to choose a sunscreen for Black and brown skin without a white cast: tinted, mineral or chemical filters, what SPF to pick, and when you need it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Choose broad spectrum, SPF 30 or more, in a formula you will actually wear.
  • For dark marks or melasma, pick a tinted sunscreen with iron oxides: it also blocks visible light.
  • White cast comes from mineral filters. Chemical filters and tinted formulas avoid it.

Strong evidence

Three kinds of sunscreen

  • Mineral (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide): gentle and effective, but leaves a white or grey cast on darker skin unless tinted. Reviews note that this puts many people off using them.
  • Chemical or organic filters (such as avobenzone): invisible on the skin. They do not block visible light.
  • Tinted (mineral or mixed, with iron oxides): blocks UV and visible light, and the tint cancels the cast. The best choice for pigment problems if you can find your shade.

Mixed evidence

When you need it

  • Every day if you are treating dark marks or melasma, including near windows.
  • Whenever the UV index is 3 or above. Check the UV map for your city.
  • On holiday in strong sun, with shade and clothing as well.

Mixed evidence

Using it well

  • Use enough: most people apply far too little.
  • Reapply every two hours outdoors and after swimming or sweating.
  • Do not forget ears, the back of the neck and a shaved or parted scalp.

When to see a doctor

A rash or stinging from every sunscreen you try: you may be reacting to a filter or fragrance, and a doctor can arrange testing.

Common questions

What SPF should Black people use?

SPF 30 or higher, broad spectrum, is the standard recommendation for every skin tone.

Which sunscreen leaves no white cast?

Chemical-filter sunscreens and tinted sunscreens. Untinted mineral sunscreens are the ones that leave a cast.
