Sunscreen for oily brown and black skin

How to choose a sunscreen for oily or acne-prone brown and black skin: gel textures, filters that leave no white cast, tinted options, and how much to apply.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Oily skin still needs sunscreen, and darker skin benefits because sun deepens dark marks.
  • Look for gel, fluid or gel-cream textures labelled non-comedogenic.
  • Chemical filters or tinted mineral formulas avoid the grey cast.

Mixed evidence

What to look for

  • SPF 30 to 50 with high UVA protection.
  • Texture: gel, fluid, water-based or 'matte'. Avoid thick creams and oils.
  • Filters: modern chemical filters go on clear. Mineral zinc and titanium need a tint to disappear on deep skin.
  • Tinted with iron oxides if you have melasma or dark marks, to block visible light.
  • Alcohol-based fluids feel light but can dry and sting. Fragrance-free is safer for reactive skin.

Strong evidence

How to use it

  • Two finger-lengths for face and neck. Less than that gives much less protection.
  • Last step of skincare, before make-up.
  • Reapply every two hours outdoors. Powder and stick sunscreens help over make-up but do not replace the first layer.
  • A separate moisturiser may be unnecessary in hot, humid weather.

Limited evidence

Hot and humid climates

In India, West Africa and the Caribbean, sweat and sebum break sunscreen down faster. Water-resistant gels, blotting before reapplying, and hats and shade do more than a higher SPF number. Sunscreens labelled only with SPF may have weak UVA protection, so check for a UVA rating.

When to see a doctor

Breakouts or a rash from every sunscreen you try, or facial darkening that keeps spreading.

Common questions

Which sunscreen is best for oily dark skin?

A gel or fluid SPF 30 to 50 with chemical filters or a matching tint, labelled non-comedogenic.
