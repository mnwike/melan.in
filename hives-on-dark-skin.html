Hives on Black and brown skin

Hives on dark skin are raised weals, skin-coloured or slightly lighter or darker, rarely red. How to recognise and treat them, and when it is an emergency.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Hives are raised, itchy weals. On dark skin they are often skin-coloured, so feel for them.
  • Each weal fades within a day and others appear elsewhere. That moving pattern is the giveaway.
  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing, is a 999 emergency.

Limited evidence

Recognising hives without redness

Advice written for pale skin says 'red, raised rash'. On Black and brown skin the weals may match your skin, look slightly paler in the middle, or slightly darker. They are easier to feel than see, and easier to photograph in side lighting. Each one lasts minutes to hours and leaves normal skin behind.

Strong evidence

Common triggers

  • A viral infection, the commonest cause in children.
  • A food, medicine (including aspirin, ibuprofen and antibiotics) or insect sting.
  • Heat, cold, pressure, sweat or scratching.
  • Often no trigger is ever found, especially when hives last more than six weeks.

Strong evidence

Treatment

  • A non-drowsy antihistamine from a pharmacy.
  • Cool, loose clothing. Heat makes it worse.
  • Photograph the weals. They may have gone by the time you are seen.
  • Keep a short diary if it keeps happening.

Mixed evidence

If it leaves marks

Ordinary hives leave no mark at all. Weals that last longer than a day, hurt more than they itch, or leave bruise-like dark patches are a different condition and need a doctor.

When to see a doctor

Call 999 for swelling of the tongue, lips or throat, wheeze, faintness or difficulty breathing. See a GP for hives lasting more than a few days, coming back, or leaving marks.

Common questions

What do hives look like on black skin?

Raised, itchy weals that are skin-coloured or slightly lighter or darker, rather than red. They move around within hours.

Do hives leave dark marks?

Ordinary hives do not. If yours do, see a doctor.
