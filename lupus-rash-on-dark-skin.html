Lupus rash on Black and brown skin

Lupus is more common and more severe in Black and Asian people. How the butterfly rash and discoid lupus look on dark skin, and why early treatment matters.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Lupus is more common in people of African, Caribbean and Asian ancestry than in white Europeans.
  • The butterfly rash across the cheeks looks dusky, purple or dark brown, not red.
  • Discoid lupus causes scarring patches with lasting colour loss, often on the face, ears and scalp.

Mixed evidence

The butterfly rash

A flat or slightly raised rash across both cheeks and the bridge of the nose, sparing the creases beside the nose. On dark skin it is a deeper brown or violet shade and may feel warm. It often follows sun exposure, and comes with tiredness, joint pain, mouth ulcers or hair thinning.

Mixed evidence

Discoid lupus

Round, scaly, thickened patches on sun-exposed skin, the ears and the scalp. As they heal they leave a pale or white centre with a dark rim, and scars. On the scalp they destroy follicles, causing permanent bald patches. It is more frequent and tends to be more disfiguring in Black patients, which is why early treatment matters so much.

Strong evidence

What helps

  • See a GP promptly. Diagnosis needs blood tests and sometimes a small skin sample.
  • Strict sun protection: high SPF sunscreen, hats, shade. Sun triggers flares in all skin tones.
  • Prescription creams, and tablets such as hydroxychloroquine.
  • Stopping smoking improves how well treatment works.
  • Camouflage products for areas that have lost colour.

When to see a doctor

A new facial rash with joint pain, fever, chest pain, breathlessness or swelling of the legs is urgent. Any scarring patch on the scalp, ears or face needs a dermatologist.

Common questions

What does a lupus rash look like on black skin?

A dusky, purple or dark brown patch across both cheeks and nose, or round scaly patches that leave pale scars.
