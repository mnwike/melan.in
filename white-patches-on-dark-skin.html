White and light patches on dark skin

Light or white patches on Black and brown skin: telling vitiligo, pityriasis alba, tinea versicolor and marks left by eczema apart, and when to see a doctor.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Most light patches are not vitiligo. They are pigment temporarily turned down after a rash, dry skin or a yeast.
  • Chalk-white with a sharp edge points to vitiligo. Paler-than-usual with a soft edge points to something else.
  • Light patches show more on dark skin, and they repigment slowly: think months.

Strong evidence

The first question: lighter, or white?

Doctors separate skin that has less pigment (hypopigmented, a few shades lighter, with a blurry edge) from skin that has none (depigmented, milk-white, with a clear edge). The first is common and usually temporary. The second suggests vitiligo.

Strong evidence

The usual causes

  • Marks after a rash. Eczema, a burn, a graze, nappy rash or a strong steroid cream can leave paler skin behind. There was a rash or itch there first.
  • Pityriasis alba. Pale, slightly dry patches on a child's cheeks or arms, linked to eczema and dry skin. It shows more in darker skin and after summer, and settles by itself over months to a couple of years.
  • Tinea (pityriasis) versicolor. A normal skin yeast overgrows on the chest, back and neck. On dark skin it usually shows as light, finely scaly patches. Scratch one gently and fine scale lifts.
  • Vitiligo. The immune system switches off pigment cells. Patches are stark white with sharp borders, often around the eyes, mouth, fingers, wrists and genitals, and hair in the patch can turn white.
  • Small white dots on the shins and forearms in middle age. A harmless effect of years of sun.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • Pityriasis alba: moisturiser, gentle soap, sunscreen and time.
  • Tinea versicolor: antifungal shampoo used as a body wash, or antifungal cream, from a pharmacy. The yeast clears in weeks. The colour takes months to return, so do not keep re-treating.
  • Marks after a rash: treat the rash properly so new marks stop forming.
  • Vitiligo: prescription creams and light therapy can bring pigment back, best on the face and when started early. A cream that targets the immune signal (a JAK inhibitor) is now licensed for some patients.

Limited evidence

The emotional side

On dark skin a white patch is visible in a way it is not on pale skin, and in many communities it carries stigma or is confused with infection. Vitiligo is not contagious. It is reasonable to ask a doctor for treatment and for camouflage products, and to ask for support if it is affecting how you live.

When to see a doctor

Milk-white patches with sharp edges, patches that are spreading, numb patches (loss of feeling needs checking promptly), or light patches in a baby that were present from birth.

Common questions

What causes white patches on black skin?

Most often a mark left by eczema or another rash, pityriasis alba in children, or the yeast condition tinea versicolor. Vitiligo causes stark white patches.

How do I know if it is vitiligo?

Vitiligo is milk-white with a sharp edge and no scale. A doctor can confirm it with a special lamp.
