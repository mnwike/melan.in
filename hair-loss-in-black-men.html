Hair loss in Black men

Why Black men lose hair: male pattern baldness, traction from tight styles and sharp line-ups, and scarring conditions. What a shiny scalp means, and what helps.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Most hair loss in men is male pattern baldness: gradual, painless and inherited.
  • Itching, pain, bumps, pus or patches are not male pattern baldness and need a doctor.
  • Treatment keeps hair you still have far better than it brings back hair you have lost. Early is everything.

Strong evidence

Male pattern baldness

This is the commonest cause of hair loss in men, affecting 30 to 50 in every 100 by the age of 50. Hormones shrink the follicles at the temples and crown over years. Studies suggest men of African descent tend to keep their front hairline longer and go bald later and less extensively than white men, but it is still common. Only two medicines are licensed for it: minoxidil, rubbed on the scalp, and finasteride tablets. Both must be kept up to keep working.

Mixed evidence

'My scalp has gone shiny'

A bald scalp looks shiny in ordinary male pattern baldness, because the oil glands remain after the hairs have shrunk. On its own, shine is not a danger sign. Shine together with itching, soreness, bumps, pus or scaling is different: those point to inflammation that can scar the follicles for good, and need a dermatologist.

Strong evidence

Loss you can prevent

  • Tight cornrows, twists and locs pulled back hard thin the hairline over time.
  • Having the hairline 'lined up' further back each visit removes hair that may not return.
  • Very close cuts at the nape are linked with bumps and scarring there.

Mixed evidence

Things that do not work

  • Biotin and 'hair vitamins', unless a blood test shows a deficiency.
  • Oils and growth serums for male pattern baldness.
  • Waiting. Follicles that have scarred or fully shrunk do not recover.

When to see a doctor

Hair loss with pain, itching, pus, bumps or scaling; patches; or sudden shedding. For slow pattern thinning, see a pharmacist or GP early if you want to treat it.

Common questions

Do Black men go bald less?

Studies suggest baldness tends to start later and be less extensive than in white men, but it is still very common.

Why is my scalp shiny where I am losing hair?

A bald scalp is often shiny in male pattern baldness. Shine with itching, pain or bumps needs a doctor.
