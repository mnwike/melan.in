A dark line on a nail

Brown or black lines along the nails are common and usually harmless in Black and brown adults. The signs that mean a nail line needs checking for melanoma.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Brown lines running the length of the nail are common in darker skin and usually harmless.
  • Several similar lines on several nails is reassuring. One new or changing line on one nail is not.
  • Nail melanoma is rare, but it is the melanoma most likely to be missed in Black people.

Strong evidence

Why the lines appear

The lines are pigment laid down by melanocytes at the root of the nail. Doctors call it longitudinal melanonychia. In people with darker skin those cells are simply more active, so many adults have faint brown bands on several fingers and toes for life. Pregnancy, some medicines, and rubbing or picking at the nail fold can add more.

Strong evidence

Why this matters more in darker skin

Melanoma is much rarer in Black people than in white people. But when it does occur it is far more often the type that grows on palms, soles and under nails, which is not caused by the sun and is easy to overlook. So the usual 'check your moles' advice misses the places that matter most.

Mixed evidence

Signs that a line needs checking

Doctors use the letters A to F.

  • A: age and ancestry. Most cases are in people aged about 50 to 70, and African, Asian and Native American ancestry raises the share of melanomas found here.
  • B: a band that is very dark, wider than about 3 mm, or has blurred or uneven edges.
  • C: change. It is widening, darkening, or the nail is splitting or lifting.
  • D: the digit. Thumb, big toe and index finger are the commonest sites, and a single nail is more worrying than several.
  • E: extension. Pigment spreading onto the skin around the nail.
  • F: family or personal history of melanoma.

Limited evidence

What to do

  • Photograph the nail next to a ruler today, and again in a month.
  • Look at all twenty nails. Lines on many nails that look alike are usually your normal.
  • Remove polish before any appointment.
  • Check your soles and palms at the same time.

Strong evidence

What a doctor will do

A dermatologist looks with a magnifying light called a dermatoscope. If doubt remains, a small sample is taken from the nail root under local anaesthetic. That is the only way to be sure.

When to see a doctor

One new dark line in an adult, a line that is widening or changing, colour spreading onto the skin beside the nail, or a nail that splits, bleeds or lifts without an injury. Ask for a referral under the urgent suspected cancer pathway.

Common questions

Is a black line on my nail cancer?

Usually not. In Black and brown adults most nail lines are harmless pigment. A single new or changing line should be checked.

Is nail melanoma caused by the sun?

No. Melanoma under the nails and on palms and soles is not linked to sun exposure.
