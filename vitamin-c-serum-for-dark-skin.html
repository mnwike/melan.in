Vitamin C serum for Black and brown skin

Vitamin C serum can brighten and help fade dark marks, but it is unstable and can irritate. How to choose one for brown and black skin and how to use it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Vitamin C is an antioxidant that also slows pigment production. Evidence for fading marks is moderate.
  • The pure form, ascorbic acid, works best and stings most. Gentler forms suit sensitive skin.
  • A serum that has turned dark orange or brown has oxidised and should be thrown away.

Mixed evidence

What it does

Vitamin C neutralises some of the damage from sun and pollution, supports collagen, and interferes with the enzyme that makes melanin. Small trials show improvement in dark marks and melasma over three months, usually alongside sunscreen. It is one of the most searched skincare products in India and worldwide.

Limited evidence

Choosing one

  • L-ascorbic acid at 10 to 20% in an acidic formula: most evidence, most sting.
  • Derivatives such as sodium ascorbyl phosphate or ascorbyl glucoside: gentler, less proven.
  • Opaque, air-tight packaging.
  • Colourless to pale yellow when fresh.
  • Price is not a guide to quality.

Limited evidence

Using it on darker skin

  • Start every other morning and build up.
  • Apply to clean, dry skin, then moisturiser, then sunscreen.
  • If it stings, burns or leaves skin darker, stop. Irritation undoes the benefit.
  • Some formulas leave an orange tint on the skin surface, which washes off.
  • Niacinamide is a gentler first choice if your skin reacts easily.

When to see a doctor

A rash or burning after use, or dark patches that keep spreading.

Common questions

Does vitamin C serum help dark spots on black skin?

It can, over about three months with daily sunscreen. Choose a formula that does not irritate.

Sources

  • Structural and functional differences in skin of colour (Clinical and Experimental Dermatology, 2022)
  • Photoprotection for skin of colour (American Journal of Clinical Dermatology, 2022)
