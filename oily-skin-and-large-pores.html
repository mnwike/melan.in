Oily skin and large pores on darker skin

Oily skin is common in brown and black skin, and so is the shine people try to scrub away. What controls oil, what pores can and cannot do, and what to avoid.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Pores do not open and close. Their visible size depends on oil, age and genetics.
  • Harsh scrubs and alcohol toners irritate skin and can leave dark marks.
  • Niacinamide, salicylic acid and retinoids have the best evidence.

Mixed evidence

Why skin is oily

Sebaceous glands make sebum under the influence of hormones. Output peaks in the teens and twenties and is higher in hot, humid weather. Some studies report larger pores and more sebum on average in people of African and South Asian ancestry than in East Asian or European groups, though individuals vary more than groups do.

Mixed evidence

What helps

  • A gentle foaming cleanser twice a day. No more.
  • Niacinamide 2 to 5%: reduces surface oil in small trials and helps marks.
  • Salicylic acid 0.5 to 2%: clears the pore lining.
  • A retinoid at night: makes pores look smaller over months and treats acne.
  • A light, oil-free moisturiser. Skipping moisturiser does not reduce oil.
  • Gel or fluid sunscreen, which also stops pores slackening with sun damage.
  • Blotting papers or clay masks once a week for shine.

Mixed evidence

What to avoid

  • Scrubs with gritty particles, and cleansing brushes used hard.
  • Alcohol-heavy toners and lemon juice.
  • Pore strips used often.
  • Thick butters and oils on the face if you are acne-prone.
  • Squeezing, which leaves marks that outlast the spot.

Limited evidence

Procedures

Superficial peels and some lasers can reduce the look of pores, but need a practitioner experienced with darker skin because of the risk of pigment change.

When to see a doctor

Sudden oiliness with new facial hair, irregular periods or hair thinning can point to a hormone condition such as polycystic ovary syndrome.

Common questions

Can you shrink pores?

Not permanently. You can make them look smaller by keeping them clear and controlling oil.

Should oily skin use moisturiser?

Yes. A light, oil-free one. Dry, irritated skin is not less oily.
