Locs and dreadlocks: care and scalp health

How to look after mature locs: washing and drying, retwisting without thinning the roots, build-up, weight and length, and when locs are harming the scalp.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Locs are a healthy, low-manipulation style when the scalp is washed and the roots are not over-tightened.
  • The two real risks are traction from tight retwisting or heavy length, and build-up inside the loc.
  • Thinning at the root is an early warning. It can be reversed if tension is reduced.

Mixed evidence

Washing and drying

  • Every one to two weeks with a residue-free shampoo.
  • Squeeze suds through the locs. Rinse for longer than seems necessary.
  • Dry completely, with a towel and then air or a hooded dryer. Damp locs develop a musty smell.
  • An occasional soak in diluted apple cider vinegar or bicarbonate loosens build-up. Not every week.

Mixed evidence

Retwisting and tension

  • Every four to eight weeks. More often thins the root.
  • Palm rolling is gentler than interlocking done too tight.
  • Avoid styling freshly retwisted locs into tight buns or ponytails.
  • Long, thick locs are heavy, especially wet. Shorter or thinner locs, or wearing them down, reduce pull.
  • Combine or repair locs that are thinning at the base before they break.

Limited evidence

Products

Light oils and water-based sprays. Heavy waxes, butters and creamy conditioners lodge inside the loc and are very hard to remove. Lint collects from towels and bedding: use microfibre and satin.

When to see a doctor

A receding hairline, widening partings, locs hanging by a few hairs, or a sore, scaly scalp.

Common questions

How often should you retwist locs?

Every four to eight weeks. Retwisting more often can thin the roots.

Sources

  • Hair care practices and highly textured hair (Cutis, 2022)
  • Wong et al., Evidence-based care of textured hair in Black women: a review of 29 studies (Cutis, 2025)
