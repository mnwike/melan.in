Urea cream

Urea cream draws water into dry skin and softens thick, rough patches. Which strength to use for ashy skin, rough knees, cracked heels and bumpy arms.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Urea is one of the most effective ingredients for very dry, rough skin. It is cheap and sold in pharmacies.
  • 5 to 10% moisturises. 20% and above dissolves thick, hard skin.
  • It can sting on broken skin. That is the main drawback.

Strong evidence

What it is

Urea is a small molecule your skin already makes as part of its natural moisturising factor. Dry skin and eczema-prone skin contain less of it. The urea in creams is made in a laboratory. It holds water in the outer layer and, at higher strengths, loosens the bonds between dead cells so that rough skin sheds.

Mixed evidence

Which strength for what

  • 5%: daily body moisturiser for dry or ashy skin.
  • 10%: very dry skin, rough shins, knees and elbows, bumpy upper arms.
  • 20 to 25%: cracked heels and thick skin on feet.
  • 40%: thick nails and calluses, usually on advice.

Limited evidence

Why it suits darker skin

Rough, thickened skin on knees, elbows and knuckles looks darker than the skin around it. Softening that thick layer with urea often makes the area look more even, without any lightening ingredient. It is also fragrance-free in most pharmacy versions.

Mixed evidence

Using it

  • Once or twice a day on damp skin.
  • Expect a brief sting on cracks or freshly shaved legs.
  • Avoid eyes, lips and broken skin.
  • Check with a pharmacist before using on babies and small children.
  • Give it two weeks before judging.

When to see a doctor

Deep cracks that bleed, any sign of infection, or thick scaly skin that does not improve after a month.

Common questions

What is urea cream used for?

Dry, rough and thickened skin: ashy legs, rough knees and elbows, cracked heels and bumpy upper arms.

Is urea cream safe for dark skin?

Yes. It is a moisturiser and softener, not a lightening agent.
