Azelaic acid for Black and brown skin

Azelaic acid treats acne, rosacea and dark marks at once, with a low risk of irritation. Strengths, how to use it on brown and black skin, and what to expect.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Azelaic acid is one of the best-suited treatments for darker skin: it treats spots and the marks they leave.
  • It only slows overactive pigment cells, so it does not lighten normal skin.
  • It is considered safe in pregnancy, which few pigment treatments are.

Strong evidence

What it does

Azelaic acid is made by yeast that lives on normal skin and is found in grains. It unblocks pores, reduces the bacteria and inflammation of acne, calms rosacea, and slows the enzyme that makes melanin, mainly in abnormally active pigment cells.

Mixed evidence

Strengths and where to get it

  • 10% in cosmetic serums and creams.
  • 15% gel and 20% cream on prescription in the UK.
  • Trials in darker skin found 20% cream improved dark marks and melasma over about six months.
  • Often paired with a retinoid at night or niacinamide.

Mixed evidence

Using it

  • Once a day to begin, then twice.
  • Tingling or itching in the first weeks is common and usually settles. Apply over moisturiser if it stings.
  • Sunscreen every morning.
  • Give it three months for marks.
  • Temporary lighter patches have been reported rarely in very dark skin. Stop and ask if this happens.

When to see a doctor

Burning that does not settle, or dark patches that keep spreading despite treatment.

Common questions

Is azelaic acid good for dark skin?

Yes. It treats acne and dark marks together and rarely irritates.
