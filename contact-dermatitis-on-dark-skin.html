Contact dermatitis on Black and brown skin

Contact dermatitis on dark skin looks dark brown, purple or grey and leaves marks. Common triggers include hair dye, wig glue, relaxers, nickel and fragrance.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • A rash where something touched the skin. On dark skin it is darker, purple or grey, dry and itchy.
  • Hair dye, wig and lash glue, relaxers, fragrance and nickel are frequent causes.
  • The mark it leaves can outlast the rash by months, so finding the trigger matters.

Strong evidence

Two kinds

Irritant dermatitis is direct damage, from soap, sanitiser, bleach, a relaxer left on too long, or wet work. Allergic dermatitis is the immune system reacting to a specific substance, often one you have used for years without trouble. It appears a day or two after contact.

Strong evidence

Triggers worth knowing about

  • Hair dye. PPD in dark permanent dyes and 'black henna' is a leading cause. Reactions can be severe, with a swollen face.
  • Adhesives for lace-front wigs, weaves, lashes and nails.
  • Relaxers and texturisers: irritant burns on the scalp, hairline and ears.
  • Fragrance and preservatives in creams and hair products, including some natural oils.
  • Nickel in earrings, belt buckles and jean studs.
  • Rubber and latex in gloves and elastic.

Limited evidence

What it looks like

Itchy, dry, cracked skin in the shape of the contact: a hairline, an ear lobe, eyelids, a watch strap. The colour is dark brown, purple or grey. With time the skin thickens. Afterwards it is commonly darker, sometimes lighter.

Strong evidence

What to do

  • Stop the suspected product. Wash it off with lukewarm water.
  • Moisturise often with something plain.
  • A pharmacist or GP can advise on a steroid cream for a short course.
  • Ask for patch testing if it keeps returning. It is done by a dermatologist and identifies the exact allergen.
  • Do the 48-hour allergy alert test before every hair dye, even one you have used before.

When to see a doctor

A swollen face, eyes or lips after hair dye, blistering, broken skin on the scalp after a relaxer, or a rash that keeps coming back.

Common questions

What does contact dermatitis look like on black skin?

A dry, itchy patch that is dark brown, purple or grey, in the shape of whatever touched the skin.

Can hair dye cause it?

Yes. PPD in dark hair dye is one of the commonest causes of allergic contact dermatitis.
