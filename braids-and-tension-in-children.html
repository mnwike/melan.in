Braids, extensions and tension in children

Tight braids and heavy extensions can cause lasting hair loss in children. How to spot too much tension and how long styles should stay in.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Children can get traction alopecia, the same hair loss from pulling that affects adults.
  • Pain, bumps or a shiny, pulled-back hairline mean the style is too tight.
  • Loose, light styles that come out on schedule are fine.

Strong evidence

The risk

Hair loss from tension can occur with any curl pattern and is more common in Black children because of styling practices. Dermatologist Dr Susan Taylor has warned that long, heavy extensions in young children can pull hard enough to cause it.

Mixed evidence

Rules of thumb

  • If it hurts during or after styling, it is too tight. Take it out or loosen it.
  • Choose shorter, lighter extensions, or none.
  • In children, take extensions and weaves out every three to four weeks.
  • Avoid bonding glues.
  • Leave gaps between tight styles.
  • Keep washing the scalp while braids are in.

Limited evidence

Talking to a stylist

Say before they start that you want the edges left loose. A good braider will not be offended. If your child comes home with bumps at the hairline or cannot sleep on the style, it needs to come out.

When to see a doctor

Thinning or bald patches at the hairline or parting, or bumps and soreness that do not settle once the style is removed.

Common questions

How long can a child keep braids in?

For styles with added hair, three to four weeks is the guidance for children. Wash the scalp while they are in.

Can tight braids cause permanent hair loss?

Yes, if the pulling continues for long enough. Caught early, the hair grows back.
