Men's hair and beards for work

Smart, low-maintenance hairstyles and beard options for Black and brown men at work, how to keep them neat, and your rights if a dress code objects.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Neat is about edges and condition, not about straightness or length.
  • A short cut needs a barber every one to three weeks. Locs, twists and cornrows need less often but more care in between.
  • If shaving gives you bumps, a short, shaped beard is the medical fix as well as a style.

Limited evidence

Styles that read as smart

  • Low or skin fade with a short top: the easiest to keep sharp between cuts.
  • Waves: brushed daily, with a durag or wave cap at night.
  • Short or medium afro: shaped regularly and moisturised so it holds an even outline.
  • Twists or coils: tidy for two to four weeks with a satin cap at night.
  • Cornrows: neat, close to the head and good under helmets and hard hats.
  • Locs: retwist on a schedule that suits your hair, usually every four to eight weeks, and tie back if long.

Limited evidence

Keeping it sharp between cuts

  • Moisturise hair and scalp, especially in winter. Dry hair looks dull and unkempt before it looks long.
  • Do not let line-ups creep back: repeatedly pushing the hairline back to keep it crisp thins it over time.
  • Wash the scalp weekly if you exercise most days.

Strong evidence

Beards and razor bumps

Razor bumps come from close shaving of curly hair. Dermatology reviews describe leaving stubble of about a millimetre, or growing the beard out, as the most reliable fixes. A short beard kept to an even length with clean cheek and neck lines is a smart look and a treatment at once.

Strong evidence

If your workplace says otherwise

In Britain, treating someone worse because of a hairstyle tied to their race can be race discrimination under the Equality Act 2010. Employers may set a dress code, but it has to be fair and applied evenly. The Halo Code is a voluntary pledge some employers sign to protect afro hairstyles at work. If a clean-shave rule gives you razor bumps, ask for an adjustment and bring a doctor's letter.

Limited evidence

Helmets, hard hats and hygiene rules

  • Cornrows, short cuts and flat twists sit well under hard hats and helmets.
  • Long locs: tie low, and ask for a larger hat size or loc-friendly hairnet.
  • Face-fit masks need a clean seal. If you have razor bumps, ask about a hooded respirator instead of a daily close shave.

When to see a doctor

Razor bumps that scar, a hairline that is receding at the temples from tight styles or sharp line-ups, or bumps at the back of the scalp that harden.

Common questions

Are locs and cornrows allowed at work in the UK?

A ban that singles out hairstyles tied to race can be unlawful race discrimination under the Equality Act 2010.

Can my employer make me shave if I get razor bumps?

Ask for an adjustment and get a doctor's letter. Razor bumps are a recognised medical condition made worse by close shaving.
