Rosemary oil for hair

Rosemary oil has one small trial behind it. What that study found, what it did not test, how to use rosemary oil safely, and whether it suits afro hair.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Rosemary oil has more evidence than most hair oils, which is still only one small trial.
  • In that trial it performed about as well as 2% minoxidil for pattern hair loss after six months.
  • Essential oil must be diluted. Neat, it can burn and irritate the scalp.

Mixed evidence

The evidence

A 2015 trial gave 100 men with pattern hair loss either rosemary oil or 2% minoxidil for six months. Both groups had a similar, modest rise in hair count, and the rosemary group reported less scalp itching. There was no placebo group, the minoxidil dose was the weaker one, and nobody has repeated the study. It did not include women, afro-textured hair, traction alopecia or scarring hair loss.

Limited evidence

How to use it

  • Dilute: a few drops in a tablespoon of carrier oil, or buy a ready-diluted product.
  • Massage into the scalp a few times a week.
  • Rosemary water, made by steeping the herb, is far weaker than the oil used in the trial.
  • Give it six months before judging, and take a photo first.
  • Avoid in pregnancy unless your midwife or doctor agrees.

Limited evidence

For afro and curly hair

Thin hairlines in Black women are most often traction, and crown thinning may be a scarring condition. Rosemary oil has not been tested for either. If a style is pulling, loosening it does more than any oil. Oiling the scalp often and washing rarely can also feed dandruff.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does rosemary oil grow hair?

One small trial found it similar to 2% minoxidil for pattern hair loss. The evidence is limited.

Sources

  • Wong et al., Evidence-based care of textured hair in Black women: a review of 29 studies (Cutis, 2025)
