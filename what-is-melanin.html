What is melanin?

Melanin is the pigment that colours skin, hair and eyes. What it is made of, the types, what it does for the body, and the common myths about it.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Melanin is a pigment made by cells called melanocytes. Everyone has it.
  • It absorbs ultraviolet light and protects the DNA in skin cells.
  • People differ in how much they make and which type, not in whether they have it.

Strong evidence

What it is

Melanin is a family of dark pigments built from the amino acid tyrosine. In the skin it is made by melanocytes in the lowest layer of the epidermis, packaged into granules called melanosomes, and passed to the surrounding skin cells, where it sits like a cap over each cell's nucleus. Hair follicles and the coloured part of the eye make it too, and a related form is found in the brain and inner ear.

Strong evidence

The types

  • Eumelanin: brown to black. Dominant in darker skin and in brown and black hair. The better sunscreen.
  • Pheomelanin: yellow to red. More prominent in fair skin, freckles and red hair. Protects poorly.
  • Neuromelanin: in the brain. Unrelated to skin colour.

Strong evidence

What it does

Melanin absorbs ultraviolet radiation and neutralises some of the damage it causes. Deeply pigmented skin is estimated to filter several times more ultraviolet than very fair skin, which is why sunburn and sun-caused skin cancer are much less common. The trade-off is that less vitamin D is made from the same sunlight.

Mixed evidence

Myths

  • White people do have melanin. Only some people with albinism have almost none.
  • Having more melanin does not make anyone immune to sunburn or skin cancer.
  • Melanin has no proven link to intelligence, strength or spiritual ability. Those claims are not science.
  • You cannot meaningfully raise or lower it with food or supplements.
  • 'Melanin is worth more than gold' refers to laboratory-grade synthetic melanin sold in tiny research quantities. It says nothing about skin.

When to see a doctor

A pigment question becomes a medical one when colour changes: new white patches, spreading dark patches, or a changing mole.

Common questions

Does everyone have melanin?

Yes. People differ in the amount and type, not in whether they have it.

What does melanin do?

It absorbs ultraviolet light and protects skin cells from sun damage.
