Durags and 360 waves

How 360 waves form, what a durag does, the brushing routine, and how to avoid the forehead line, pomade acne and scalp problems that can come with them.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • Waves are your natural curl pattern brushed flat while short. No chemical is needed.
  • A durag holds the pattern down, protects it at night and keeps moisture in.
  • Tie it snug, not tight. Wash it and your hair regularly.

Strong evidence

How waves form

Tightly curled hair cut to roughly a centimetre or two will lie in ripples when it is repeatedly brushed in one direction from the crown outward. 360 waves run all the way round. It takes weeks of daily brushing, and coarser curls generally wave more easily.

Limited evidence

The routine

  • Brush for several minutes, two or three times a day, from the crown outward in a set pattern.
  • Use a medium or soft brush on a low cut. Hard bristles scratch the scalp.
  • Moisturise lightly before brushing.
  • Wear a durag for 30 minutes after brushing and overnight.
  • Wash weekly, brushing the pattern in under the shampoo.

Mixed evidence

Keeping scalp and skin healthy

  • A deep line on the forehead means the durag is too tight. Tie over the hairline, not below it, or use a wave cap.
  • Heavy pomade causes spots along the forehead and temples. Use less and keep it off the skin.
  • Wash durags weekly.
  • Brush burn, soreness and flaking mean ease off.
  • Durags are also simply good hair protection for braids, locs and fresh cuts.

When to see a doctor

Bumps at the back of the scalp, a receding hairline, or a scalp that is sore, scaly or weeping.

Common questions

What does a durag do?

It presses the hair pattern flat, protects it from friction at night, and holds in moisture.
