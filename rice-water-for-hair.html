Rice water for hair

Rice water is a traditional hair rinse from East and South-East Asia. What is in it, the evidence for growth, how to make it, and why it stiffens some hair.

Not yet clinically reviewed · Last updated November 2026

The short answer

  • No clinical trial has tested rice water for hair growth.
  • It leaves a light starch and protein film that can make hair feel stronger and smoother.
  • On low-porosity or protein-sensitive hair it can cause stiffness and breakage.

Strong evidence

Where it comes from

Rinsing hair with the water left from washing or soaking rice is a long tradition in China, Japan and South-East Asia. The Yao women of Huangluo in China, known for very long hair, are the example usually given. It spread worldwide through social media, including across India and the natural hair community.

Limited evidence

What the evidence says

Rice water contains starch, small amounts of amino acids and a compound called inositol. One cosmetic industry study suggested inositol stays in the hair after rinsing and reduces surface friction. That is the whole evidence base. Long hair in the villages that use it has many other explanations, including never cutting it and wearing it wrapped.

Limited evidence

Using it

  • Soak rinsed rice in water for 30 minutes, or use the cooking water, cooled and diluted.
  • Fermented versions smell sour and are more acidic. Keep them in the fridge and discard after a few days.
  • Apply after shampoo for 5 to 20 minutes, then rinse out and condition.
  • Once a week or less. Stop if hair feels hard, straw-like or snaps.

When to see a doctor

Hair coming out in handfuls, bald patches, a sore or scaly scalp, or a rash after using an oil. No oil treats scarring hair loss, and delay costs hair.

Common questions

Does rice water make hair grow faster?

There is no evidence that it changes growth. It may reduce friction and breakage for some hair.
